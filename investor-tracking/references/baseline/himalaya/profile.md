## 1. The short version, for someone with a 13F open

Li Lu runs one concentrated, long-horizon fund. He describes it as a Graham–Buffett–Munger practice applied to the whole world, with a stated tilt toward Greater China. The 13F shows only the US-listed slice of that fund: about 14–17% of regulatory assets under management over these three years. It cannot show his Hong Kong and mainland positions, which is where he says the opportunities are. Those include BYD, held since 2002, and a Postal Savings Bank of China stake that he partly sold in 2024–25.

The 13F side changes rarely. Of the twelve quarters, roughly half had no share-count change in any existing position. When something does change, it usually means one of four things. He has found a better use for the money, often a Chinese company listed in the US. He is correcting a mistake. A price has become extreme. Or he needs cash for redemptions. He named those four reasons himself (see section 3), and they are the right lens. He never comments on individual 13F moves, so every reason for a trade given here is an inference unless it is quoted.

For the next filing, the things that matter most:

- Share counts in the core names: Alphabet, PDD, Berkshire, East West Bancorp. A change there is meaningful.
- Any new position that reaches about 5% or more of the 13F.
- Any further addition to Chinese ADRs.

Weight changes caused by price, sub-2% satellite positions, and one-quarter round trips are mostly noise.

## 3. The enduring philosophy, in his words

These ideas recur unchanged from his older writing (the 2010 *Poor Charlie's Almanack* foreword, the 2013 Graham & Doddsville interview, the 2015 and 2019 PKU lectures, and the 2020 book *Civilization, Modernization, Value Investing and China*) through the talks in this period. Treat them as stable. They are dated here only to show that he still says them.

- **The six principles.** Restated at PKU on 2024-12-07 ([transcript PDF](https://offpisteinvesting.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Global-Value-Investing-in-Our-Era.pdf)) and again at Columbia on 2026-10-05 ([transcript PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/36th%20Annual%20Graham%20&%20Dodd%20Luncheon%20Transcript%20%2820261005%29%20CBS.pdf)):
  - stocks are fractional ownership
  - Mr. Market serves you rather than guides you
  - margin of safety
  - a circle of competence
  - buying great companies at reasonable prices
  - the sixth, which is his own: "Wealth is the proportion of purchasing power in the economy. The goal of value investing is to hold shares of the most dynamic companies in the most vibrant economies." (PKU 2024)
- **Macro and micro.** "The macro is what we must accept; the micro is where we can make a difference" (PKU 2024). He spends much of every talk on macro and history, but he says the decisions are company by company.
- **Selling.** From the Munger-anniversary interview of 2024-11-28 (English translations: [Hermit Research, 2025-03-11](https://www.hermitresearch.com/p/oij-24-li-lu-interviews) and [Medium, 2024-12-16](https://medium.com/@iamalexcarter/latest-interview-transcripts-li-lu-on-value-investing-charlie-munger-and-the-ai-revolution-34f3605a124f)): "Value investing does not mean that you will never sell after you buy. You can grow with some good companies for a long time; sometimes you will make mistakes and correct them immediately; sometimes you will find better companies and sell them. Sometimes, as an asset manager, you have no choice but to sell when investors want to redeem." He gives 1998 as the example: forced selling for redemptions during a 19% loss year.
- **Drawdowns test the circle of competence.** On BYD's repeated 50–80% falls: "Every time the stock price falls sharply, it will test the authenticity of the boundaries of your circle of competence" (PKU 2024).
- **Fish where the fish are.** He credits Munger with extending value investing beyond the US: "to places outside the United States where there are still many great companies and are in a growth period" (Nov 2024 interview). At Columbia in 2026, using Buffett's "church with a casino attached" image, he said: "We chose to build that church in Asia as well as other regions, as a global investor."
- **Long horizon.** The brochure gives a 5–10+ year horizon. The fund charges no management fee, only 25% of gains over a 6% compounded hurdle. That keeps the investor base patient and makes flows small relative to the book.

Each period below sets what he said beside what the filings show.

### December 2024 to Q1 2025: anxiety about China; trims and no buys

**Said:** In the PKU speech (2024-12-07) he set out the "perplexity of the times":

He framed China as stuck at a "2.5" stage, the middle-income consolidation every industrialising country goes through. He said he still has to invest in "the most dynamic economies," while matching purchasing power to where investors consume.

This was "the first interview Mr. Li Lu has accepted with domestic media after four years." So 2024–25 marks a return to public speaking after a quiet spell.

**Filed (Q1 2025):** The quarter had sales only:

- Alphabet C cut 19% (3,044,000 to 2,451,300).
- BAC cut 23% (18.08M to 13.85M).
- Apple cut 65% more, to a residual 110,600 shares.

At quarter-end prices that is about $320M sold and nothing bought. In Hong Kong, PSBC sales continued, taking the stake below 5% on 2025-05-08.

**Inferred (low–medium):** Selling in both the US and Hong Kong at the same time, with no visible buys, fits two explanations equally well. One is redemptions, which is reason four in his own list. The other is raising cash for something invisible on the 13F, such as mainland or Hong Kong names after the late-2024 China rally.

Form D does not settle it. The onshore fund's cumulative gross sales rose $339M between September 2024 and September 2025, but Form D does not net out redemptions.

The Alphabet C trim happened near the early-2025 low, during fears that AI would disrupt search, and he has not added since. This was a small trim of one share class, not an exit. I would not read a view on Alphabet into it.

**Said (Columbia Graham & Dodd luncheon, 2026-10-05, [transcript](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/36th%20Annual%20Graham%20&%20Dodd%20Luncheon%20Transcript%20%2820261005%29%20CBS.pdf)):**

- On AI and markets: capital concentrated at the top means "a whole bunch of otherwise wonderful businesses trading at very low prices. This is the same here, in China, or anywhere else. In China, it is probably more extreme." And: "this bifurcation offers those with a rational mind a better opportunity set than at other times."

**Inferred (medium–high):** The 2025–26 arc is consistent and in his own words. He has rotated the visible book out of US financials, energy and opportunistic satellites, and into Chinese consumer-internet ADRs (PDD, TME) that he regards as globally competitive and cheap. He kept Alphabet and added to Berkshire as the US anchors.

The new money of 2025–26 probably funded part of the PDD and Berkshire buying, alongside the BAC proceeds (medium confidence; Form D figures are gross, and some new money surely went to Hong Kong).

He talks at length about AI risk while his largest US position is Alphabet, at about 45%. That is not a contradiction he has addressed. Do not read an Alphabet sale into his AI remarks unless he actually sells.

## 6. How to read the next 13F

### Changes that are probably signal

- **Any share-count change in Alphabet, Berkshire, EWBC or PDD.** These names barely move. Berkshire went unchanged for at least ten quarters before the Q2 2026 add. EWBC has not moved since 2023. Alphabet A has not moved; Alphabet C moved once, in Q1 2025.
  - An add to one of these is a conviction statement.
  - A cut is either a switch into something better, possibly in Hong Kong where you cannot see it, or a redemption. Check whether the same filing shows a large buy elsewhere. If it does, a switch is the likelier reading.
- **A new position that starts at 5% or more**, or grows to that within two quarters. PDD went in at 18%. Big first purchases are rare and deliberate.
- **More Chinese ADRs.** This direction matches his public view (Columbia 2026; ADV 2026), so an addition is consistent with his thesis. A sale of PDD or TME would be a genuine surprise worth flagging: it would suggest either a view change on China or a mistake being corrected. He sold PDD once before, in 2021–22.
- **A full exit from a long-held name.** Micron in 2023 and BAC in 2025–26 were exited over several quarters, not all at once. A first cut of 20–25% in a core name can be the start of an exit. Watch the next one or two quarters before concluding.

### Changes that are probably noise, or need care before reading

- **Weight changes caused by price.** Alphabet went from 35% to 48% of the 13F with no purchase. Always compare share counts, not percentages.
- **Satellites under about 3%.** OXY, Sable, Crocs, the four information-services names and the Apple residual. They come and go. Their entry or exit tells you little about his main thinking. Sable was a loss he simply closed.
- **Quarter-to-quarter swings in the 13F total.** These reflect US prices plus switching between the US and Hong Kong. They are not fund flows. Form D amendments, filed each September and July, and Form ADV RAUM, filed each March, are better flow indicators.
- **Alphabet A versus C.** He holds both classes in near-equal amounts. Read them together. Any apparent shift between the classes should first be checked for a labelling error (see section 7).
- **Line count.** The jump to 14 lines in Q1 2026 came from small satellites. It does not mean the fund was diversifying.

### Where the 13F misleads for this manager

- **Five-sixths of the book is invisible.** That includes Hong Kong H-shares (BYD; possibly the PSBC remainder), mainland A-shares, other Asian markets, private companies (up to 40% allowed), cash, any borrowing, and any rare short.
  - A US sale with no US buy is often a move into Asia, not a cash raise.
  - HKEX disclosure-of-interests notices (di.hkex.com.hk, search the name "Li Lu") are the only public window on that side. They appear only when a holding crosses whole-percentage thresholds above 5% of a share class.
- **Redemptions are real but rare.** He names them as a reason to sell, the brochure says he has borrowed to meet them, and the 1998 episode shows it has happened. Since 2024, though, the Form D data show net inflow pressure. That includes about $2B of gross new subscriptions between September 2024 and September 2026 and a new feeder for wealth platforms. A broad, proportional cut across all US names would be the redemption signature. The cuts in this period were selective, which points to switching.
- **The 45-day lag** matters less than usual, because he trades so rarely. It does matter for PDD and TME, which are volatile ADRs.
- **Key-person risk.** Li Lu was born in 1966 and is the sole portfolio manager. No succession change was found. A change of portfolio manager would make this profile obsolete.
- **Look-alike entity.** "Himalaya Capital Ltd" (EDGAR CIK 2094573, formerly NovumX Ltd) filed a Form D/A on 2026-11-10. It reports $100K sold, a $10K minimum and every exemption box ticked, and it names Li Lu as director at "301 2nd Ave, Suite 2100, Seattle". That is one digit off Himalaya's real address. A FinCEN money-services registration and a GlobeNewswire release dated 2026-11-16 also promote it.
  - **Inferred (medium):** It is not Himalaya Capital Management (CIK 1709323, 1301 2nd Ave, Suite 2100), and its filings should not be read as his. Its profile does not fit a firm with no management fee and a $25M minimum. It may be misusing the name, or it may be an unrelated vehicle; I could not verify either.
  - What would change my mind: Himalaya Capital Management acknowledging it in an ADV or on himcap.com.

## 7. Filing mechanics that matter for this filer

- **Values in thousands in the first two quarters.** The Q4 2023 and Q1 2024 information tables report values in thousands of dollars, even though the SEC switched to whole dollars in 2023. Whole dollars start with Q2 2024. If a value looks 1,000× too small, check the cover-page total. The CSVs here are rescaled to dollars.
- **The Alphabet class-label amendment.** The original Q4 2025 13F-HR (0002043585-26-000011, filed 2026-02-17) swapped the share counts between Alphabet A and C. The 13F-HR/A (0002043585-26-000012, filed 2026-02-18, amendment type RESTATEMENT, amendment no. 1) corrected it. Any automated diff against the original would show a spurious A↔C switch. Always use the latest amendment, and compare A and C combined.
- **Filing agent.** Since Q1 2025 the 13Fs have been filed through a filing agent, and accession numbers start with 0002043585. N-PX and earlier 13Fs use Himalaya's own 0001709323 prefix. Look in the CIK 1709323 archive folder rather than guessing accession numbers.
- **Cover page.**
  - Report type: always "13F HOLDINGS REPORT."
  - Other included managers: 0 (no other-manager column). All rows have sole voting authority.
  - No confidential treatment requested in any quarter.
  - All rows are SH (no PRN).
  - No options.
  - Since Q1 2025 the cover also shows CRD 000157594 and SEC file 801-72763. The filer, report type and amendment flag are the cover-page fields to check.
- **Timing.** Every filing in the period was on time. The Q3 2026 filing arrived on 2026-11-16 because November 14 fell on a Saturday. The two-day gap between the Q4 2025 original and its amendment is the only oddity.
- **N-PX.** Filed 2024-08-26 and 2026-08-31 as "institutional manager notice report – did not exercise voting."
