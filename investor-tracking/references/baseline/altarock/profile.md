# AltaRock Partners: investor profile and 13F baseline

- **Filer:** AltaRock Partners LP (until early 2024 AltaRock Partners, LLC), CIK 0001631014, 13F file no. 028-16669, CRD 161005
- **People:** Mark T. Massey, founder, CIO, sole portfolio manager and 75%+ owner. Shannon T. Cabot, CCO, signs the filings. Scott Bradford, often still named as co-manager by 13F tracker sites, left in 2017.
- **Offices:** West Palm Beach, FL (principal office since 2024) and Boston, MA (3 investment staff). Before 2024 the principal office was Beverly, MA.
- **Period covered:** 13Fs for Q4 2023 through Q3 2026 (12 quarters), with older filings read for context
- **Written:** 2026-11-19. Holdings CSVs are in `holdings/`; sources and filing details are in `log.md`.

## Who this is, in one paragraph

AltaRock is one man's concentrated, long-only portfolio of a handful of large, high-quality US businesses, run with Buffett-style "owner" language. It manages about $5.7B through two vehicles with the same holdings. One is a Delaware LP of about 90 wealthy investors, 16% owned by Massey and related persons. The other is a Cayman master fund fed by Brown Brothers Harriman's wealth-management clients (about 2,100 of them). Massey makes every decision. He has said almost nothing publicly since a 2014 interview. That means nobody will tell you why a position changed. You have to read intent from the size, the timing and the price path of the change, and from the fact that the book holds only six real positions.

## What they say they are doing

Everything here is in their own words. Most of it is old, but the 2026 ADV brochure shows the philosophy has not changed.

The current statement is the Form ADV Part 2A brochure dated 2026-03-23 ([IAPD](https://files.adviserinfo.sec.gov/IAPD/Content/Common/crd_iapd_Brochure.aspx?BRCHR_VRSN_ID=1024091)). It says AltaRock invests "in the equity securities of a select group of companies we deem to be both undervalued and to possess sustainable competitive advantages." Its test: "If we would be unwilling to invest a large percentage of our own net worth into the equity in question for at least ten years, it is an undeniable signal to us that the idea is flawed." On sizing: "Since high return/low risk ideas are rare, we will invest disproportionately in these opportunities when presented. The intent is to hold these companies in the portfolio for the long-term, provided that they are effectively executing on their long-term business goals and continue to represent good value." Note the second condition, "continue to represent good value". Price is an explicit reason to hold or not, and the trading record bears that out. The brochure also says "The Funds managed by AltaRock follow the same strategy and have the same holdings" and "Mark Massey is the portfolio manager for the Funds and makes all investment decisions."

A BBH description dated 2023-12-31, quoted by Old Well Labs ([2025-10-14](https://newsletter.oldwell-labs.com/p/owl-s-low-turnover-club)), gives the hurdle: AltaRock "will only purchase these when it can reasonably expect a 15% gross (12.5% net) return over at least the next 10 years." This is the most useful sentence for reading their trades. A hurdle expressed as a 10-year IRR means the same business becomes a buy when its price falls and a trim when its price rises, with no change in view.

The 2014 Manual of Ideas interview (full text reposted [here](https://stockmarketeers.blogspot.com/2015/06/mark-massey-and-wide-moat-investing.html)) is the only extended first-person source. Massey calls the portfolio "The AltaRock Conglomerate": "we would never sell or trade one of our subsidiaries unless we were absolutely convinced that we were getting significantly more than we were giving up." On concentration: "most of the time we end up owning between five and ten businesses (stocks) at any one time, and we really try to buy for keeps." On patience: "feeling comfortable doing nothing for a long time is important." On markets as pari-mutuel betting: great businesses "are almost always priced very expensively... You only bet when you are convinced that you have a near cinch." His only screen is for heavy share buybacks. A mid-2010 partner letter ([Market Folly](https://www.marketfolly.com/2010/08/hedge-fund-altarocks-investing.html)) adds his view of cash: it is an option on bargains, but "A bargain in a great company is our holy grail; to ignore it in the hopes of even better deals in the future seems foolish to us."

His background, from the 2014 interview and OWL: Fidelity equity analyst from 1986, then his own firms (Eureka Capital, Massey Capital Management), then AltaRock, launched April 2002 after he read everything Buffett and Munger wrote. Some tracker sites say he worked at Baupost; nothing primary supports that.

No partner letters later than 2011 circulate publicly. No interviews, talks or podcasts after 2014 were found. No reason for any 2023-2026 trade has been stated anywhere public.

## The structure behind the 13F, and why it matters

From the April 2026 Form ADV and the Form D filings:

| Gross assets (ADV, 2026) | $2.41B | $3.25B |
| Investors | about 90 (Form D: 121) | about 2,100 (Form D feeder: 2,395) |
| Minimum | $5M | $100K |
| Owned by Massey and related persons | 16% | 5% |
| Cumulative subscriptions per Form D | $382M (Jan 2024), $1,215M (Jan 2025), $1,283M (Jan 2026) | $1,672M (Jan 2024), $1,850M (Jan 2025), $2,056M (Jan 2026) |

Three consequences for a reader.

First, **more than half the money is BBH private-banking clients.** Their flows come from a wealth manager's allocation decisions and client liquidity, not from the market. Form D shows gross money in (roughly $1.0B in 2024, about $270M in 2025) but never redemptions. The jump of $833M in AltaRock Fund LP's cumulative subscriptions during 2024 is unexplained. It could be large new money or a transfer between vehicles at the time of the LLC-to-LP succession; I couldn't tell which.

Second, **the 13F is about 90% of the book.** Regulatory AUM was about $5.41B at 2024-12-31 and $5.66B at 2025-12-31, against 13F totals of $4.94B and $5.10B. The rest (about $0.5B) is cash and/or non-US holdings. One non-US holding is confirmed: the 2024 N-PX shows a vote on 17,143 shares of **Constellation Software** (Toronto-listed, roughly $50M at spring-2024 prices by my estimate) in May 2024. It never appears on a 13F. No Constellation vote appears in the 2025 or 2026 N-PX, so it may have been sold, but that is not established. The fund documents allow leverage, shorts and options; no option or short exposure has ever appeared in a filing I read.

Third, **this is one person's book.** The ADV names Massey's loss as a key-person risk. Any sign of a change in who makes decisions (ADV Schedule A, a new signatory pattern, a sudden change in style) would matter far more than any single trade.

## How the portfolio behaved, 2015 to 2026

By 2021 Microsoft and Facebook had come in. Amazon arrived during 2022 and Facebook was gone. So the core names (TransDigm, Moody's, Mastercard and Visa) have been held since at least 2015. Alphabet was held from about 2019 to 2026, Microsoft from about 2020-21, Amazon from 2022. Roughly one new large position arrives every couple of years, and an old one leaves. That is the true turnover of ideas.

**Concentration and turnover.** There are always 8 to 10 rows, but only 5 or 6 positions above 3%. The top three went from 60% to 82% of the 13F over the three years. Estimated dollar turnover (the smaller of buys and sells, valued at average quarter-end prices, over average 13F value) was about 15% in 2024, 17% in 2025 and 14% in the first nine months of 2026. That is low in absolute terms, but busier than "buy for keeps" suggests, because they lean on position sizes a lot.

**They sell more than they buy, persistently.** Across the 12 quarters, estimated 13F buys were about $2.4B and sells about $4.3B, net about −$1.85B. Nine of twelve quarters were net sells; two quarters (Q1 2024, Q4 2025) had no purchases at all. Meanwhile regulatory AUM rose ($5.41B to $5.66B in 2025) and Form D shows fresh money coming in. Something is absorbing the cash: redemptions, the off-13F holdings, a cash build, or performance-allocation and fee withdrawals. The filings can't say which. *Inference, medium confidence:* the steady net selling is mostly fund-level (redemptions in a 2,000-investor BBH vehicle and an LP that charges incentive allocations on withdrawals), and partly a deliberate cash or non-US reserve. It is not a market call.

## The story of the three years, in order

Read this with the caveat that every "why" below is mine. AltaRock stated none of it.

**Late 2023 to mid 2024.** The book entered the period as TransDigm (28%), Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Visa, Moody's and Charter, with a small Mastercard stake. In Q4 2023 they more than doubled **Moody's** (+135%, at about $390) and added again in Q2 2024 (+36%). Everything else was trimmed a few percent a quarter. In Q2 2024 they sold 99% of **Charter**, a holding since at least 2015 and their largest position at end-2021 (about 23% of the 13F). That ended a long, apparently disappointing ownership as cable broadband came under competitive pressure. *Inference: thesis exit after a long decline. Medium confidence on "thesis", high confidence it was a deliberate exit.* A tiny residue lingered three more quarters. **Fair Isaac** appeared as a 1% starter position in Q2 2024.

**Second half of 2024.** Q3 2024 was a rotation inside the payment networks plus Amazon. Visa −37%, Mastercard +132% (taking Mastercard from 2.4% to 6%), Amazon +31% at about $186, Alphabet −17%. Fair Isaac rose from about $1,490 to about $1,990 and was 98% sold in Q4 2024, six months after purchase. *Inference: a valuation round-trip. The stock hit their price before the position was built out.* **Hilton** arrived as a new small position in Q4 2024.

**First half of 2025.** In Q1 2025 they added 36% to **Microsoft** at about $375, after the stock had fallen, and built Hilton. Q2 2025 brought the period's biggest decision: **Alphabet −70%** (from 10.7% of the book to 3.4%), with Visa cut another 37%, while Amazon (+21%) and Hilton (+99%) were added. Alphabet was sold mostly at its lows (implied quarter-end price about $155 to $176), during the 2025 worry about AI chatbots taking search share. That is the opposite of their usual sell-after-a-run pattern. *Inference, medium confidence: a change of view on Alphabet's moat or management, not valuation.* The only vote against management in three years of proxy records is their 2026 vote against Alphabet's say-on-pay. That is consistent with disaffection but is not proof. The rest of Alphabet was sold steadily through Q1 2026 as the stock more than doubled. About 40,000 shares remain.

**Second half of 2025.** Q3 2025 shows the clearest flow fingerprint in the record. Every holding except Amazon and Alphabet fell by exactly 0.4%, the Fair Isaac stub included (670 to 667 shares). Amazon was added 20% (to 30% of the book), and Alphabet was cut another 67%. In Q4 2025 they trimmed almost everything, with no buys: Microsoft −19% at about $484, Moody's −14%, Mastercard −7%. They exited the Fair Isaac stub.

**2026.** In Q1 2026 Hilton was 97% sold after about 15 months. Microsoft was cut another 10% while its price fell to about $370. Moody's rose slightly (+6%). The 13F fell to $4.13B, mostly from prices (my estimate: about −13.5% if nothing had traded). Q2 2026 was a large rotation, the busiest quarter in the period (gross trading about 26% of the book). **Amazon −24%** and **Moody's −65%** were sold. **Microsoft +25%** at about $373, **Visa +68%** and **Mastercard +46%** were bought, which took Visa back up after two years of cutting it. Q3 2026 continued the same direction: Amazon −16%, Moody's −56% (now 1.4%, effectively exiting a name held since at least 2015), Microsoft +13%. Microsoft became the largest position at 32%. The filing came in on 2026-11-13 at $4.21B.

The Moody's exit is not explained by price. The implied price was about $436 to $454 across Q1 to Q3 2026, and they were still adding in Q1. The N-PX shows 897,036 shares at the April 2026 meeting's record date, higher than at year-end. So something changed their view in spring 2026, or Moody's was simply the funding source for Microsoft and the networks. *Low confidence either way.*

**TransDigm** deserves its own line. It has been the anchor since at least 2015 (OWL estimates more than $1.5B of profit on it), and its share count fell in eleven of twelve quarters: 1.31M shares at 2023-09-30, 0.84M at 2026-09-30, −36%. Its weight stayed between 22% and 31%. *Inference, medium-high confidence:* this is size control on a position that kept growing, plus the default source of cash for flows, not a fading thesis.

## How to read their next 13F

**Expect a short, simple filing.** 8 to 10 rows, all common stock, SH, sole discretion, no options, no other managers, no confidential treatment. It should arrive on the last day or two before the deadline, under a filing-agent accession number (prefix 0001104659). Twice in twelve quarters it slipped one business day: Q4 2023 was accepted after the 5:30 pm cutoff on deadline day, and Q2 2026 was accepted on the Monday after a Friday deadline. A one-day slip is normal for them and means nothing. A slip of a week or more, or a 13F-NT, would be new.

**Separate flows from views by the shape of the selling.**
- *Near-identical small percentage cuts across most names in one quarter* (Q3 2025 at exactly −0.4% each; Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 at −3% to −7% in several names) are fund-level cash needs. These are redemptions, withdrawals or rebalancing between the two vehicles. Treat them as noise. Changes in the stubs are always noise.
- *TransDigm trims of 2% to 7% a quarter* are routine. Read a TransDigm cut of more than about 15% in one quarter, or its weight falling below about 18% without a price fall, as a real change.
- *Real decisions look like large changes (more than 20% of shares) in one to three names, usually paired:* money taken from one or two holdings and put into one or two others. Examples: Q3 2024 Visa to Mastercard and Amazon; Q2 2025 Alphabet and Visa to Amazon and Hilton; Q2 2026 Amazon and Moody's to Microsoft, Visa and Mastercard. Name both sides of the swap when you describe it.

**Check every big move against the quarter's price path before calling it a change of mind.** Their stated hurdle is a 15% ten-year return, so their default is to buy a held name after it falls and trim it after it rises. Microsoft shows this most clearly. They bought at about $375 (Q1 2025) and trimmed 19% at about $484 (Q4 2025). They trimmed another 10% in Q1 2026, a quarter in which the price fell, so that sale's timing within the quarter is unknown. They bought again at about $373 (Q2 2026). Quarter-end prices are only a proxy for trade prices. Visa shows it too: cut at $275 to $355, rebuilt around $300 to $343. A sale after a run is valuation discipline. A sale into weakness (Alphabet in Q2 2025, Moody's in 2026 at flat prices) is the kind that may signal a changed view of the business. Only in that case should you write "thesis" with any confidence, and then still as inference.

**New names start small and often don't survive.** Fair Isaac (2024) and Hilton (2024-26) both started below 3% and were gone within 6 to 15 months, apparently sold once price caught up. A new name means little until it passes about 5%. A new name that goes straight to 5% or more would be unusual and important. It could also be a name they owned years ago coming back, so check the history.

**Weights move a lot with prices.** In a six-stock book, one stock's quarter moves the weights several points: Microsoft's weight swung 16.8% → 20.5% → 32.1% over 2026, partly on price. Compare share counts, not weights. The implied price (value ÷ shares) is a quick check on what the stock did.

**Remember what the 13F can't see.** About 10% of AltaRock's assets are off-13F. At least one Canadian holding (Constellation Software) is invisible except through proxy votes. A drop in 13F value may be partly money moving into a foreign stock or cash, not out of the firm. Check the August N-PX each year: its say-on-pay rows name every company voted (foreign ones included) and give share counts at record dates between quarter-ends. Check the Form ADV each spring (regulatory AUM, fund gross assets, investor counts) and the January Form D amendments (gross subscriptions) to tell whether a quarter of selling coincides with asset shrinkage.

**Watch next:** whether Moody's and the Alphabet and Hilton stubs go to zero (expected), whether the Amazon trimming continues (two quarters in a row now, from 38% of the book at its peak), whether Microsoft's rise to the top position continues, and whether a new large name appears. With Moody's, Alphabet and Hilton gone, the book is effectively five stocks (Microsoft, Amazon, TransDigm, Mastercard, Visa). Past behaviour suggests a new idea usually arrives within a year or two of such a thinning. Any change of portfolio manager or signatory, or a material change in the ADV brochure's Item 8, outranks everything else.

## Confidence summary

- **Stated, primary:** philosophy, ten-year test, concentration intent, one decision-maker, same holdings across funds (ADV 2026); fee terms; the 15% hurdle (BBH via OWL, secondary quote of a primary document); "conglomerate" framing and buy-for-keeps language (2014 interview).
- **Shown by filings, high confidence:** holdings, weights, share changes, filing dates, the RAUM-vs-13F gap, the Constellation Software vote, net selling on the 13F book.
- **My inference:** flow vs view reading of uniform trims (high); TransDigm trims as size control (medium-high); valuation-driven trading around a fixed roster (medium-high); Alphabet sold on a change of view (medium); Charter exit as thesis exit (medium); Moody's exit reason (low); what absorbs the net selling (low to medium). A partner letter, an interview, or a later ADV/N-PX revealing large off-13F holdings would be the things most likely to change these.
