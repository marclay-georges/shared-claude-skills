# Fundsmith LLP: how to read its 13F

- **Filer:** Fundsmith LLP, London. CIK 0001569205, 13F file number 028-15226, CRD 160365.
- **People:** Terry Smith, founder, CEO and portfolio manager of Fundsmith Equity Fund. Julian Robins is a partner and minority owner of the Mauritius affiliate. Simon Barnard ran Smithson until 1 October 2026; Jonathan Imlah succeeds him, based in the US. Tom Armstrong, Director, signs the 13F and 13G filings.
- **Sister filer:** Fundsmith Investment Services Ltd (FISL), Mauritius, CIK 0001868537. It is the investment manager of the flagship fund and files its own 13F.
- **Period covered:** 13Fs for Q4 2023 (period ending 2023-12-31) through Q3 2026 (2026-09-30), plus letters, the February 2026 shareholder meeting, factsheets to October 2026, 13G/As, N-PX and Form ADV.
- **Written:** 2026-11-19. Sources and accession numbers are in `log.md`; LLP positions are in `holdings/`, FISL's in `holdings/fisl/` (same columns, same twelve quarters).

Throughout, **Said** marks the manager's own words, **Filings show** marks numbers from SEC filings, and **My read** marks my inference with a confidence level.

## The short version

Fundsmith is a quality-growth shop whose mantra for fifteen years was "Buy good companies. Don't overpay. Do nothing." For most of this window the 13F behaved accordingly: about 20 large US names carried 90% of the value, entries and exits were rare and explained in the January and July letters, and most quarter-to-quarter change was the fund shrinking. The flagship fund went from about £23bn at the start of 2024 to about £11bn by September 2026 on four-plus years of underperformance and heavy redemptions. Then, in the first half of 2026, Smith changed the process. Turnover went from 3–13% a year to 51.8% in six months. He said the fund would "take more account of momentum" and "be more active in future". Half the large-cap book was replaced.

A reader of the next filing should hold three things in mind. First, this 13F is not the fund. It is roughly three-quarters of the group's US large-cap position: the sister company FISL files the rest separately. It sits beside a basket of Smithson small caps that belong to a different team. And it leaves out the non-US holdings: about 25–30% of the flagship before the 2026 rotation and about 15–20% since. Second, the default reading of a cut across many names is still redemptions, not a change of view. But Fundsmith now says it will trade more, so a single large cut in a big name can be a real decision. The letters, the meeting and the monthly factsheet are where you tell the two apart. Third, the Smithson sleeve has a new US-based manager from October 2026 and a new US entity being appointed. Changes there, or the sleeve's disappearance from this filer, say nothing about Terry Smith's views.

## What the 13F actually contains

This section comes before the history because every number later depends on it.

**Several vehicles in one filing.** Fundsmith LLP's 13F aggregates every US-listed holding the LLP has discretion over:

- Fundsmith Equity Fund (FEF, the UK OEIC, by far the largest)
- the Fundsmith SICAV versions of it
- Fundsmith Stewardship Fund (called Sustainable Equity Fund until 24 March 2025)
- the Delaware LPs named in the LLP's Form ADV
- Smithson, an investment trust until its liquidation in February 2026, then the Smithson Equity Fund OEIC

The rows sort themselves by size. About 20 names at 1.5–9% of the filing are the large-cap strategy that FEF and Stewardship share. A tail of 15–25 names at 0.05–0.6% is mostly Smithson: Sabre, Qualys, Paycom, Napco, Doximity, Medpace, Catalyst, ADMA, Nutanix, Badger Meter, FICO, InterDigital, Rambus, Credo, Rollins, Verisign, MSCI, Graco, Exponent, Oddity, Cognex, IDEX, Choice Hotels, Vertiv, Manhattan Associates and others.

A few tiny large-cap lines come from the Stewardship fund, because it holds some names FEF does not:

- Mastercard until 2025
- Home Depot
- McDonald's
- Johnson & Johnson
- early Zoetis

**My read (high confidence):** the vehicle attribution is inferred from size, timing and overlap with each fund's published portfolio. No filing states it, and the 13F has no other-manager column entries to help.

**A sister filer holds the rest.** FISL in Mauritius is FEF's investment manager. Under its own CIK it files a separate 13F, about $4.5bn in Q2 2026. **Filings show:** the 2024 group 13G/As give the whole group's holdings at 31 December 2023:

| Stock | Group shares (13G/A) | LLP 13F shares | LLP's share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Waters | 4.94m | 3.80m | 77% |
| McCormick | 19.17m | 15.09m | 79% |
| Brown-Forman B | 15.67m | 11.65m | 74% |

At 31 March 2026 the Waters figure was again 75% (3.21m of 4.31m). Sabre, a Smithson name, was 100% in the LLP both times. FISL's own twelve 13Fs confirm this; see the next section for how to combine them. The LLP filing lists no other included managers and says nothing about FISL.

**Combining the LLP and FISL into the true US-listed book.** **Filings show** (`holdings/fisl/`, all twelve FISL 13Fs reconcile with their covers):

| 2024-Q4 | 23.46 | 7.69 | 31.15 | 75.3% |
| 2025-Q2 | 23.02 | 7.39 | 30.40 | 75.7% |

| 2026-Q2 | 13.65 | 4.52 | 18.17 | 75.1% |
| 2026-Q3 | 12.92 | 4.15 | 17.07 | 75.7% |

- **The split is stable.** In every quarter, for every large-cap name the two filers share, the LLP holds 69–82% of the combined shares (median 74–77%).
- **Changes nearly always match.** Matching by CUSIP, the LLP's quarter-on-quarter change in a large name was within 8 points of the combined change in all but four cases over 11 transitions:
  - Zoetis: the Q1 2025 build ran a little faster at FISL.
  - Procter & Gamble in Q2 2026: LLP -3%, combined -13%, because FISL cut from 1.76m to 1.09m shares.
  - Veeva and Yum in Q3 2026: builds, with FISL proportionally slightly ahead.
- **No shifting between filers.** I found no quarter where a position moved from one filer to the other.
- **The Smithson tail is LLP-only.**
- **FISL-only lines are small and not FEF decisions.** All are under 0.6% of FISL. Examples:
  - First Citizens, Nvidia, Reddit, Shopify, Coupang and IES Holdings in 2024–25
  - Waste Management in 2025–26
  - Paychex in Q3 2026
  - Badger Meter in Q4 2025
  - tiny Apple, Amazon, TJX and TSMC lines from December 2025

The tiny December 2025 lines match the Fundsmith Equity ETF's share counts exactly, for example Amazon 715 and Apple 605 shares. The ETF launched on 1 December 2025 and FISL advises it. **My read (medium confidence):** the other FISL-only names are an unaffiliated US mandate that FISL sub-advises (its Form ADV lists one). They are not evidence of a Terry Smith view.

**How to use this.** For a large name, the LLP's percentage change is a reliable proxy for the group's. Divide the LLP share count by about 0.75 for the group position. Check FISL only in these cases:
- when you need the firm-wide dollar amount;
- when the LLP move is small but the story matters (P&G in Q2 2026 looked flat at the LLP and was a 13% group cut);
- when a name appears or disappears at one filer only. A new FISL-only line is most likely the ETF or the third-party mandate. A name dropping out of the LLP while staying at FISL would be the first sign of a real shift between filers, which has not happened yet.

**Non-US holdings are invisible, and that has mattered.**

- **Filings show / Said:** at end 2024 the flagship was about 75% US by listing. At December 2025 it was about 70%. After the 2026 rotation it was 84% at 31 July 2026, 82% at 30 September and 81.6% at 30 October (factsheets; cash 1–3%).
- **Filings show (N-PORT):** the Fundsmith Equity ETF (ETFT, ALPS Series Trust) files full holdings, non-US names included. It is the same team's strategy but not identical to FEF: it held Apple, Amazon and Danone. Non-US-listed stocks as a share of the ETF's equities:
  - 35.5% at 31 December 2025, 12 of 32 names
  - 31.8% at 31 March 2026
  - 15.0% at 30 June 2026: Amadeus, L'Oréal, Legrand and Sage
  - about 20% at 30 October 2026, from its website country split, after Bureau Veritas appeared
  - TSMC is held as the US-listed ADR; it is counted as US above but is a Taiwanese issuer.

  This confirms the factsheet picture. The 13F missed about a third of the book before the rotation and misses about a sixth to a fifth now.
- Names that were never in the 13F include Novo Nordisk, L'Oréal, LVMH, Coloplast, Atlas Copco, EssilorLuxottica, Wolters Kluwer, Unilever, Diageo, Amadeus, Legrand, Sage and Magnum Ice Cream. Several were major decisions in this window.
- **My read (high confidence):** moving money from non-US into US names makes the 13F total rise while the fund shrinks. In Q2 2026 the 13F total went up from $12.8bn to $13.6bn while FEF lost assets. Non-US sales such as Novo, Unilever, Atlas Copco and LVMH were funding new US positions. Never read a rising 13F total here as inflows.

**Filing mechanics that apply to this filer:**

- All twelve quarters reconcile exactly with their cover pages.
- There are no amendments, no 13F-NT notices and no confidential-treatment gaps.
- There are no options, no puts or calls and no PRN rows. Every row is SH with SOLE discretion. That fits the firm's "No Debt or Derivatives / No Shorting" rules (factsheet) and the Form ADV statement that it uses no derivatives.
- The accession prefix changed twice: agent 0001520023, then 0001801547 for Q2 2025, then the firm's own CIK from Q3 2025. Find filings in the CIK folder, not by guessing from a prefix.
- The filing is signed from Mauritius by Tom Armstrong.
- McCormick non-voting shares correctly show all-"None" voting authority. Any other row with "None" voting would be worth a look.
- Filed dates are the usual 14th–17th of the second month after quarter end.

## Who they are and what they say they do

**Said (enduring):** the Owner's Manual and every factsheet state the fund invests in "high quality businesses that can sustain a high return on operating capital employed", "whose advantages are difficult to replicate", that do not need leverage, can reinvest at high returns, are "resilient to change, particularly technological innovation", and are attractively valued. It "generally invests in 20 to 30 stocks". Its values line reads "No Debt or Derivatives, No Shorting, No Market Timing, No Index Hugging, No Trading, No Hedging". Fees: 1.05% OCF on the T class. The Form ADV gives 1.0% / 0.9% for the US vehicles. ([Owner's Manual and documents](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/documents/), [factsheet](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/factsheet))

**Said, on pay:** nowhere in the material I read does Smith set out a voting policy. **Filings show:** in the year to June 2026, FISL voted against say-on-pay at 20 of 23 US companies, including Microsoft, Alphabet, Visa, Stryker, Waters, PMI, ADP, Marriott and TI. The LLP, voting the Smithson names, voted against at 7 of 12. The earlier years are the same:
- FISL voted against 17 of 21 say-on-pay items in the year to June 2024 and 20 of 24 in the year to June 2025.
- IDEXX was the steady exception, voted for in all three years. Home Depot and McDonald's, both Stewardship names, were also voted for.
- The LLP voted against about 9 of 12–13 items in each of 2024 and 2025. Its 2024 N-PX/A restated a single Paycom vote from for to against.

Voting against management pay is routine for them and says nothing about intent to sell.

**Ownership:** 13G Item 8 and the Form ADV say FISL is 77.83% owned by Eighth Wonder Limited under The Eighth Wonder Foundation, which Terry Smith set up; Julian Robins holds 13.83% and Mark Laurence 8.34%. The LLP is a "Securities Affiliate" of FISL. Regulatory AUM at 31 March 2026 was $18.5bn for the LLP and $23.8bn for FISL, much of it the same money counted at both. The LLP's Form ADV Part 1 (26 June 2026) adds detail:
- 5 discretionary accounts
- $17.9bn of AUM from non-US clients
- 43 employees, 5 of them in investment roles
- two Delaware private funds: Fundsmith Equity Fund LP (gross assets $505m) and a second LP ($104m)

The US-domiciled money is small. The 13F is driven by UK and European fund flows.

## The three years, in order

### 2023 into 2024: "do nothing", and the outflows begin

**Said:** the [2023 letter](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/31plodnq/2023-fef-annual-letter-to-shareholders.pdf), written in January 2024, reported FEF +12.4% against the MSCI World's +16.8%. The detractors were the consumer staples and tools: Estée Lauder, McCormick, Diageo, Mettler-Toledo and Brown-Forman. The winners were Meta, Microsoft, Novo, L'Oréal and IDEXX. They sold Adobe, Amazon and Estée Lauder, bought Procter & Gamble, Marriott and Fortinet, and turnover was 11.1%. On AI: they would not try to pick winners, "we will suspend judgement." The [H1 2024 letter](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/uznnt5w2/2024-fef-semi-annual-letter-to-shareholders.pdf) reported +9.3% against +12.7% and turnover of 3.7%. It said they had begun accumulating Texas Instruments and an undisclosed name, later revealed as Atlas Copco.

**Filings show:**

- At 31 December 2023 the LLP 13F was $23.9bn across 40 names. The top ten were 65% of value: Microsoft 12.5%, Meta 7.4%, Stryker, IDEXX, Visa, Philip Morris, ADP, Waters, PepsiCo and McCormick.
- Through 2024 the large names were trimmed by a near-uniform 1–3% a quarter. Microsoft went from 7.94m shares to 6.19m over the year.
- Texas Instruments appeared in Q1 2024 and built gradually.
- McCormick fell from 15.1m to 12.2m to 10.0m to 1.7m shares and was gone in Q4 2024. The November 2024 13G/A shows the group at 0.8%.
- Apple, a small holding, exited in Q4 2024.

**Said:** the [2024 letter](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/pirmvyly/annual-letter-to-shareholders-2024.pdf) reported +8.9% against +20.8% and turnover of 3.2%. It gave reasons for the exits:

- **Diageo:** management, plus the threat from weight-loss drugs.
- **McCormick:** slow to pass on input costs, and own-label competition.
- **Apple:** its rating rose about 50% above the S&P while sales grew 2%.

The [2024 short-form report](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/2l1p53ou/2024-short-form-report-for-the-twelve-months-ended-31-december-2024.pdf) shows the asymmetry: £0.79bn of purchases against £3.70bn of sales. The largest sales were McCormick £835m, Novo £546m, Microsoft £517m, Diageo £490m and PMI £294m. The T accumulation share count fell from 605.7m to 549.3m.

**My read (high confidence):** 2024 is the template for an outflow year. Uniform small trims everywhere fund redemptions. One or two names are sold out entirely for stated business reasons, and the sale proceeds partly fund the redemptions too. A reader seeing McCormick fall 19% in Q1 2024 would have been premature to call it a decision; by Q3 (-83%) it clearly was, and the letter confirmed it.

### 2025: the selective cuts, and Big Tech halved

**Said:**

- The [H1 2025 letter](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/bvgden5v/2025-fef-semi-annual-letter-to-shareholders.pdf) reported -1.9% against +0.1% and turnover of 9.2%. They had bought Zoetis, Intuit and EssilorLuxottica, and sold PepsiCo and Brown-Forman.
- On Novo and Coloplast the letter described "increasingly thin patience".
- The Stewardship fund's [2025 letter](https://www.fundsmith.eu/media/qvkearfi/2025-annual-letter.pdf) says it sold Mastercard "ahead of the Trump administration proposals to cap rates on credit card lending". That is the small Mastercard exit in the Q3 2025 13F, and it is a Stewardship decision, not FEF's.

**Filings show:**

- PepsiCo exited in Q1 2025, Brown-Forman in Q2 2025.
- Zoetis went from 0.23m to 2.55m shares in Q1 2025 and kept building to 3.9m by Q4.
- Intuit was new in Q1 2025.
- In Q3 2025 Microsoft was cut 48% (4.94m to 2.58m) and Meta 56% (3.57m to 1.56m), while Waters, Zoetis and Intuit were added to.
- Alphabet was cut 44% in Q4 2025, along with Otis (-47%), IDEXX (-28%) and Stryker (-20%).
- The LLP 13F total fell from $23.0bn (Q2) to $17.1bn (Q4).

**Said:**

- The [2025 letter](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/4hcfd1pg/2025-fef-annual-letter-web.pdf), written in January 2026, reported +0.8% against +12.8% and turnover of 12.7%. It blamed index concentration, passive flows and dollar weakness. Smith said he would neither index-hug nor chase momentum, "We are not going to do either", yet ended: "Any year that you don't destroy one of your best-loved ideas is probably a wasted year… More to follow."
- On Novo, fewer businesses "can be run by an idiot" than he had thought, and "engagement to change it is less effective than selling".
- The [2025 short-form report](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/uxvf5sjz/2025-short-form-report-for-the-twelve-months-ended-31-december-2025.pdf) shows the largest sales: Meta £1.70bn, Microsoft £1.45bn, PepsiCo £669m, Alphabet £646m and PMI £624m. Total sales were £7.69bn against £1.39bn of purchases. Estimated outflows for the year were about £6.5bn (Citywire).

**Said, at the [February 2026 shareholder meeting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W8vYeazBox4)**. The transcript extract does not attribute every answer; Smith and Julian Robins were both answering.

- On Microsoft dropping out of the top ten: "we roughly halved the holding in Microsoft Meta and slightly less so in Alphabet… because of concerns about what we're seeing… in terms of the spending."
- They put 2026 hyperscaler capex at 87% (Microsoft), 91% (Meta) and 97% (Alphabet) of free cash flow. "These are not going to be capital light businesses. This is going to change fundamentally the characteristics of those companies in a way that we don't like. So yes, we sold them for that reason."
- They added that FEF had 5.2m Microsoft shares (7.4% of the fund) a year earlier and was now "under 2 million… but the fund size has gone down by 30% as well. So there's kind of a lot of moving parts."

**Said, same meeting, on how they meet redemptions.** This is the most useful single passage for reading this filer:

> "We're subject to a thing called the concentration rules… we can't have more than 10% of your fund in one company and we can't have more than 40% of your fund in companies which are over 5%… quite often the thing that we're selling is something to prevent a breach in those rules… that's quite often where the liquidity comes from. Outside of that, we think about things along the lines of valuation."

**My read (high confidence):** the 2025 Big Tech cuts were both things at once. They were the most liquid, most appreciated and largest positions, which made them the natural source of redemption cash under the 5/10/40 rule. They were also a stated change of view on AI capex. The 13F alone could not separate the two; the meeting did. Expect the same ambiguity in any outflow quarter: the biggest winners get cut hardest for mechanical reasons, so look for a statement before calling it conviction.

### Late 2025 to Q1 2026: Smithson leaves the building

**Said / reported:**

- In November 2025 Smithson's board proposed rolling the trust into an OEIC. Saba Capital had built a 16% stake and the discount had persisted ([RNS, 12 November 2025](https://www.londonstockexchange.com/en/news/article/restructure-proposals/6b21-87-31-8a-55-410191)).
- 58.6% of holders chose cash: about £967m left and £682m rolled into Smithson Equity Fund ([AIC, February 2026](https://www.theaic.co.uk/aic/news/industry-news/over-half-of-smithson-shareholders-opt-for-cash-exit)).
- The liquidation became effective on 27 February 2026.

**Filings show:**

- In the Q1 2026 13F almost every small cap fell by 58% (Rollins, Paycom, Verisign, Nutanix, Doximity, Clorox, Manhattan Associates, Sabre and others). Sabre went from 21.5m to 9.0m shares, confirmed by the May 2026 13G/A.
- Oddity and Graco exited.
- The small-cap tail fell from 6.8% to 2.9% of the filing.
- The large names were also cut 8–22% in the same quarter, and Intuit and Nike exited. The total fell from $17.1bn to $12.8bn.

**My read (high confidence):** an identical percentage cut across a block of names is a vehicle event, not a view. In Q1 2026 two things happened at once: the Smithson cash exit (the uniform 58%) and continuing FEF redemptions plus the start of the rotation (the large names).

### First half of 2026: the strategy change

**Said** ([H1 2026 letter, July 2026](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/lfhpxi1x/fundsmith-equity-fund-semi-annual-letter-to-shareholders-2026.pdf)):

- FEF returned -2.9% against +11.2%, with turnover of 51.8%. "A buy and hold strategy can only work if you are not subject to flows, and we are… You should therefore expect that we will be more active in future."
- "We will take more account of momentum, both fundamental and share price… we will be much less willing to deploy the time-honoured technique of buying quality companies when they hit a glitch."
- He stressed that turnover of 51% should not become "the norm" but "may well be higher than our historic average".
- Bought: AppLovin, GE Vernova, Legrand, Mastercard, Netflix, Nextpower, Sage, TJX, TSMC, Uber, Veeva and Yum! Brands.
- "Exited or have started exiting": Atlas Copco, Coloplast, EssilorLuxottica, Intuit, LVMH, Magnum Ice Cream, Mettler-Toledo, Nike, Novo Nordisk, Otis, Unilever, Wolters Kluwer and Zoetis.
- Reduced IDEXX "on valuation grounds" and Microsoft.
- Appendix 2 gives a reason for every trade. Some examples:
  - **Zoetis:** "unimpressed with management".
  - **Intuit:** switched into Sage, which has less stock-based compensation and a lower rating.
  - **Atlas Copco:** anaemic growth for a sub-3% FCF yield.
  - **Mastercard:** bought alongside Visa "to achieve >6% exposure to payments without excessive stock-specific risk or breaching UCITS concentration rules".
- He acknowledged selling some names at a loss or soon after buying them, and buying back names sold before.

**Filings show (Q2 2026):**

- New large positions: Uber 4.7%, Mastercard 4.7%, TSMC ADR 4.0%, Netflix 3.7%, GE Vernova 3.5%, AppLovin 2.6%, TJX 2.5%, Nextpower 2.5%, Veeva 1.1% and Yum 0.7%.
- Exits: Mettler-Toledo, Zoetis and Otis.
- Deep cuts to the old core: Philip Morris -43%, Fortinet -41%, Alphabet -40%, Visa -35%, ADP -33%, Texas Instruments -31%, IDEXX -30%, Waters -29% and Microsoft -23%.
- Entries rose from 34 to 41, and the top ten fell from 69% to 53% of the filing.

**My read:**

- *Visa down while Mastercard came in:* this is the stated payments split. High confidence.
- *IDEXX and Microsoft:* stated. High.
- *PMI, Fortinet, Alphabet, ADP, TI and Waters:* these are still holdings, and Fortinet, TI, Alphabet and PMI were the top H1 contributors. I read these cuts as funding the twelve purchases and redemptions, and as trimming winners toward the concentration limits, rather than as loss of conviction. Medium confidence. Nothing names them as reductions.

### Q3 2026: finishing the rotation in July, then quiet

**Said:** the [July 2026 factsheet](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/yshdfb44/july.pdf) said "A number of portfolio changes were implemented in July. These are detailed in the Semi-Annual Letter." The [August](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/rs5hmxd0/august.pdf), [September](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/n4hdflc1/september.pdf) and October factsheets each say "There were no outright sales or purchases of holdings made in the month." FEF had 28 holdings throughout. Fund size fell from £11.8bn (July) to £11.1bn (September) to £10.9bn (October).

**Filings show (Q3 2026):**

- Builds: Yum +648% (0.56m to 4.16m shares, now 4.4%), Veeva +231% (to 6.0%), Nextpower +47%, TJX +44%, Mastercard +17% and Visa +11%.
- Cuts: GE Vernova -57%, Microsoft -51%, Marriott -47%, Alphabet -45%, Meta -43%, P&G -36%, IDEXX -30% and ADP -29%.
- Smithson sleeve: Rambus and Credo new, Clorox exited.
- At 30 September the top three were Waters 7.7%, Visa 6.3% and Mastercard 6.2%. Microsoft was 3.1%, Meta 3.4% and Alphabet 2.6%.

**My read (medium-high confidence):** Veeva and Yum were "began accumulating" names in the letter, so their Q3 growth is completed builds. Microsoft and IDEXX continue stated reductions. The Marriott, P&G, ADP, Alphabet, Meta and GE Vernova cuts are not explained by name anywhere I found.

"Outright" in the factsheets means whole new positions or full exits. Trims and top-ups go unreported there. So the August–September quiet does not prove these cuts all happened in July, though the July note makes July most likely.

Cutting GE Vernova by more than half within a quarter of buying it is new behaviour for this manager and fits the stated momentum discipline. The stock was a top-five detractor in August. That fit is my interpretation, not theirs. Marriott had been a top contributor in H1 and was over 7% of the filing, which makes concentration-rule trimming plausible.

Note one third-party error. A widely read 13F tracker, investortracks, labels the Veeva change as adjusted for a 1-for-2 reverse split. Veeva has had no split, and the raw change is +231%.

## How to read the next filing

The next 13F covers the quarter to 31 December 2026 and should arrive around 14–17 February 2027. Fundsmith's own explanation for that quarter will come in the annual letter and short-form report in January 2027 and at the shareholder meeting around February 2027. Monthly factsheets say whether any whole positions were opened or closed, and the short-form report gives the year's five largest purchases and sales in sterling.

**Start by splitting the filing into its parts.**

1. The large-cap strategy: about 20–25 names above 1.5%. This is Terry Smith's FEF and Stewardship book.
2. The Smithson small-cap tail: below about 0.6%.
3. Any odd tiny large-cap line, which is probably Stewardship-only.

Read each part separately. A change in the tail is Smithson's, now Jonathan Imlah's. It tells you nothing about Smith.

**Then ask whether a change is a flow or a decision.**

- **Probably flow (noise).** Most large names cut by similar percentages, and the 13F total down. FEF has had net redemptions every year in this window. Also, under the 5/10/40 rule, the largest and best-performing names get cut harder for mechanical reasons. A 10–20% cut in Waters, Visa or Stryker in a quarter when the fund shrank is not a view.
- **Probably a decision (signal).**
  - A new name above about 1%.
  - A full exit of a large-cap name.
  - A cut of more than 40% in one large name while others are flat or added to.
  - A name built over two or three consecutive quarters. The factsheet hides positions "being built", so a quietly growing 13F line is often the first public sign.
  - Under the 2026 process, a quick reversal of a recent purchase is also a decision. It is not a mistake in the data.
- **Vehicle events.** An identical percentage across a whole block of names is a fund closing, merging or being restructured. The Smithson cash exit gave 58% across the tail in Q1 2026. Watch for the same when the new Smithson manager takes over.

**Where this filing misleads, specifically.**

- **It is not the whole group.** FISL holds roughly another third on top of the LLP's figure in each large-cap name. The two usually move together (see "Combining the LLP and FISL"). Check FISL's 13F (CIK 0001868537, filed the same day) before writing a firm-wide dollar figure, or when an LLP change looks too small for the story.
- **It cannot see the non-US half of the decisions.** Novo, L'Oréal, LVMH, Unilever, Diageo, Atlas Copco, Coloplast, Essilor, Wolters Kluwer and Magnum all went unseen. So do the current non-US holdings: Amadeus and L'Oréal, both top ten in October 2026, and Legrand and Sage. A rising 13F total or more US names can simply mean money moved out of Europe.
- **13F weights are not fund weights.** Waters at 7.7% of this filing is not 7.7% of FEF. The factsheet's top ten, which is unweighted, and its country split are the cross-check.
- **The 45-day lag is long relative to letters.** The July letter described H1 trades that only fully appeared in the Q2 and Q3 filings. Read the letter for the half before calling a 13F move unexplained.

**What to watch for specifically in late 2026 and 2027.**

- **Whether the "more active" regime persists.** The factsheets show no outright trades from August to October 2026. If the Q4 2026 filing is similarly quiet in the large-cap block apart from flow trims, the H1 2026 burst was a one-off reset. If new names keep arriving and recent buys keep being cut, momentum is now part of the process and the old "do nothing" prior should be dropped for good.
- **Further reduction of Microsoft, Meta and Alphabet.** The stated thesis, AI capex destroying capital-light economics, implies further reduction rather than re-entry. A re-buy would be a notable reversal and should come with an explanation.
- **Smithson.** Simon Barnard stepped down on 1 October 2026. Jonathan Imlah, US-based, takes over, and Fundsmith Partners US LLC "will be appointed portfolio manager" ([letter, 14 September 2026](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/xjwpufit/portfolio-manager-change-september-2026.pdf)). Expect turnover in the tail. If the small caps vanish from this filing, check whether a new 13F filer has appeared before calling it a sale.
- **Flows.** FEF assets fell about 50% in under three years. Flows dominate quarter-to-quarter change unless redemptions stop. The letters, monthly fund size and trade-press flow estimates (Citywire, Calastone) are the evidence.

## What would change my mind

- **On the FISL ratio:** I checked the "LLP is about three-quarters of the group" figure against all twelve FISL 13Fs, and it held at 74–78% of combined value. If a quarter breaks that range, use FISL's own numbers. Possible causes are the ETF (about $8m in November 2026, immaterial so far) or a new US client growing inside FISL.
- **On vehicle attribution:** a Smithson Equity Fund or Stewardship Fund holdings report naming a tail stock I've attributed elsewhere would correct it.

If they remain holdings and go unmentioned, my flow-and-concentration reading stands.
