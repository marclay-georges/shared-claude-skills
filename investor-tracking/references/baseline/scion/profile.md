## 1. What a future reader most needs to know

If you are reading this with a "new Burry disclosure" in hand, it is almost certainly not a 13F. It is a Substack post, a press story about one, or an X post. Four things matter most.

1. **The 13F record is over, and while it existed it showed little of what he owned.** Scion's 13Fs were small (long stock between $13M and $111M a quarter) and turned over almost completely each quarter. They missed his Hong Kong, Korean and Australian shares, his OTC holdings such as Fannie Mae, his short sales and his intra-quarter trades. Burry himself says in his Substack's About page that the media "wildly misinterpreted many of my mandatory SEC filings". Use the 13F history to understand his habits, not to estimate what he owns today.
2. **Option notional is not money at risk.** A 13F reports an option at the value of the underlying shares. The Q3 2025 Palantir puts were reported at $912M. Burry said they cost $9.2M (50,000 contracts at $1.84, $50 strike, 2027 expiry) ([Benzinga quoting his 2025-11-13 X post](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/hedge-funds/25/11/48856898/michael-burry-clears-the-air-on-math-behind-his-palantir-options-bet-teases-new-move-after-shutting-down-hedge-fund)). Headlines like "Burry bets $X against Y" have overstated the cash he put up by roughly 10x to 100x every time.
3. **His puts were sometimes hedges, not bets against the company.** In Q3 2024 and Q1 2025, the puts on China ADRs sat against China and Hong Kong holdings. The Q1 2025 cover page says the puts "may serve to hedge long positions which are not eligible to be reported". Read a put by asking what it might be protecting before you read it as a forecast.
4. **He trades fast and changes his mind in public.** That holds for the funds and for his personal account. Alibaba and JD went from long stock (2024) to puts (Q1 2025) to calls (Q2 2025). He sold Lululemon in October 2026 and bought it back on 2026-11-11. One trade means little. A long write-up he keeps adding to over months means much more.

## 2. Who he is and what he is trying to do

**Said.** Burry describes himself as a value investor who came to markets through writing. He was a Stanford neurology resident who ran Valuestocks.net in the late 1990s. His first Substack post reprints a 1999 article titled "Buffett Revisited" ([Foundations: My 1999](https://michaeljburry.substack.com/p/foundations-my-1999-and-part-of-2000), 2025-11-23). He ran Scion Capital from 2000 to 2008, the fund in *The Big Short*. He then ran Scion Asset Management, from 2013, as what he called "essentially a friends and family fund. I didn't market it or treat it like most do, and I wasn't trying to grow assets" ([Bloomberg, 2025-11-18](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-18/burry-says-he-s-active-in-markets-after-fund-is-deregistered)). He says the compliance rules that came with running outside money "effectively muzzled my ability to communicate", leaving him only "cryptic fragments" ([About page](https://michaeljburry.substack.com/about)).

His stated style has three parts, and they pull against each other:
- **A patient value buyer of mid-caps.** "These are all 3-5 year holds minimum ... The 2-12B market cap range is the most fertile area as I see it today" (Substack post of 2025-11-26, quoted by [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/big-short-michael-burry-substack-stock-portfolio-lululemon-fannie-mae-2025-11)). He likes companies with "significant owners earnings, little debt, and large buybacks" at under 10x earnings. He compares his 2026 buying to his purchases of Ross Stores, Clayton Homes and Dun & Bradstreet in 2000 ([Trading Post 2026-06-12](https://michaeljburry.substack.com/p/trading-post-june-12-2026)). He keeps returning to beaten-down healthcare insurers (Molina, "Ghosts of GEICO Past", 2025-12-30), consumer brands (Lululemon, Estée Lauder, VF), China internet and Hong Kong (he ran Asia funds from a Hong Kong office from about 2005), and payments and software.
- **A bubble-spotter who expresses that view with cheap, convex options.** Since late 2025 his public theme is that AI is a capital-spending bubble like the 1999–2000 fibre and router build-out, with Nvidia cast as Cisco. He argues hyperscalers understate depreciation (by $176B over 2026–2028, in his estimate; [X post 2025-11-10](https://www.mixedtimes.com/technology/big-short-investor-michael-burry-accuses-ai-giants-of-inflating-earnings-through-accounting-tricks)) and that stock-based compensation is understated as a cost. On the Michael Lewis podcast (2025-12-02) he said he bought far out-of-the-money puts on Palantir and Nvidia with about two years to run: "I thought two years would be enough" ([transcript excerpt](https://www.realclearpolitics.com/video/2025/12/05/michael_burry_nvidia_is_like_cisco_in_2000_were_near_the_level_of_when_the_nasdaq_peaked_in_the_dot-com_bubble.html)).
- **Early, and he knows it.** On 2023-01-31 he tweeted "Sell" and on 2023-03-30 he wrote "I was wrong to say sell" ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2023-03-31/michael-burry-i-was-wrong-say-sell)). In May 2026 he wrote: "I am now a meme for the number of times I have called a crash. I have become the boy who cried wolf" ([About That Boy Who Cried Wolf, 2026-05-11](https://michaeljburry.substack.com/p/short-thoughts-may-10-2026)).

**Inferred (high confidence).** The useful model is a stock-picker with a large, rotating set of cheap longs and a separate, small-premium put book that carries his macro view. The longs are where he does his work. The puts are where the headlines come from. When he says he is "living through spring of 2000 again", look for it in how the put book is built and how fast he cuts losing longs. Do not expect the long book to shrink much. In October 2026 he said he planned to spend the next 6–9 months "minimizing losses" on longs and keeping "maximum downside leverage" (paid post summarised by quantdata.uk; secondary).

## 5. How to read a Burry disclosure from now on

**What is signal.**
- *A long write-up and then repeated adds on weakness.* Molina (written up in December 2025, added in August 2026), MercadoLibre, Adobe, JD and Fannie/Freddie follow this pattern. These are the closest thing he has to conviction holdings. When he calls something a "full position", treat it as sized to his normal maximum for a single name. He has not published a percentage for "full", and he has said one can drift down in relative size without his selling.
- *Changes in how the bear view is built.* Consolidating into index and sector puts (October 2026), "moving timelines up", or switching from puts to outright shorts (floated on 2026-11-16) tell you about his timing and conviction more than any single ticker does.
- *Explicit exits with a reason.* For example, the Alphabet donation "akin to a sell" on a weaker five-to-six-year view.
- *A name that has appeared across several regimes.* JD has been held as stock, puts and calls in 10 of the 12 filings and is still held in 2026. Molina, Lululemon and Alibaba/Hong Kong are similar. Persistence across a decade of churn is rare for him and therefore meaningful.

**What is usually noise.**
- *Single Trading Post buys and sells in Q4.* He says Q4 is tax-loss season. The Fannie/Freddie swap and the restructuring of the semiconductor puts both realised tax losses. An exit in October or November can be a tax trade that he reverses within weeks: Lululemon was sold in October and rebought on 2026-11-11.
- *Closing a put after a fall in the underlying*, such as the Oracle and Caterpillar puts on 2026-11-16 "after continued downward volatility". This is taking profit or managing premium, not a change of view.
- *End-of-quarter trades.* He says he trades against June window dressing (2026-06-30).
- *Market-timing calls.* His "Sell", "crash" and "bubble" calls have often been early: a two-month reversal in 2023, and in May 2026 a "boy who cried wolf" post while the market kept rising. The position change that goes with the call carries more information than the call does.
- *Starter positions in round lots* or "small but full" positions in lists of 5–10 names. In the 13F era most new names were gone within one or two quarters.

**How to read an options headline.** First turn it into contracts: shares ÷ 100. Then ask what premium is plausible. He buys far out-of-the-money, long-dated options, so premiums are typically 1–5% of notional or less. Then ask whether it is a hedge: does he hold the same or a related exposure in a form that is not shown? Finally, ask whether he has said anything himself. He now usually gives the strike and expiry in the paid post, and the free preview or press often repeats them. Never report a 13F-style notional as the size of his bet.

**Where the record can mislead.** Since late 2025 the record is self-reported, unaudited and partly paywalled. Free previews give only the first few paragraphs. Aggregators that summarise paid posts (from2028.com, quantdata.uk, themarkets.today, TradeandTell) are useful for detail but sometimes disagree on strikes and expiries; prefer CNBC, Reuters, Bloomberg or Business Insider for anything you will act on. He also runs a subscription business with an incentive to be interesting. I have no evidence he misstates positions, but there is no outside check. A filing can still appear in one case: if he crosses 5% of a US-registered company he must file Schedule 13D or 13G, and at 10% Forms 3 and 4. A small cap like Build-A-Bear is the kind of holding where that could happen. EDGAR full-text search for "Burry" is the quickest check (see `log.md`).

## 6. What would have to happen for 13Fs to resume

Form 13F is filed by an "institutional investment manager". That means any person *other than a natural person* investing for its own account, or anyone exercising investment discretion over other people's accounts, once 13(f) securities under its discretion reach $100M at any month-end in a calendar year. Filing then runs for that year-end and the next three quarters. As a natural person trading his own account, Burry is outside the rule no matter how large his account is. **I am fairly confident in this reading of Section 13(f)(6)(A), but it is my reading, not legal advice.** 13Fs would resume only if one of these happens:
- he again manages money for others (a new or re-registered adviser or fund) above the threshold. Watch for a new Form ADV on IAPD, a new Form D for a fund, or a new 13F-HR under CIK 1649339 or a new CIK; or
- he moves his own money into an entity (LLC, trust or family-office vehicle) holding at least $100M of US-listed stock and options at a month-end.

If a 13F does appear, read it with everything above in mind. Treat options as notional, check the cover-page note (he uses it to say puts are hedges), expect most of the book to be outside the 13F, and expect the next filing to look completely different.

## 7. Filing mechanics that applied to Scion (for the historical CSVs)

- Values are in whole dollars, and every quarter reconciles to its cover page. `pct_of_portfolio` in the CSVs is row value ÷ reported total, which includes option notional, so it is meaningless in option-heavy quarters (Q2 2023, Q3 2023, Q1–Q3 2025). For stock weights, divide by the sum of the stock rows.
- The cover-page "additional information" note is Burry's only in-filing commentary. Read it: it says when puts may be hedges. Q2 and Q3 2025 had options but no note.
- "Other included managers" toggled between 0 and 2 (Scion Asset Partners LP, Scion Capital Group LLC) without any change in what was reported.
