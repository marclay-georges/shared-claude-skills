# Altimeter Capital Management (Brad Gerstner): investor profile and 13F reading guide

- Filer: Altimeter Capital Management, LP. CIK 0001541617. SEC file 028-14752 (13F); adviser file 801-73411, CRD 160017.
- People: Brad Gerstner, founder (2008), CEO, sole managing member of the general partner, and the only voice that matters for reading this book. Compliance signers on the 13F changed (Mara Davis through Q3 2024, Kate Karustis for Q4 2024 and Q1 2025, Annie Hancock from Q2 2025); that is administrative, not a change of portfolio manager.
- Period covered: 13F-HR for Q4 2023 through Q3 2026 (twelve quarters, all original filings; no amendments or NT filings in the window), plus the firm's 13G/13G-A, N-PX, Form D and Form ADV filings and Gerstner's public statements over the same span.
- Written 2026-11-19. Holdings CSVs are in `holdings/`; sources and filing quirks are in `log.md`.

Throughout, I keep three kinds of statement apart and label them: **Said** (Gerstner's own words, with a link), **Filed** (numbers from the filings), and **My read** (inference, with a confidence level). A reason with no source is marked as mine.

## 1. Who this is, and what the 13F is a slice of

Altimeter is two businesses under one adviser, and almost every mistake in reading its 13F comes from forgetting that. The March 2026 ADV brochure describes four strategy families: a long-focused public fund (the "Long Opportunities Fund"), a long/short public "Hedge Fund", multi-stage venture and growth "Flagship Funds", and single-company "Co-Investment Funds", plus one separately managed account that makes public investments ([ADV Part 2A, March 2026](https://files.adviserinfo.sec.gov/IAPD/Content/Common/crd_iapd_Brochure.aspx?BRCHR_VRSN_ID=1038994)). The ADV counts 33 private funds, 31 of them venture funds and 2 hedge funds, and $18.75B of regulatory AUM at 2025-12-31, up from $12.69B a year earlier ([Form ADV summary via SpecStream](https://specstream.ai/data/investment-advisers/firm/altimeter-capital-management-lp-160017); [Form ADV Part 1](https://reports.adviserinfo.sec.gov/reports/ADV/160017/PDF/160017.pdf)). The one SMA is a sovereign wealth fund or foreign official institution with $716M.

The 13F, at $5B to $10B, is therefore well under the firm's total, and it mixes two kinds of holding. Most rows are the public funds' trading positions. But because the 13F covers every 13(f) security the adviser has discretion over, a venture holding appears on it the day the company lists, and drops off or shrinks when the venture funds sell or distribute it. Cerebras is the clean proof: the 13G says its shares are "held of record by several private funds" ([13G, Aug 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1541617/000154161726000009/primary_doc.xml)), and they appear on the 13F only from Q2 2026, after the May 2026 IPO. Snowflake and Confluent, both pre-IPO Altimeter investments (Altimeter led Snowflake's 2015 Series C), are the earlier examples.

What the 13F cannot show, and Gerstner talks about constantly: shorts (the Hedge Fund runs them; **Said**, May 2024: exposure cut "by both adding shorts and reducing some of the overall position sizes", [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/05/07/altimeters-brad-gerstner-says-he-trimmed-positions-across-portfolio-after-2024s-strong-tech-run.html)); cash; private stakes such as OpenAI and Anthropic (**Said**, May 2026: "you are an investor in both", put by the host and not disputed, [Milken interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T3Stli61soU)); swaps and other OTC derivatives; non-US listings; and Gerstner's personal shares (the Snowflake 13G/A filed Feb 2024, as of 31 Dec 2023, counts about 3.1M Snowflake shares beyond the 12.4M on the 13F, "held directly"; the Sept 2026 Cerebras 13G/A shows 360,097 Cerebras shares with sole power, outside the 13F's 5.95M).

## 2. What Gerstner says the strategy is

Gerstner's stated method, repeated across the three years, has three parts.

**Own the companies at the centre of a "supercycle", founder-led where possible, and push them on efficiency.** The enduring statement is the October 2022 open letter to Meta, "Time to Get Fit" (pre-period, but it defines the posture): "we are long-term shareholders", "I am a strong supporter of founder-led companies", "we believe that augmented and artificial intelligence has the potential to drive more economic productivity than the internet itself", and a demand for 20% lower headcount cost and capped metaverse spending ([Medium, 2022-10-24](https://medium.com/@alt.cap/time-to-get-fit-an-open-letter-from-altimeter-to-mark-zuckerberg-and-the-meta-board-of-392d94e80a18)). It ended "We don't have any demands": public pressure without a 13D. Meta has been on every 13F in the window. In Nov 2025: "My experience living through three supercycles — internet, social media and cloud — they all ended up far bigger than we thought" ([CNBC, 2025-11-07](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/07/altimeters-brad-gerstner-says-market-pullback-is-healthy-sees-no-bubble-in-tech.html)).

**Manage net exposure actively and talk about it openly.** Gerstner reports exposure in round terms: net long about 95% at the start of 2023 and about 80% at the start of 2024, lower at the start of 2025 (BG2 podcast, Jan 2025, [episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yPD1qEbeyac), figures from a [summary of it](https://finance.biggo.com/podcast/4cbbdaa4dbd491d5)); "bomb shelter" in early April 2025; "medium", then "medium-small", then "the lowest they've been in three years" in Sept and Oct 2026. The 2025 BG2 framing names the macro trigger he watches: if the 10-year goes to 5.25%-5.5% "it becomes an anchor on the stock market". In Sept 2026 the 10-year crossed 5% and he cut, citing exactly that ([CNBC, 2026-09-24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8dND0Pj7PA0)).

**Separate the trading book from the long-term view, and say so.** The clearest statement, Oct 2026: "I live in two simultaneous but different worlds. One world is I manage money for people in a hedge fund. And so my job is to make sure I avoid some of the drawdown in my long-only fund and in my ... growth funds and venture funds. We're investing for the next 5 to 10 years ... when I look at '27 and '28, I'm even more positive" ([CNBC transcript, 2026-10-15](https://ceointerviews.ai/interview/3067810/)). For a reader this is the key to the gap between a bullish interview and a bearish-looking filing: both can be true on the same day.

He also frames trades as provisional. When selling Uber in Nov 2024: "I reserve the right. I may be buying this stock tomorrow or in a month or in two months" ([quoted in Matterfact, 2026-09-23](https://www.matterfact.com/newsletter/2026-09-23-track-record-gerstner-tesla-uber-robotaxi)).

Three structural facts sit under those numbers.

**A small, stable core and a large, fast periphery.** Seven positions were on all twelve filings: Nvidia, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Uber, Snowflake, and the Grab warrants (a leftover from Altimeter's own SPAC, see section 5). Nvidia, Meta, Microsoft and Amazon are the real core: together 34% of the book at Q4 2023, rising steadily to 64% at Q1 2026, then 38% and 46% once the 2026 IPOs and the chip basket arrived. Around them, 56 distinct positions appeared in twelve quarters (counting each put as its own position), 23 of them in only one filing. A typical quarter has two to nine new names and two to eight exits. The periphery is where the noise is.

**The core rotated from software to semiconductors.** At Q4 2023 Snowflake was 41% of the book, a venture-era legacy. By Q1 2026 Nvidia was 28.6%. In between, Snowflake was sold down from 12.4M shares to 1.93M (held flat at 1,926,250 for four quarters since). Nvidia went from 6.6% to 22% to 29%, and the chip complex (Nvidia, TSMC, Arm, Broadcom, Micron, Astera, Cerebras, the equipment names) reached about 60% at Q2 2026 by the Filings Report's count ([Filings Report, 2026-11-16](https://thefilingsreport.substack.com/p/the-ai-bull-sold-the-equipment-makers)). **Said** (May 2026): "We own compute. We own logic. We own memory. The substrate of AI." ([Milken](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T3Stli61soU)).

**The reported total swings with exposure, not with fund flows.** The 13F total fell from $6.75B (Q3 2024) to $4.97B (Q1 2025), when Gerstner described being in a "bomb shelter", and nearly doubled to $9.83B by Q2 2026 after what he called "our best month in the history of Altimeter" (April 2026). The hedge fund's Form D shows cumulative subscriptions of $3.463B (Apr 2023), $3.470B (Apr 2024), $3.544B (Apr 2025) and $3.704B (Apr 2026): gross inflows of a few tens to $160M a year, small next to the swings. **My read** (fairly confident): moves in the total are mostly market moves, exposure decisions and IPOs of venture holdings landing on the 13F, not new money. The ADV's jump in RAUM to $18.75B is mostly private-fund marks and new venture vehicles, which reach the 13F only when a company lists. The Long Opportunities Fund's Form D was not found, so its flows are unknown.

## 4. The three years in order: what was said, then what was filed

**Late 2023 to mid 2024: concentrated legacy book, adding AI hardware, trimming into strength.** The Q4 2023 13F is 11 names, five of them 86% of the value. Q1 2024 adds the first AI-infrastructure satellites (Astera Labs at its IPO, Broadcom, Coherent, Dell) plus Alphabet and Coupang, while Snowflake and Confluent are trimmed. On 7 May 2024 Gerstner said he had cut exposure by 10 to 20 points in the hedge fund and the long-only fund, citing a year's return in a few months, fewer expected rate cuts and the 2017 tax cuts expiring, while wanting to stay in "names like Nvidia that are reaccelerating" and "Amazon and Google and Microsoft and Snowflake" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/05/07/altimeters-brad-gerstner-says-he-trimmed-positions-across-portfolio-after-2024s-strong-tech-run.html)). The Q2 2024 filing matches that only loosely: the long book grew, Tesla was sold out, and Apple, Amphenol, Salesforce, Samsara, and IPO allotments in Rubrik and Webtoon were added. The exposure cut came through shorts the 13F does not show. Nvidia's jump from 0.79M to 7.74M shares in Q2 2024 is its 10-for-1 split (a slight trim, in fact).

**Snowflake: the sell-down nobody explained.** Snowflake went from 41% to 5% of the book over nine quarters, while Gerstner still named it among the "reaccelerating" companies in May 2024. I found no statement in which he gives the reason for selling. **My read** (medium-low confidence): part of the decline is likely the venture funds selling or distributing in kind long-held pre-IPO stock, which looks like a sale on the 13F but is a fund-lifecycle event, not a change of view. The rest (Q4 2024: down 42%; Q1 2025: down 41%) coincides with the firm-wide de-risking described below. What would change my mind: a Gerstner statement that the Snowflake thesis broke, or 13G/A evidence of distributions.

**Q3 2024: the China trade and the return to Tesla.** New in the quarter: KWEB (China internet ETF, $193M), Futu, a re-bought Tesla stake, TSMC, Broadcom, Zillow, Duolingo, and an IPO allotment in Lineage. KWEB and Futu were gone by year-end. I found no Gerstner statement on the China trade. **My read** (medium): a tactical bet on China's late-September 2024 stimulus, held under a quarter. Its return in Q1 2025 (Alibaba) and Q3 2025 (Alibaba, PDD), each sold within one or two quarters, fits a recurring tactical China sleeve, not a long-term view.

**Nov 2024: Uber to Tesla, the clearest example of words and filing diverging.** On 21 Nov 2024 Gerstner told CNBC: "Leading up to the election… we have been taking down our position size and we've been rotating into Tesla … After the election, we bought more Tesla and we rotated out of our remaining Uber" ([Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-tsla-acts-as-replacement-for-uber-in-legendary-investors-portfolio/); [CNBC video](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2024/11/21/trade-tracker-brad-gerstner-sells-uber.html)). The year-end 13F shows Uber cut 58%, from 13.3M to 5.59M shares, not to zero, and Tesla also cut, from 662k to 355k. By Q1 2025 the 13F shows no Tesla at all, yet on 3 April 2025 CNBC billed a segment as Gerstner explaining "why he continues to own Tesla" ([CNBC video](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2025/04/03/brad-gerstner-explains-his-tesla-position.html)). **My read** (confident about the gap, not its cause): his public descriptions are of firm-wide economic exposure at the moment of speaking, across funds and instruments the 13F omits (swaps, options, intra-quarter trades). The quarter-end 13F can show the opposite of what he said six weeks earlier. Uber was rebuilt to 8.8M shares by Q3 2026, and in Sept 2026 he cited Uber approvingly as a business.

**Q4 2024 to Q1 2025: de-risking into the "bomb shelter".** The 13F total fell 26% in two quarters. Q4 2024 cut Uber, Snowflake (down 42%), Microsoft (down 37%) and Meta (down 19%). Q1 2025 sold out of Alphabet, Tesla, TSMC, Salesforce, Samsara, Twilio, Micron and Duolingo and added a **QQQ put on 988,000 shares, $463M notional**. In January 2025 Gerstner had said net exposure was below the prior years' 80%-95% because valuations were higher, and named the 10-year yield as the swing factor. On 3 April 2025, the day after the "Liberation Day" tariffs: "We had puts on the NASDAQ, we had a tremendous number of shorts, and we were sitting in a lot of cash. We only had 50% of our long book even positioned in the market ... we're going from the bomb shelter to simply our safety positioning ... adding about 15% of net exposure", with Nvidia the named add ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/04/03/nvidia-positioned-to-weather-trump-tariffs-says-altimeters-gerstner.html); fuller quote at [Finviz/Insider Monkey](https://finviz.com/news/38283/brad-gerstner-says-hes-buying-nvidia-nvda-amid-off-the-charts-gpu-demand-secular-growth)). The QQQ put on the 13F is the visible tip of that hedge. By Q2 2025 it was gone and the total was back to $6.93B, with Amazon, Arm, TSMC, Coupang, Robinhood and Nvidia rebuilt.

**H2 2025: "wall of worry", fully invested in Nvidia, trimming the rest.** Q3 2025 shows another burst of small new names: Broadcom, Alibaba, PDD, Synopsys, Alphabet, and IPO allotments in Gemini (1.6M), Pattern and Netskope. Most were gone by Q4 2025. On 7 Nov 2025 Gerstner said the Nvidia position "going into earnings was as large as it had ever been" but "We rolled back our overall exposures at the end of last week because we felt like the market was pretty pumped up" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/07/altimeters-brad-gerstner-says-market-pullback-is-healthy-sees-no-bubble-in-tech.html)). In Oct 2025 on BG2 Gerstner called out "circular revenue" red flags "further out the risk curve ... startup neo clouds" while saying "I'm not concerned as a shareholder in Nvidia" ([BG2, Oct 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KX6q6lvoYtM)). Yet CoreWeave, a neocloud, was sold out in Q3 2025 and re-bought in Q4 2025 (3.2M shares), and was 6.1M shares by Q2 2026: he holds positions he publicly worries about.

**Q1 2026: the narrowest book.** 13 rows and $5.70B, Nvidia 28.6%. Confluent's exit is not a decision: IBM agreed on 7 Dec 2025 to buy it for $31 cash and closed on 17 March 2026 ([IBM](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-03-17-ibm-completes-acquisition-of-confluent,-making-real-time-data-the-engine-of-enterprise-ai-and-agents)). The Q4 2025 cut from 11.3M to 6.9M shares came mostly after the deal was announced, which is deal-spread selling, not a change of view. Coupang, Zillow, MercadoLibre, Shopify and Bloom were also sold out. Gerstner later described March 2026 as a bottom: "a tough month the month before, but we stayed the course."

**Q2 2026: the semiconductor peak and two IPOs.** **Said** (Milken, 4 May 2026): April was "our best month in the history of Altimeter"; "We own compute ... logic ... memory"; "I think Nvidia will be the first $10 trillion company"; Nvidia is "one of our largest holdings" ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T3Stli61soU); [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/05/52311247/nvidia-10-trillion-valuation-altimeter-brad-gerstner-forecast-market-concerns)). On 14 May, at the Cerebras IPO: "we invest in Cerebras 8 to 9 years ago", he sat on the board, and he warned retail buyers against "Yoloing into this thing at 400 bucks" ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q8tjCXs3xvA)). **Filed:** Cerebras arrives at $1.60B (16.2%); SpaceX (IPO 12 June 2026 at $135) at 1.78M shares, $304M; plus Qualcomm, Micron, Applied Materials, KLA, Lam, Synopsys and Alphabet. 32% of the quarter's value is new. Strip out Cerebras, which arrived by listing rather than by purchase, and the deliberate new buying was the equipment-and-memory basket.

**Q3 2026: cutting the cyclical part of the chip trade, hedging the rest, adding Tesla.** **Said:** at the All-In Summit in mid-September Gerstner said exposure was "medium-small". On 24 Sept, with the 10-year above 5%: "we've taken our exposure ... to a medium level ... when I saw the 10-year go above five ... that becomes a really big headwind" ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8dND0Pj7PA0)). On 15 Oct: "our exposures are the lowest they've been in three years", because after "the biggest move in semis in history" in April-May the AI stocks "got ahead of themselves" ([transcript](https://ceointerviews.ai/interview/3067810/)). **Filed:** every equipment name and Qualcomm sold, all of them bought only the quarter before; Cerebras down 18% (13G/A: 7.16M on 14 Aug to 5.95M on 30 Sept); CoreWeave down 21%; SpaceX down 41%; a new **SMH put on 840,000 shares, $512M notional**; and adds to Nvidia (to 10.0M shares, a fourth straight increase), Meta (up 25%, after he praised Meta's new "Muse" consumer AI product on 24 Sept) and Uber, plus a new Tesla stake of $680M. **My read** (medium-high): this is the filing version of "medium-small exposure". The long book was kept close to $9.8B, but its cyclical, order-book-dependent part was sold and the sector was hedged with the put. Shorts and cash, which carry most of an exposure cut for this manager, are invisible. Tesla's return coincides with Gerstner's October talk of a possible SpaceX-Tesla combination ([CNBC, 2026-10-21](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/10/21/brad-gerstner-cheaper-capital-may-be-key-to-spacex-tesla-merger.html)). I found no statement from him that gives the reason for the Tesla buy, so treat any causal link as unproven.

**After the period (context for the next filing).** Nvidia announced on 12 Nov 2026 that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, CEO and director (the board accepted it on 11 Nov). CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and Harvey Jones became board chair ([Nvidia 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000082/nvda-20261111.htm); [Nvidia press release](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition)). On Friday 13 Nov, Gerstner and Atreides' Gavin Baker posted a joint letter to Nvidia's board. It asked for "a slow, measured, and deliberate process" on future leadership that leaves all options open, "including the potential return of Jensen Huang", and said Kress would lead "capably" in the meantime ([CNBC, 2026-11-14](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/14/nvidia-investors-jensen-huang-return-board-letter.html)). On 16 Nov he said the letter was not a call for Huang's immediate reinstatement ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/16/2026/altimeters-gerstner-urges-nvidias-board-to-reconsider-huangs-ouster-to-get-this-right)). The same day a source told CNBC the board was not considering Huang's return ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang.html)). On 16 Nov he said Altimeter was "putting in for $5 billion" in Anthropic's IPO, "more than 5X the largest order we have ever placed in an IPO ... a cornerstone compounder", and that Altimeter wants "to continue to be a major shareholder of Anthropic" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/altimeters-brad-gerstner-investing-5-billion-in-anthropic-ipo.html); [excerpts](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/cnbc-exclusive-excerpts-altimeter-capital-founder-ceo-brad-gerstner-speaks-with-cnbcs-scott-wapner-on-halftime-report-today.html)).

## 5. How to read Altimeter's next 13F

**Start with the total and the options, then the core, then everything else.** For this manager, the single most informative fact in a filing is usually the overall exposure posture, and the 13F shows it only partly. Compare the filing against what Gerstner said about exposure during the quarter (he almost always says something on CNBC, BG2 or All-In). A falling total with a new index or sector put means de-risking. A rising total with no new option rows and no IPO arrivals means re-risking. Remember that most of a cut is shorts and cash you cannot see.

**Option rows are hedges until proven otherwise, and their value is notional.** In the window there were exactly two: the QQQ put (Q1 2025, $463M notional, 9.3%) and the SMH put (Q3 2026, $512M, 5.0%). Both were index or sector puts, both coincided with Gerstner publicly describing reduced exposure, and the first was gone a quarter later. Neither is a short view on a company. The 13F gives no strike, expiry or premium, so the dollar figure is the size of the reference, not the money at risk. Exclude option rows when computing long-book weights. Watch for a quirk: the QQQ row was filed with "PUT" in the title-of-class field and **no** put/call element, so several aggregators recorded it as a long QQQ holding. One even wrote that the SMH put was Altimeter's first option since 2019 ([Filings Report](https://thefilingsreport.substack.com/p/the-ai-bull-sold-the-equipment-makers)). Read the class field as well as the put/call field. A single-stock put or a call would be new behaviour and worth flagging.

**Meaningful signals, roughly in order of weight:**
1. Changes in the Nvidia / Meta / Microsoft / Amazon core. These are the views he defends on air. Nvidia's share count rose four quarters running to Q3 2026. A real cut there, beyond a few percent, would be news, especially now with Huang gone (see below). Meta has swung (1.95M → 1.35M → 1.70M shares in 2026) in line with his public view of its AI product, so Meta changes are view changes.
2. A position that grows over two or more quarters after its first appearance (Coupang 2024-25, CoreWeave 2025-26, Arm, TSMC, Robinhood in 2025). Those were conviction builds. A single-quarter appearance is not.
3. Whole-category exits, such as the equipment basket in Q3 2026, the China names repeatedly, or the software satellites in Q1 2025. These track a stated thematic call more reliably than any single-name change.

**Routine, low-signal changes:**
- Round-lot stakes in companies that just listed (Astera 400,000; Rubrik 175,000; Webtoon 120,000; Lineage 400,000; ServiceTitan 40,000; SailPoint 250,000; Netskope 325,000; Gemini 1,600,000). These are IPO allocations. Most were held one or two quarters and sold. Treat them as access, not conviction, unless the next filing shows them growing (Astera and Rubrik did, for a while).
- Small satellite names that come and go within a quarter or two (Duolingo, Twilio, Datadog, Axon, Bloom, Shopify, Sandisk). In twelve quarters, 23 positions appeared exactly once. Exits of these are not changes of mind about the companies.
- Share-count jumps from splits (Nvidia, Q2 2024) and moves in the total that are price only. Check the share column before calling a buy.
- Corporate actions: Confluent's disappearance was IBM's cash takeout.

**Where the 13F misleads for Altimeter in particular:**
- *Venture holdings that IPO.* A newly listed company Altimeter backed privately (Cerebras 2026; SpaceX 2026; and probably Anthropic next) arrives as a large "new position" that was not bought that quarter. Later "sales" of it can be venture-fund sales or in-kind distributions to LPs at lock-up expiry, not a change of view. The Cerebras lock-up ran to 9 Nov 2026 per insiders' Form 4s, so Q4 2026 is when distributions or sales could show. Check the 13G/13G-A (filed in Altimeter's own CIK folder) for counts at dates between 13Fs: the Cerebras 13G gives holdings at 31 May, 30 June and 14 Aug 2026.
- *Anthropic in the Q4 2026 filing (due mid-Feb 2027).* If the IPO happened as described (around 19 Nov 2026), expect Anthropic to appear, possibly as the largest or second-largest position, combining whatever IPO allocation Altimeter got on a $5B order with any existing private stake that became listed stock. That one row could move every other weight by several points. It will be a mix of a stated, high-conviction buy and a venture mark becoming visible, and should not be read as a rotation out of everything else.
- *Words versus filing.* Gerstner's interviews describe firm-wide, real-time exposure. The 13F is a quarter-end snapshot of long 13(f) positions only. Uber (said fully exited Nov 2024; 5.59M shares at year-end) and Tesla (said still owned Apr 2025; zero at 31 Mar 2025) are the documented gaps.
