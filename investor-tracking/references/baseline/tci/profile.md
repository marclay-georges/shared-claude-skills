# TCI Fund Management: investor profile and 13F reading guide

- **Manager:** TCI Fund Management Limited, London (The Children's Investment Fund). 13F filer CIK 0001647251, 13F file number 028-16990.
- **People:** Sir Christopher (Chris) Hohn, founder (2003) and sole portfolio manager; Managing Director of the filer. Angus Milne, Head of Compliance, signs the 13Fs. No change of portfolio manager in the period.
- **Period covered:** 13F-HRs for Q4 2023 through Q3 2026 (twelve quarters, all originals, no amendments), plus TCI's 13D/13G, N-PX, Form ADV and related filings, and Hohn's public words from 2021 to November 2026.
- **Written:** 2026-11-19, as a draft baseline. Numbers are in `holdings/` and every source is in `log.md`.

Throughout, I mark three kinds of statement. **Said** means Hohn or TCI said it, with a link. **Filings** means the numbers. **Inference** means my reading, with a confidence level.

## Who this is

TCI is one person's very concentrated, very long-term equity fund. It is now about $77bn of assets (Forbes and LCH, end of 2025), and it posted the largest one-year dollar gain any hedge fund has ever reported: $18.9bn in 2025, a 27.8% net return. Over the three years in this baseline it returned +33% (2023), +15% (2024), +27.8% (2025) and about -2% for 2026 to the end of October, after a -9.4% first quarter ([Bloomberg 2024-01-05](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-01-05/chris-hohn-s-hedge-fund-bounces-back-with-33-gain-in-2023), [Institutional Investor 2025-01-30](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2ecrz2s6w3g2k4h9soo3k/hedge-funds/how-chris-hohns-concentrated-portfolio-fared-last-year), [II 2026-01-19](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/chris-hohns-tci-tops-hedge-fund-gains-2025), [II 2026-11-09](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/after-record-2025-chris-hohn-still-digging-out-slump)). A large share of the firm's profits goes to Hohn's foundation, CIFF, and the foundation's own capital is managed by TCI alongside the fund. That matters for the 13F, as explained below.

The firm's reputation is activist: Deutsche Börse and ABN Amro in the 2000s, CSX in 2008, Safran/Zodiac in 2017, Canadian National in 2021. By 2025 Hohn describes activism as the exception. The business is now mostly long-only ownership of what he calls monopolies. **Said** ([Norges Bank "In Good Company", May 2025](https://exa.ai/library/podcast/fdv4tvl897t/episode/wt08ppqg9ws)): "The business always wins… it's pointless being an activist in a B business… What people call activism is really an exception to us." He says TCI still steps in when something goes wrong at a company it already owns, and compares it to fighting off someone who "attack[s] you to try to take your wallet."

His philosophy is consistent and stated plainly:

- **Hate competition, fear disruption.** "I hate competition," because it destroys profits and makes "predictability and valuation impossible." The test he applies is whether the business will still be strong "in 20 or 30 years" ([Money Maze Allocator Summit, Nov 2025, via Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/news/chris-hohn-good-companies-stay-good/)). He looks for "such high barriers to entry that we can have a reasonable idea that they'll be around… in 20 or 30 years" (same talk, quoted by [Alpha-File](https://alphafile.substack.com/p/tci-a-73-billion-portfolio-of-monopolies)).
- **Hold for years.** TCI's average holding period is "about eight years," with some names held 10 to 13 years. "Good companies stay good and bad companies stay bad." Asked what's new, he answers: "do you need to change your wife every year?" (Money Maze 2025).
- **Size big.** Quoting Soros: "If I'm right on a 1% position, it kind of doesn't make any difference. So we'll take positions of 10 to 15%, in the past even 25% of the fund" (Norges Bank conference 2025, via Alpha-File).
- **Risk means not knowing what you own.** He takes Soros's definition of risk as "not knowing what you're doing." "If it's not obvious you probably just leave it alone" (Money Maze 2025).
- **Rarely short.** "We were rarely short" (Tangen 2025). The 13F's lack of shorts hides almost nothing here.
- **Climate is the one ESG issue he pushes hard.** TCI's ESG policy (March 2023, [tcifund.com/ESG](https://www.tcifund.com/ESG)) requires portfolio companies to disclose emissions, report to CDP and publish a credible transition plan. It commits TCI to vote against directors who fall short and to "evaluate divestment" where a company refuses. TCI originated "Say on Climate" resolutions and filed them at CN, CP, Moody's, S&P Global, Union Pacific, Charter, Alphabet and Aena ([engagement page](https://www.tcifund.com/ESGEngagements)). "I never really got involved in the S and the G, just the E" (Tangen 2025).

What he says he owns, in sector terms. **Said** (Q1 2025 letter, via [Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/investor-letters/sir-christopher-hohn-tci-2025/)): the master fund was $61bn at 2025-03-31. Gross exposure was 38% aerospace, 22% infrastructure, 18% information services, 15% technology, 10% railroads, 9% payments and 1% real estate debt. That sums to 113%, so the fund runs modest gross leverage. Top positions, in order: GE Aerospace, Safran, Airbus, Microsoft, Moody's, Vinci, Visa, S&P Global, Ferrovial, CPKC, Alphabet, CN, Cellnex, Aena.

## How much of TCI the 13F shows (roughly 60%)

This is the most important caveat for this manager. The 13F shows only US-listed long stock held directly. TCI's second-, third- and fourth-largest economic positions are not in it at all, and one 13F position is shown at about a quarter of its true size.

**Not in the 13F at all** (**filings** of European regulators, aggregated by [Alpha-File, July 2026](https://alphafile.substack.com/p/tci-a-73-billion-portfolio-of-monopolies), plus the letter list above):
- Safran: 7.1%, about $10.6bn at end-2025.
- Airbus: about 5%, 39.5M shares, about $9bn (AFM, March 2026).
- Aena: 6.26%. TCI wrote to Aena's board in September 2025 as holder of 6% ([letter](https://www.tcifund.com/files/corporateengageement/aena/Aena%20Letter%20to%20the%20Board%2025%20September%202025.pdf)).
- Cellnex: 10.15% (CNMV, November 2025).
- Vinci: a long-held top-ten position per the Q1 2025 letter, size not disclosed.
- Smaller stakes in Deutsche Börse and LSEG, per Alpha-File.

Alpha-File put the disclosed European stakes at about $27bn against a $45.2bn 13F at Q1 2026.

**Understated in the 13F: Ferrovial.** The 13F shows 20.94M shares, about $1.1bn and roughly 3% of the company. The Dutch AFM notification of October 2026 shows TCI at 13% of Ferrovial: 3.07% in shares plus 9.97% through cash-settled swaps (72.75M voting rights), worth about €4.15bn in total ([Forbes España 2026-10-20](https://forbes.es/ultima-hora/1049407/el-fondo-britanico-tci-eleva-su-participacion-en-ferrovial-al-13-y-se-afianza-como-segundo-mayor-accionista/)). The 13F therefore shows about a quarter of the economic position. The swap structure is long-standing: July 2023 showed 1.53% in shares and 8.50% in swaps. Ferrovial only appeared in the 13F from Q2 2024 because its shares began trading in the US that quarter. TCI did not buy it then.

**Estimate, with working.** At Q1 2025 the letter's sector mix lets you split the book. The 13F-visible parts are technology (MSFT, GOOGL), railroads (CN, CPKC), payments (Visa), US information services (Moody's, S&P), GE and Ferrovial's direct shares: about 69% of NAV. The non-13F parts are European aerospace (38% aerospace less GE's ~16%, so about 22%), most of infrastructure (about 20%) and real-estate debt (1%): about 43% of NAV. On that basis the 13F shows **roughly 60–65% of gross equity exposure**. Ratios of 13F value to reported AUM tell the same story:

| Date | 13F value | Reported AUM | 13F / AUM |
|---|---|---|---|
| End-2024 | $42.4bn | LCH end-2024 AUM $57.6bn | ~74% |
| Q1 2025 | $43.4bn | Master fund $61bn | ~71% |
| End-2025 | $53.6bn | Firm AUM $77bn | ~70% |

Those ratios flatter the 13F, for two reasons. The 13F also includes the foundation's vehicle (CIFF Capital UK LP), which is not in the master fund. And gross exposure exceeds NAV. **Inference, medium-high confidence:** treat the 13F as a bit under two-thirds of TCI. Treat sector conclusions drawn from it alone as wrong. The 13F makes TCI look like a US financials-and-industrials fund. Its biggest theme is aerospace: GE, Safran and Airbus together are close to half the fund by Alpha-File's estimate. Its second is European infrastructure, and the 13F barely shows either.

What this means in practice: when the 13F's total value or weights move, the cause may be a move in a book the reader cannot see. A rise in a US name's 13F weight does not mean it rose in TCI's real portfolio, because the denominator is wrong. When someone asks "what is Hohn's biggest bet," the 13F answer (GE Aerospace, about 30%) understates the aerospace concentration and misses Safran entirely.

The story in order, with what was said alongside what was done:

**Early 2024: the book as it stood.** The holdings were GE (built through 2023), the two Canadian railroads, Moody's, S&P, Visa, Microsoft and Alphabet, plus a 4.6% Thermo Fisher stake and a sliver of Ferguson. Thermo was halved in Q1 2024 and gone by Q2 2024; Ferguson went in Q1 2024. I found no stated reason for either. TCI was still a 5%+ holder of CN, under a 13D dating from its 2021 campaign, and of CPKC (5.96%, 13G) and Moody's (6.58%, 13G). It added GE in Q1 to Q2 2024 (41.7M to 48.2M shares). It sold the GE Vernova shares it received in the April 2024 spin-off within the same quarter, since no GEV row ever appears. It kept only the engine business.

**Mid-2024 to mid-2025: Microsoft up, Canadian National down.** TCI added to Microsoft from 10.5M to 17.6M shares (Q3 2024 to Q2 2025), making it about 15–17% of the 13F. Hohn listed Microsoft and Alphabet among his technology holdings. In May 2025 he called Alphabet "maybe our most risky investment… one of our smallest investments as a result… There's a risk of search fragmenting… We don't think so… But competition is increasing" (Tangen 2025, quoted by [Alpha-File](https://alphafile.substack.com/p/tci-a-73-billion-portfolio-of-monopolies)). He trimmed Alphabet from 23M shares (Q2 2024) to 7.6M (Q3 2025). That is consistent with sizing a position by risk, as he described.

Canadian National was sold steadily, from 40M shares to 9.4M by mid-2026. The CN 13D/A of November 2024 lists daily sales from September to November 2024. The April 2025 13D/A records TCI falling below 5% (4.2%, with the 5% line crossed on 2024-12-11) and shows it buying and selling in the same month ([13D/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1647251/000090266425002033/0000902664-25-002033.txt)). **I found no TCI statement of why it sold CN.** Alpha-File's reading is that TCI was "consolidating its rail bet into CPKC." **Inference, low-to-medium confidence:** this was a gradual loss of conviction in CN specifically, not in railroads. CPKC was trimmed only about 20% over the same period. But it may also have been a funding source for Visa and Microsoft purchases; I can't distinguish the two. The point for a reader is that this slow multi-year bleed is what a TCI exit from a former activist target looks like.

**Q2 to Q3 2025: Visa nearly doubled.** TCI added 2.4M Visa shares in Q2 2025, 9.0M in Q3 2025 (the largest single add in the window) and 2.7M more in Q1 2026. Its stake went from 16.8M to 30.5M shares at implied prices of roughly $340–355. Hohn's general view: Visa is "this huge, ever-growing network connecting every customer and every bank… as the network grows, it becomes just ever harder to replicate" (Money Maze 2025). **I found no letter excerpt explaining the timing.** Alpha-File's suggestion that stablecoin and regulation fears made it cheap is their inference, not TCI's.

**Late 2025: record year, then the Microsoft reversal.** TCI earned a record 27.8% in 2025, driven by GE, Safran and Airbus ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/hanktucker/2026/01/18/hedge-fund-billionaire-chris-hohns-tci-profited-by-a-record-189-billion-in-2025/)). Microsoft was still nudged up in Q4 2025 (+1.1%). Then in Q1 2026 TCI cut it 84% and finished the exit in Q2 2026. **Said** (Q1 2026 letter, via FT, quoted by [TheFly/TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/christopher-hohns-tci-slashes-stake-in-microsoft-ft-reports-thefly-news)): "We reduced our investment in Microsoft because the rapid progress in AI introduces uncertainty over Microsoft's competitive position in the future. We are primarily concerned about Microsoft's Office productivity software franchise, where AI could change established workflows and lead to the emergence of new productivity platforms, but we also see some risks in Azure." The letter said Microsoft went from about 10% of the fund to about 1% by end-March. In the same quarter Alphabet went from 3% to 5% of the fund ([Techzine](https://www.techzine.eu/news/applications/141193/tci-sells-off-billions-in-microsoft-stake-due-to-ai-concerns/)). The 13F shows Alphabet rebuilt to 12.4M shares (A+C) by Q3 2026. This is the cleanest example in the window of Hohn's stated rule in action. Disruption risk is the thing he says matters most, and when it appeared in a long-held name, he sold a 15% position within two quarters of adding to it.

**Q1 2026: drawdown, and buying the ratings agencies.** TCI fell 11.9% in March 2026 and 9.4% in the quarter after the US–Iran war began. Every large holding fell: GE -17% in March, Visa -14% in the quarter, Moody's -14.5%, S&P -18.5%, Safran -18% in March ([II 2026-04-14](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/after-record-year-tci-hits-speed-bump)). Into that, the 13F shows TCI adding S&P Global (+19%, to 14.0M shares), Moody's (+7.7%, to 14.3M, now 8.21% of the company per the [May 2026 13G/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1647251/000090266426002468/0000902664-26-002468.txt)), Visa (+10%) and Alphabet. **Inference, medium confidence:** this was the Microsoft proceeds being redeployed into existing franchises that had sold off, not a new theme.

**Q2 to Q3 2026: aggregates and a spin-off.** TCI opened Martin Marietta and Vulcan Materials in Q2 2026 together (about $1.5bn) and added to both in Q3 2026 (MLM +37%, VMC +13.5%). That brought the pair to $1.54bn, about 3% of the 13F and well under 2% of the fund. **No TCI statement found.** **Inference, medium confidence:** the pair fits the stated framework closely. Quarries are irreplaceable, local and hard to permit, much like the railways and airports Hohn favours, so they look like monopolies. Buying the two leaders as a pair matches how TCI holds both ratings agencies and, until recently, both Canadian railroads. Because both are still small and still being bought, watch whether they grow toward a normal TCI position size (5% or more of the fund). S&P Global spun off Mobility Global on 2026-07-01. TCI would have received about 14.1M shares and kept 0.62M, so it sold about 96% as soon as it could. The small Mobility Global row in Q3 2026 is a leftover, not a purchase. In October 2026, outside the 13F window, TCI raised Ferrovial to 13% (AFM).

## How to read TCI's next 13F

**Value changes are mostly price, so look at share counts.** GE went from 15% to 34% of the 13F with almost no change in shares after mid-2024. TCI lets winners run and does not rebalance them down; the trims are tiny (tens of thousands of shares). A falling 13F total (Q1 2026 -16%, Q3 2026 -6%) has been market moves, not redemptions. **Inference, high confidence.** I found no press report of redemptions in this window.

**What a sale means here.** TCI sells in three ways, and they mean different things:

1. **Small trims of a few percent** in GE, CPKC, Moody's or Visa are routine. Hohn has never explained them, and they don't change the thesis. Do not report them as signals.
2. **Multi-quarter bleeds.** CN went from 40M to 9M over two years, and Alphabet was halved in 2025. These are sizing by conviction or risk and can reverse. Alphabet was rebuilt within two quarters of its low, after Hohn had called it his riskiest holding. Read a bleed as reduced conviction, not a verdict, unless he says otherwise.
3. **Fast, large exits.** Microsoft went from 15% to zero in two quarters. Before this window, Alpha-File documents Charter Communications going from as much as 38% of the 13F at end-2016 to zero by mid-2022, after Hohn had called it a long-term holding in spring 2021. The Microsoft exit came one quarter after a small add, so recent buying does not protect a position. When a large, long-held TCI position drops sharply in one quarter, treat it as a probable thesis break, usually about competition or disruption. Look for the investor letter coverage in the FT or Bloomberg within weeks. The Microsoft letter was reported on 2026-05-08, a week before the 13F was filed. That makes a sharp sale the most informative event a TCI 13F can contain.

The brief mentions TCI exiting whole sectors at once. In this window the closest cases are Microsoft (a disruption thesis applied to one name, with the proceeds moved to Alphabet in the same sector) and the CN wind-down. I did not find a sourced all-at-once sector exit in 2023–2026. Charter in 2021–22 is the best-documented earlier example of a fast full exit from a core holding. **Low confidence** on any broader "sector exit" pattern beyond that.

**What a buy means here.** New names are rare: in three years only Martin Marietta and Vulcan (Ferrovial's 13F entry was a listing artifact and Mobility Global a spin-off). A genuinely new US name is significant. TCI usually starts at 1–3% of the 13F and builds over several quarters, as GE did in 2023 and Visa in 2025. Expect paired entries into duopolies or oligopolies. Large adds to existing names during sell-offs have been TCI's usual habit (Q1 2026).

**Spin-offs get sold.** GE Vernova (2024) and Mobility Global (2026) were both dropped at once. A small row in a spin-off, or its disappearance next quarter, is noise.

**Share-class and name noise.** Alphabet's A and C lines shift between classes: the A line vanished in Q3 2025 and returned in Q1 2026. Always combine them. GE Aerospace (formerly "General Electric Co") and Ferrovial NV (formerly "Ferrovial SE") changed names on the same CUSIPs. Aggregator sites have misreported these as exits and new buys.

**The non-US book can be the real story.** A quiet 13F can coincide with big moves in Safran, Airbus, Aena, Vinci, Cellnex or Ferrovial swaps. For TCI those are reported to European regulators (AMF, AFM, CNMV, the UK's FCA/RNS, BaFin) at thresholds such as 3%, 5% and 10%, not to the SEC. When a TCI 13F seems to show little, check press coverage of European holding notifications before concluding nothing happened.

**Activism shows up in 13Ds, not 13Fs.** In this window the only 13D was the residual CN one from 2021, which ended at 4.2% in April 2025. Moody's (8.21%) and CPKC (5.96% in 2024) are on passive 13Gs filed under Rule 13d-1(c). A new 13D from TCI on a US-listed name, or a 13G converting to a 13D, would mean an activist campaign. Item 4 would state the purpose, as the 2021 CN letters did. Climate engagement runs through shareholder resolutions and director votes, not through 13F changes. TCI's policy permits divestment over climate refusal, but **I found no case in this window where a 13F sale was attributed to climate.** Treat that explanation as unlikely unless TCI says so.

**What the 13F mechanics look like for this filer.**
- One filer, no other included managers, every row SOLE discretion and SH. No options or puts in any quarter. No confidential-treatment omissions seen, and no amendments.
- Filed on day 45 or the next business day: Feb 14–17, May 15, Aug 14, Nov 14–16.
- The positions combine The Children's Investment Master Fund and CIFF Capital UK LP (the foundation's vehicle), per the 13G filings. The 2024 N-PX shows two voting rows per stock that sum to the 13F share count.
- No Form 4s, because the large stakes are in foreign private issuers (CN, CPKC, Ferrovial) or below 10% (Moody's).
- The N-PX shows TCI voting FOR every say-on-pay resolution at every US holding in 2024, 2025 and 2026. TCI's disagreements with management are not expressed through pay votes.
- The filer is an SEC exempt reporting adviser (802-102468). Its Form ADV lists only the real-estate lending funds, and there is no Part 2A brochure, so ADV gives no useful equity AUM figure. The real-estate funds raise money through Form Ds and are separate from the equity book.

**The 45-day lag matters less here than for most managers** because turnover is so low. The exception is a thesis-break sale, which the letter usually reveals first.

## Where my understanding is thin

- No primary investor letter was available. Every letter quote here is second-hand (FT via TheFly, Hedge Fund Alpha summaries). Hedge Fund Alpha's Q1 2025 sector breakdown is the backbone of the 60–65% coverage estimate. If a newer letter gives a different sector mix, redo that estimate.
- No stated reasons for the CN sale, the Visa near-doubling, the Thermo Fisher exit or the aggregates entry. If a letter or interview explains any of these, it should replace my inferences.
- European stake sizes come from one aggregator (Alpha-File) plus press reports of AFM and CNMV notices. They are threshold disclosures, so the true positions can sit anywhere between thresholds.
- What would change my reading of TCI overall: a sale of GE Aerospace or Visa beyond small trims, which would be the first move in a core aerospace or payments thesis; a new 13D; or press reports of redemptions or capital being returned, which would mean flows start to matter. None has happened through Q3 2026.
