## The short version

Berkshire's 13F covers only one slice of a conglomerate. That slice has been run by three, then two, sets of hands, and it went through a change of control during this period. Most of the reading problem comes from those two facts.

Three things matter most for the next filing.

First, the 13F is not Berkshire's equity portfolio. It omits:
- the five Japanese trading houses, worth $35.4B at the end of 2025 and roughly $50B or more by September 2026 (inferred from the gap between the 13F total and the equity book);
- $350B+ of cash and T-bills;
- every wholly owned business.

What Berkshire does with its cash shows up first in the 10-Q cash-flow lines and in 8-Ks and press releases, not in the 13F.

Second, size tells you whose decision a change was, and since Q1 2026 the rules for that have changed. Under Buffett, a position under about $1–2B was usually Combs's or Weschler's. Combs's whole book was sold in Q1 2026. What's left is:
- a core Abel calls "very manageable";
- a handful of large positions Abel and Buffett discuss together;
- Weschler's roughly $20B.

New small positions in 2026, such as Macy's, NYT, Bending Spoons, and arguably Delta, can't be confidently assigned to anyone. Berkshire doesn't say.

Third, the change that would mean the most is any share-count change in the four core US holdings (Apple, American Express, Coca-Cola, Moody's). Abel's first letter said there would be "limited activity in these holdings" unless something "fundamental" changes. Apple was cut in seven of the nine quarters through Q4 2025, then held flat for three. A renewed cut there, or any sale of AmEx or Coke, would break a stated policy. Steady trims of Bank of America, by contrast, are a two-year pattern and no longer news on their own.

## Who this investor is, and how that changed across the three years

### Buffett era, through 2025: the stated philosophy

**Said:**
- The 2023 letter (2024-02-24, https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2023ltr.pdf) states the goal plainly: to "own either all or a portion of businesses that enjoy good economics that are fundamental and enduring."
- The same letter warns that size has removed any chance of "eye-popping performance" because "there remain only a handful of companies in this country capable of truly moving the needle."
- It says Berkshire did not buy or sell a share of Coca-Cola or American Express in 2023 and would "most certainly leave our holdings untouched," because "when you find a truly wonderful business, stick with it."
- It says Occidental, at 27.8%, would be held "indefinitely," with "no interest in purchasing or managing" it.
- It says the Japanese houses are "passive and long-term."

**Said:** The 2024 letter (2025-02-22, https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2024ltr.pdf) is the key text for reading the turnover that followed:
- Berkshire's equity activity is "ambidextrous" between whole businesses and marketable stocks.
- "We can't come and go on a dime. Sometimes a year or more is required to establish or divest an investment."
- "Berkshire will never prefer ownership of cash-equivalent assets over the ownership of good businesses."
- Marketable equities fell from $354B to $272B during 2024, and Buffett did not explain the selling.
- It says the trading houses agreed to "moderately relax" the 10% ceiling, and that ownership will "likely… increase somewhat."

**Said:** At the 2024 meeting (2024-05-04, transcript linked in log), Buffett gave the reason for the first Apple sale:
- Part of it was tax: "we are paying a 21% federal rate on the gains," and higher rates were likely.
- He said Apple would very likely remain the largest holding.
- He added: "I don't mind at all, under current conditions, building the cash position."
- On Paramount, sold at a loss: "It was 100 percent my decision."
- Responsibility for capital allocation after him "should lie with Greg Abel."

**Said:** At the 2025 meeting (2025-05-03), Buffett told shareholders Abel would become CEO at year-end with "the final word… in operations, in capital deployment." He said the Japan stakes would not be sold "in the next 50 years," and Abel echoed "50 years or forever."

**Said:** The Thanksgiving letter (2025-11-10, https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/nov1025.pdf) said Buffett was "going quiet. Sort of." It said "ideas are few – but not zero."

### The transition, December 2025: one of the two lieutenants leaves

**Said:** Berkshire's release of 2025-12-08 (https://berkshirehathaway.com/news/dec0825.pdf) announced that Todd Combs, GEICO's CEO, had "resigned to accept an interesting and important job at JPMorgan." He went to run a $10B strategic investment group.

Morningstar (secondary, https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/berkshire-hathaway-combs-departure-leaves-investment-portfolio-duties-hands-abel-others) put Combs's oversight at "more than $30 billion" of the portfolio.

### Abel era, 2026: concentrated core, active edges, and spending

**Said:** Abel's first letter (2025 letter, released 2026-02-28, https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf) is now the governing statement. It sets out three tiers.

1. The core is Apple, AmEx, Coca-Cola and Moody's: $158.6B at year-end 2025. "This concentrated approach will continue, with limited activity in these holdings, though we may significantly adjust a holding if we see fundamental changes in its long term economic prospects."
2. The Japanese houses, "comparable to our major U.S. holdings in importance": $35.4B at a $15.4B cost, financed with yen debt at 1.2%. Together with the core this makes $194B, "nearly two-thirds" of a $297.8B equity book.
3. "Meaningful positions in a small number of other companies where our capital allocation has been more dynamic in recent periods, as relative values and opportunities change," some of which "may become part of our core holdings."

On who decides, the letter says: "responsibility ultimately resides with me as CEO. Ted Weschler manages about 6% of our investments, including a portion of the portfolio formerly overseen by Todd Combs."

The letter also lists "Act quickly and concentrate our capital in a few high conviction ideas" among Berkshire's criteria. It says the cash pile does not signal "a retreat from investing."

**Said:** At the 2026 meeting (2026-05-02, transcript https://www.good-investing.net/berkshire-hathaway-annual-meeting/transcripts/2026-transcript/), Abel described the same structure in numbers:
- the core plus Japan was "just under 200 billion";
- the next group ("a BofA, a Chevron, a Google, companies like that") was "another $70 billion," where "we may still be acquiring shares or rationalizing what's the right position";
- "Ted manages… just under 20 billion";
- he and Buffett are "absolutely collaborating" on the large holdings.

He also said Berkshire would "act decisively and with significant capital" when the opportunity is right.

**Filings:** The Q3 2026 10-Q (https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/3rdqtr26.pdf) now says buybacks happen when the CEO, "after consultation with the Chairman," judges the price below intrinsic value.

**Filings (proxies and 8-Ks):** The formal documents add little on who runs the portfolio. None of the 2024–2026 proxy statements mentions Weschler or Combs.
- They show Buffett's voting interest at 31.0%, 30.3% and 30.0%, and his economic interest at 15.0%, 14.3% and 13.7%. A standing voting agreement caps his votes at 49.9%. He "may be deemed" the controlling shareholder.
- The 2024 and 2025 proxies said no Buffett family member would be involved in managing Berkshire after him, though the family would help pick and oversee the CEO. The 2026 proxy keeps only the plan for a Buffett-family non-executive chairman after his death, left to the board.
- The 8-Ks record the board's unanimous vote of 2025-05-04 making Abel CEO from 2026-01-01, Charles Chang as CFO from 2026-06-01, and a 2026-05-03 by-law rewrite "to update the officer roles… to conform to Berkshire's current operating structure."

**Inference (high confidence):** Buffett has stepped back from operations, not from investing. The large holdings are a joint call between Abel and Buffett. Buffett also still signs the Section 16 filings as controlling holder, so "Buffett sold X" headlines will keep appearing and may be wrong about who decided.

### The core

**Filings:** Over the twelve quarters, these share counts did not change at all:
- American Express: 151.6M
- Coca-Cola: 400M
- Moody's: 24.67M
- Kraft Heinz: 325.6M

Occidental rose from 243.7M to 264.9M by Q1 2025, then stopped.

These are the true "never sell" names. A change in any of them is the strongest signal this filer can send.

### Apple

**Filings:** Apple fell in steps: 905.6M, 789.4M, 400M, then 300M (flat Q3 2024 to Q1 2025), then 280M, 238.2M and 227.9M at Q4 2025. It has been flat for three quarters since.

**Said:** The first cut was explained as tax and portfolio-weighting (2024 AGM), not as a change of view.

**Inference (medium-high):** The later, smaller cuts in 2025 were rebalancing, not an exit. Abel's naming Apple as core in 2026 and holding it flat supports that. A cut in Q4 2026 or later, under the new policy, would mean more than any of the 2025 trims.

### Bank of America and the other financials

**Filings:** Bank of America has fallen every quarter since Q3 2024, from 1,032.9M shares to 455.9M, and is now about 8% of the 13F.

Berkshire has also been trimming Capital One (12.5M down to 1.15M), Ally, Citi (gone in Q1 2025), Nu (gone in Q1 2025) and the Visa/Mastercard pair (gone in Q1 2026).

**Inference (medium):** This is a deliberate move out of financials other than AmEx and Chubb. No reason has been given. Morningstar's Q3 2026 headline calls it "swapping Bank of America stock for Lennar." That is a commentator's framing, not Berkshire's.

### 2024: the great selling

**Filings:** Berkshire sold about $143B of stock and bought about $9B. Cash and T-bills went from $189B at the end of Q1 2024 to $334B at year-end. Exits included:
- HP (Q1)
- Paramount and Snowflake (Q2)
- Floor & Decor (Q3)
- the S&P 500 ETFs (Q4)

Ulta came in and went out within two quarters, which looks like a typical small-manager trade.

**Said:** Buffett gave the Apple tax reason and the Paramount mea culpa. Otherwise he did not explain, as CNBC noted at the time.

### 2025: quiet, with hidden buying

**Filings:** The selling slowed. Confidential treatment hid new buys of D R Horton, Lennar and Nucor in Q1 2025, revealed on 2025-08-14. UnitedHealth arrived in Q2 2025 and was sold in Q1 2026.

Alphabet A arrived in Q3 2025 at 17.85M shares (~$4.3B). That size suggests a decision at the top. CNBC reported "we don't know who made the call."

Chubb grew to 34.25M. Chevron was trimmed in 2024, then rebuilt to 130.2M by Q4 2025. NYT arrived in Q4 2025. Amazon was cut 77% in Q4 2025 and gone in Q1 2026.

### Q1 2026: the purge

**Filings:** This was the first Abel quarter. The 13F lost 16 holdings, going from 42 positions to 29:
- Visa, Mastercard, Amazon, Aon, Charter, Domino's, Pool, HEICO, Allegion, Lamar, Diageo, Liberty Latin America, Atlanta Braves, UnitedHealth, the Formula One tracker, and nearly all of Constellation (gone in Q2).

Chevron was cut by 35% (45.8M shares, about $8B). Berkshire then bought:
- Alphabet A +36.4M and Alphabet C 3.6M (new);
- Delta, 39.8M shares (new; first airline since the 2020 exit);
- a small Macy's position.

Lennar and NYT were roughly tripled.

**Filings (other-manager column):** Every position in the sold group except UNH, Aon, Diageo, the Formula One tracker, Liberty Latin America and Atlanta Braves had been reported only under other-manager codes 4, 8 and 11. That is the GEICO chain, where Combs was CEO.

**Inference (high confidence):** Q1 2026 was mainly the liquidation of the Combs book after his departure, plus a few Weschler holdings. The new money went into a few large ideas, which fits Abel's "concentrate… in a few high conviction ideas." Morningstar ascribes most of the sold names to Combs or Weschler too.

What would change my mind: a Berkshire statement that the sales were judgments about those companies rather than about the book.

The Chevron cut is different. Chevron is a large position Abel lists among the ones he and Buffett "rationalize". **Inference (medium):** that was a deliberate top-level decision about the position.

### Q2 2026: Berkshire becomes a net buyer

**Filings:** Berkshire was a net buyer of $19.8B, its first net-buying quarter since Q3 2022. It added about 48M Alphabet shares, of which about 60% came through a $10B private placement at a roughly 6.5% discount, announced 2026-06-01.

**Filings (issuer's 8-K exhibit):** https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000119312526257724/d83560dex992.htm

Berkshire also added Delta (+17.5M), Macy's and Lennar. In the same week, Berkshire agreed to buy Taylor Morrison, a homebuilder, for $6.8B. Alphabet A and C together are now about 12% of the 13F and the third-largest holding.

Buybacks restarted: $235M in Q1 2026, the first since May 2024, then about $4.5B in Q2 2026.

### Q3 2026: a small quarter, with Form 4 previews

**Filings:**
- Lennar A was roughly doubled to 26.0M. Berkshire crossed 10% on 2026-09-17, so Form 3 and Form 4 filings showed the buying within days.
- Bending Spoons arrived: 20.69M shares, from a $600M purchase at the July 2026 Nasdaq IPO.
- Delta rose to 64.45M.
- Berkshire kept trimming BofA, VeriSign (to 5.9M), Kroger, Capital One, Nucor and DaVita.
- Buybacks reached $9.6B, the most since 2021–22.

**Said, indirectly:** Buffett had long said Berkshire doesn't buy IPOs (Motley Fool's paraphrase). Bending Spoons breaks that habit. **Inference (low-medium):** the decision-maker has changed, or the rule did.

## Who is behind a change: the attribution problem

This is the hardest part of reading Berkshire, and Berkshire deliberately doesn't help.

What we know:
- **Said:** Abel says responsibility rests with him.
- **Said:** Weschler manages about $20B, roughly 6.5% of the 13F.
- **Said:** the large holdings are discussed between Abel and Buffett.
- **Said:** before 2026, Buffett handled positions of about $1B and more (AP's framing, 2025). He sometimes named a lieutenant after the fact, as with Amazon in 2019.

Evidence from the filings:
- **The GEICO chain.** The old Combs book sat exclusively on GEICO-chain rows (4, 8, 11). Bending Spoons, bought in 2026, also sits only there. The Bending Spoons 6-K names "Government Employees Insurance Company" as the buyer (link in log).
  - But GEICO also holds pieces of Apple, Coke, BofA, Chevron and Moody's. It holds 109.7M of the 125.7M SiriusXM shares.
  - **Inference (medium-low):** a new position that sits only on the GEICO chain is more likely to be Weschler's, since Abel says Weschler took over part of the Combs book. This is suggestive, not proof.
- **Rows coded "4" alone.** These include Berkshire parent holdings (all of Kraft Heinz) and, as the November 2026 SiriusXM Form 4 shows, the subsidiaries' pension plans. The 15.98M SiriusXM shares in the pension plans are inside the 13F count.
  - Weschler has historically managed pension money. He is a named group member on the DaVita 13D/As and the Liberty Media and SiriusXM 13Gs.
  - **Inference (medium):** DaVita, SiriusXM/Liberty and the small long-held names are Weschler's or legacy Buffett positions; Louisiana-Pacific, Jefferies and NVR are probably Weschler's.
- **Size.** Alphabet ($36B+), Delta ($5.4B), Chevron, BofA and Lennar ($2B+, a 10%+ stake, bought the day after a bad earnings report) are top-level in size. **Inference (medium):** these are Abel–Buffett decisions.
  - Macy's ($170M) and NYT ($1.0B) are ambiguous.
  - Delta and Macy's sit on the NICO chain (code 11) and other insurers, not on GEICO's.

**Practical rule:** treat a change worth $2B or more, or 0.7% or more of the 13F, as an Abel–Buffett decision. Treat anything under about $1B as possibly Weschler's. Don't write "Buffett bought" or "Abel bought" unless Berkshire or a filing says so.

## How to read the next filing

### What the 13F leaves out

- **The Japanese trading houses.** Mitsubishi, ITOCHU, Mitsui, Marubeni and Sumitomo are worth $35.4B at the end of 2025, and three are now held at more than 10%. They are listed in Japan, so they are never in the 13F.
  - The gap between Morningstar's "including foreign holdings" totals and the 13F totals was $49.5B at Q1 2026 and $53.4B at Q3 2026. Most of it is Japan.
  - Changes appear in the annual letter, in Japanese large-holding reports, and in the 10-Q equity buy and sell lines.
  - **Inference (low-medium):** in Q2 2026, 10-Q purchases ($23.5B) ran about $4B above my 13F-based estimate ($19.2B), which may have been Japan buying. Q1 and Q3 2026 reconciled within $1B.
- **Cash and T-bills, $350–380B.** These drive the story more than any one stock, and they are not in the 13F.
- **Whole businesses.** These include OxyChem ($9.7B, 2025), Bell Laboratories, Taylor Morrison ($6.8B, 2026), BNSF and BHE. An acquisition can be the reason a stock was sold, as cash or as a pattern: homebuilder stocks while buying Taylor Morrison, for example.
- **Equity-method holdings.** Occidental and Kraft Heinz do appear on the 13F at market value. Berkshire also holds OXY warrants and, in the past, preferreds, which the 13F doesn't show.
- **Private placements and negotiated deals.** Examples are the Alphabet discount block and the Bending Spoons IPO and private placement. The 13F shows the shares but not the terms or the discount.

### Timing tricks

- **Confidential treatment.** Berkshire used it twice in this period. It hid Chubb in Q4 2023 and the homebuilders and Nucor in Q1 2025.
  - Check the summary page's confidential-omission flag every quarter.
  - When it is "true", positions are being built and are missing, and the visible total is understated. The amendment usually comes one or two quarters later.
- **The 45-day lag is partly beaten by Form 4s.** Where Berkshire owns more than 10% of a class, it files Form 4 within two business days. That covers Lennar A, SiriusXM, DaVita, OXY, AmEx, Moody's and Kraft Heinz. VeriSign was covered while Berkshire held above 10%; after the 2025–26 cuts to 5.9M shares it is probably below the threshold. Those Form 4s often reveal the next 13F's direction weeks early.
  - October 2026 Form 4s already show more Lennar buying.
  - SiriusXM buying resumed on 2026-10-30 to 11-03, up to 125.73M shares, after two years flat.
  - So the Q4 2026 13F should show at least 28.4M Lennar A (the Form 4 total on 2026-10-02, up from 26.0M) and at least 125.7M SiriusXM (up from 124.8M). Neither should be read as news then.
- **Announced deals.** Bending Spoons's $330M placement (10M shares at $33, closed 2026-10-29) means the Q4 2026 13F should show about 30.7M shares. That is expected, not new.

### Mechanical and administrative changes

- **DaVita is cut every quarter** under a standing agreement that keeps Berkshire at or below 45% as DaVita buys back stock. These are mechanical, not a view. The latest Form 4 (2026-10-27) took it to 28.3M shares.
- **Corporate actions.** Examples are SiriusXM's merger (2024), the Liberty Live split-off (Q4 2025) and spin-offs. They create apparent new and closed positions with new CUSIPs. Match by CUSIP and check for corporate events before calling something a trade.
- **Share classes** (Alphabet A/C, Lennar A/B, Liberty Live A/C) are separate rows. Add them up to see the issuer.
- **Rows.** About 90–140 rows collapse to about 30–42 positions. Never read a single row as a position.
- **Signer and filing agent.** Marc Hamburg still signs, though Chuck Chang became CFO on 2026-06-01. A new signer in 2027 is administrative, as was the 2025 filing-agent change.

### Noise vs signal, in practice

**Usually noise:**
- Value swings in unchanged positions. Most quarter-to-quarter change in the total is Apple's price.
- Stub rows: D R Horton 3,564 shares, NVR, Jefferies.
- DaVita trims.
- Corporate-action CUSIP changes.
- Additions already disclosed by Form 4 or a press release.

**Pattern, not news:**
- Continued BofA, Capital One, Kroger, VeriSign and Nucor trims. Note a stop or a reversal instead.

**Signal:**
- Any share change in AmEx, Coca-Cola, Moody's or Kraft Heinz.
- An Apple cut under Abel.
- A new position above about $2B.
- A full exit from one of Abel's named "other significant" holdings (BofA, Chevron, Alphabet).
- The confidential flag set to "true".
- A quarter where the 10-Q shows Berkshire as a net buyer of stock while the 13F shows little, which points to non-US buying.
- Large buybacks alongside stock sales. Abel uses buybacks much more than Buffett did, and they compete with stock buying for the same cash.

## Open questions, and what would settle them

- **Who bought Delta, Macy's, NYT and Bending Spoons?** Berkshire hasn't said. The 2026 annual letter (expected late February 2027) or the 2027 meeting may say. Until then, use the size rule above.
- **Is Abel more of a trader than Buffett?** Turnover in 2026 is high (about $31B of sales and $42B of purchases in the first nine months, per the 10-Q), but most of it was a one-time clean-up. If Q4 2026 and 2027 show 2026-scale turnover without a comparable clean-up to explain it, that answers yes. I'd currently say no (medium confidence).
- **Is the move out of financials complete?** Watch BofA. Abel named it as an "other significant" holding, and sales have continued every quarter. A stop would mean it is now at the size they want.
- **Japan.** Holdings rose above 10% in 2025. Watch the annual letter's table for further increases. They won't show in the 13F.
