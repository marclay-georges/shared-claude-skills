## The short version

Dorsey runs one concentrated global equity strategy: 10 to 15 stocks, a 15% maximum position, no shorts, no leverage, no currency hedging. A Dorsey 13F has only 9 to 13 rows, so every row is a decision. Three things make a raw quarter-over-quarter diff misleading for this manager.

1. **Client flows move every continuing position by the same percentage.** Assets are about $1.5–1.7B across only nine accounts, so flows are lumpy. In Q2 and Q3 2026, inflows added about 8% and 12% to the share count of every position Dorsey otherwise left alone. Subtract that common percentage before calling anything a buy or a trim.

2. **Price swings, not trades, drive much of the weight change.** AppLovin shows this most clearly. In Q3 2026 Dorsey added 37% to the share count, yet the position's weight fell from 14.5% to 10.2% because the price fell 44%. Corporate actions do the same thing:
   - Booking's 25:1 split in April 2026.
   - Ashtead turning into NYSE-listed Sunbelt Rentals in March 2026.
   - Smartsheet's take-private in January 2025.

3. **The 13F shows most of the book, but not all of it.** Dorsey says 30–40% of the portfolio has historically been outside the US, and the firm owns local shares abroad. US-listed foreign companies such as ASML and AerCap do appear in the 13F. Shares listed only abroad do not. The gap between the ADV's assets and the 13F total has run from about 30% (early 2024) down to roughly 10–20% (2025–26).

Beneath those mechanics is a manager who turns the book over far more than the "moat investor" label suggests:
- Only Meta survives from the Q4 2023 portfolio.
- Dorsey says most sales are admitted errors and that the client base is mostly tax-exempt, so switching costs little.
- New positions usually start at 6–7% of the book, with room to average down. An entry much larger than that, like Veeva at 14% in Q3 2026, signals unusual conviction.

## Who Dorsey is, in Dorsey's own words

Pat Dorsey ran Morningstar's equity research from 2000 to 2011 and built its economic-moat framework. Dorsey wrote *The Five Rules for Successful Stock Investing* and *The Little Book That Builds Wealth*, and founded Dorsey Asset Management in 2014.

- **Said:** "we launched with a whopping 3 million in assets under management" (Capital Allocators EP.509). Morningstar's hosts now introduce it as "a boutique asset manager serving institutional clients" ([Morningstar, *The Long View*, 2026-03-31](https://www.morningstar.com/podcasts/the-long-view/pat-dorsey-economic-moats-more)); that is their description, not Dorsey's.

**What the strategy is.**
- **Said:** the website describes a "single concentrated global equity strategy" of 10–15 businesses, chosen for moats, reinvestment runways and capital allocation. It calls the firm "geographically agnostic" ([dorseyasset.com/what-we-do](https://dorseyasset.com/what-we-do/)).
- **Said:** the firm's LinkedIn page says it does "not short, use leverage, or hedge currency exposure" ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/dorsey-asset-management/)).
- **Said:** the June 2026 deck gave about $1.7B in assets at 2026-06-01, "largely institutional", and "Twelve positions currently, with 59% of capital in top five" ([Maximizing Moats deck, June 2026](https://dorseyasset.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/maximizing-moats_reinvestment-runways-capital-allocation_june-2026.pdf)).

**Sizing and the position cap.**
- **Said:** on Capital Allocators, recorded mid-2026 ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P25Ahug2-eQ); transcript via [usetranscribe](https://www.usetranscribe.io/yt/P25Ahug2-eQ/investing-in-moats)):
  - "kind of max is 15", reserved for a business where management is "an A+", the moat "phenomenal", and the expected return "well above our 15% hurdle rate."
  - "We've historically owned between 10 and 15. And the 15's a hard number. That's sort of one in one out."
  - Most positions "will start probably closer to six or seven just to give us dry powder" in case "you need [to] average down." Some start "closer to the nine or 10 level."

**How the emphasis shifted toward management.**
- **Said:** in 2014 Dorsey weighted moat about 60–70% and management about 30%, and "I'm probably the reverse today … because I've seen just how poorly people can behave" (Capital Allocators).
- **Said:** the main management trait Dorsey looks for is humility. "I'm mainly focused on avoiding that left tail."
- **Said:** Dorsey distrusts the "cult of the founder." Founders should be judged "on a level playing field with nonfounders" (Morningstar).
- **Said:** acquisitions are usually value-destroying, but "acquiring companies in the way of a Danaher or a TransDigm or Constellation, it's a learned skill."

**Where Dorsey looks and what it avoids.**
- **Said:** Dorsey avoids price-taker industries: "spent almost no time on auto parts or insurance companies or banks in the US or chemical companies or energy" (Morningstar). The firm prefers "aerospace or semiconductors or enterprise software or internet advertising."
- **Said:** Dorsey leans away from consumer brands and CPG for lack of pattern recognition there (Capital Allocators).
- **Inference** (moderate): "auto parts" here means parts makers. The book held the retailer AutoZone through 2024–25.

**Selling, turnover and holding periods.**
- **Said:** "the bulk of our sales have been driven by errors that we needed to correct" (Capital Allocators).
- **Said:** "Your favorite holding period should be as long as the thesis holds up."
- **Said:** "you rebuy your portfolio every morning" (Morningstar).
- **Said:** "the bulk of our investors are nontaxable," which makes switching between ideas "more frictionless" (Morningstar).
- **Said:** the private structure insulates Dorsey from flow-driven behaviour. The firm is unlikely ever to run a daily-liquidity fund (Morningstar).

**Non-US holdings.**
- **Said:** "historically about 30-40% of our portfolio has been outside the US" (Capital Allocators).
- **Said:** the firm built "the operational infrastructure to enable you to own local common" shares abroad, because large US capital pools "just don't invest in non-ADR securities."
- **Said:** it puts locally driven businesses (a São Paulo drugstore, local-regulation stories) in the "too hard" pile.
- **Said:** "our largest position right now is ASML," and "We've done a lot of work on the … aerospace ecosystem with Safran and Rolls."

**Edge.**
- **Said:** Dorsey claims a behavioural rather than informational edge over short-horizon "pod shops" ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/podcasts/the-long-view/pat-dorsey-economic-moats-more)).
- **Said:** the example given was ASML: "all through '25 … ASML was going down most of the year … we owned it and just sat on it."
- **Said:** "weirder is better" is an error Dorsey once made ("Weird isn't wonderful").

**Older material for the enduring philosophy.**

## What the 13F can and cannot see for this manager

**Structure (Filed, ADV Part 1 filed 2026-03-24, and the Part 2A brochure of the same date):**
- $1,445,270,658 in regulatory assets, all discretionary, across **nine accounts**.
- Those accounts are three pooled vehicles ($861M), five charitable organisations ($569M) and one high-net-worth individual ($15M).
- The funds are Global Moat Fund LP, Global Moat Offshore Fund LP and DAM SIF I LP. Separate accounts are run pari passu with the funds.
- Currency is generally not hedged.
- Fees are 1–1.25%, with no performance fees.
- **Oddity:** the brochure labels the AUM figure "as of February 28, 2025." That label is probably stale (see log).

**Inference** (high): a handful of accounts makes flows large and lumpy relative to the book. One charity adding or withdrawing money shows up as every position moving together. Charities and pooled vehicles are tax-exempt, which is consistent with what Dorsey said about nontaxable clients.

**13F mechanics (Filed):**
- Every row is SH, SOLE discretion.
- There are no options, no other-included managers, no confidential-treatment requests, no amendments and no 13F-NT notices in the period.
- A filing agent submits the filings, so accession numbers carry the agent prefix 0001398344.

Because of all this, the cover and summary pages carry little information beyond the total value and entry count. Watch for an amendment or a confidential-treatment request precisely because there has never been one.

**What is missing from the 13F:**
- Non-US local listings.
- Cash.
- Any position on a foreign exchange.

## How to read the next Dorsey 13F

**1. Find the flow first.**
- Compute each continuing position's percentage change in share count.
- If three or more positions share the same figure to a tenth of a percent, that figure is the client flow.
- Past flows:

| Quarter | Common flow |
|---|---|
| Q4 2023 | -5.5% |
| Q1 2024 | -1.3% |
| Q2 2024 | -1.3% |
| Q3 2024 | -2.9% |
| Q1 2025 | -0.1% |
| Q3 2025 | -7.1% |
| Q4 2025 | -3.9% |
| Q1 2026 | +0.8% |
| Q2 2026 | +7.9% |
| Q3 2026 | +12.4% |

- Q4 2024 and Q2 2025 showed no clean common percentage.
- Measure decisions against that baseline. A position up 12.4% in Q3 2026 was untouched, not bought.
- With nine accounts, one client can swing this by several percent. A large negative flow is a client redemption, not a view on the market.

**2. Separate price from trading.**
- Divide value by shares for the implied price, then compare it with last quarter's.
- **AppLovin worked example:**
  - Q2 2026: 439,313 shares at $515, worth $226M, 14.5% of the 13F.
  - Q3 2026: 602,035 shares at $290, worth $175M, 10.2% of the 13F.
  - With no trading at all, the position would have been worth about $128M, or 7.5%. Dorsey bought about 163,000 shares, roughly $47M at quarter-end prices, and the weight still fell.
- **ASML went the other way in Q2 2026:** shares -7%, price +51%, weight up from 14.8% to 16.6%.
- AppLovin and ASML are volatile, high-multiple holdings whose weights will keep swinging. Read their share counts, not their weights.

**3. Check corporate actions before reading a share change.**
- **Booking:** the 25:1 split took effect 2026-04-02. Pre-split share counts must be multiplied by 25. The N-PX record-date count of 411,100 equals 16,444 × 25.
- **Smartsheet:** the take-private explains its exit.
- **Sunbelt:** shows how a foreign holding can appear on redomicile.
- If a new row debuts above 9–10% without a matching listing event, it is a real high-conviction buy. If one appears at the same time as a US listing or redomicile, suspect a conversion of a foreign holding.

**4. Remember the invisible slice.**
- Dorsey says 30–40% of the book has historically been non-US. Some of that is US-listed (ASML, AerCap) and appears in the 13F. Local-only listings do not.
- Recently the ADV/13F gap has been roughly 10–20% of assets. That includes cash, and at least one non-13F stock as of mid-2026.
- 13F weights therefore overstate true portfolio weights.
- A 13F row count below 10 does not mean the book has shrunk below its stated 10–15 range.
- When comparing with Dorsey's own statements ("12 positions", "59% in top five"), expect differences.

**5. Read sizes as conviction signals.**
- Normal entries come in at 5–8%. Large entries are rare: Veeva at 14%, AerCap at 12%.
- Adding while the price falls is in character: Danaher in 2025, AppLovin in Q3 2026, Meta in Q1 2025.
- So is trimming into strength: Meta 2023–24, ASML in 2026.
- A position above 15% of the 13F got there through price, not because Dorsey sized it there. Expect trims.
- Watch the 15-name hard cap. At 12 stocks plus the ETF, every new name now makes an exit more likely.

**6. Read exits as probable admitted errors, but verify.**
- **Said:** "the bulk of our sales" are error corrections.
- The sourced case is PayPal.
- Most other exits have no public reason:
  - AppLovin's first round
  - Herc
  - Upwork
  - Wix
  - Semrush
  - Sprout
  - Alphabet
  - AutoZone
  - Enovis
  - Danaher
  - Sunbelt
- Some, like Alphabet after a big run, look more like opportunity cost. Do not invent a reason. Say "no stated reason" unless a letter or interview supplies one.

**7. Expect Q1 rotations.**
- Q1 2025 and Q1 2026 were both turnover spikes of about 27–28%, with three exits and three or four new names each.
- **Inference** (low): this may reflect an annual portfolio review or year-end letter cycle.
- **What would change my mind:** a Q1 2027 without a rotation.

**8. Filing mechanics.**
- The Q4 2025 13F was filed on 2026-02-17 and the Q3 2026 13F on 2026-11-16. Both deadlines fell on a weekend or holiday, so a filing a day or two after day 45 is normal for this filer, not late.
- Values are in whole dollars.
- Watch for a first-ever 13F-HR/A or confidential-treatment request. That would hint at a position being built quietly.

**9. Use the other filings.**
- **N-PX (filed each August):**
  - It covers say-on-pay votes only.
  - Foreign issuers such as ASML and AerCap have none.
  - Record-date share counts give intra-quarter snapshots. For example, AppLovin was 341,541 shares at its June 2026 meeting.
  - An AGAINST vote is rare and worth noting.
- **ADV (each March):** gives assets, account count and client mix.
- **Form D:**
