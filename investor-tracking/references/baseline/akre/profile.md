# Akre Capital Management — investor profile and how to read its 13F

**Manager:** Akre Capital Management LLC, Middleburg, Virginia. CIK 0001112520, 13F file no. 028-06599.
**People:** John Neff, CEO, CIO and sole portfolio manager since September 2023 (joined 2009 from William Blair, co-PM from 2014). Chuck Akre founded the firm in 1989, stopped managing the portfolio at the end of 2020, and remains the principal owner. Chris Cerrone (PM from January 2020) left in September 2023; Tom Saberhagen (co-PM 2014–2019) left in 2019. Analysts Andrew Millette and Trey Tickner took equity in the firm in January 2025. About 12 employees, 3 in investment roles (ADV, March 2026).
**Period studied:** 13F filings for Q4 2023 through Q3 2026, plus the fund's annual and half-year reports, its quarterly N-PORT filings (holdings and monthly flows, January 2024 to July 2026), the adviser's 13G and N-PX filings, ADV Parts 1 and 2A, the Braddock funds' Form Ds, and every quarterly letter from Q3 2023 to Q3 2026, read in date order.
**Written:** 2026-11-19. Holdings are in `holdings/`, sources and problems in `log.md`.

Throughout, I keep three kinds of statement apart. **Said** means the manager wrote or spoke it, with the source in `log.md`. **Filings show** means it comes straight from a filing. **Inferred** means it is my reading of the numbers, with a confidence level.

---

## The short version

Akre runs one strategy: a concentrated portfolio of about 15–20 compounders, held for years, built around what Chuck Akre called the three-legged stool (an extraordinary business, talented and honest managers, and a record of reinvesting free cash flow at high rates). Neff has kept that discipline. What changed over these three years is not the philosophy but the plumbing, and the plumbing now dominates the 13F.

From the start of 2024 through Q3 2026, every single quarter was a net-selling quarter, and the reported 13F value fell from $11.9B to $4.4B. Almost all of that is client money leaving, first from the mutual fund and then, much faster, from the ETF it became in October 2025. So the first thing a reader must do with a new Akre 13F is strip out the flow. Most share counts fall every quarter, and that is not a decision. The decisions are in the differences: a name whose share count holds flat or rises while everything else falls 10–15% is being bought; a new name is being bought with fresh cash; and a name falling much faster than the rest is either being sold on purpose or being handed out in redemption baskets, which since the conversion Neff says he chooses deliberately.

The second thing is that this 13F does not show the whole strategy. About a fifth of the flagship fund (19–23% at every fund quarter-end from July 2025 to July 2026, per N-PORT) sits in Canadian- and European-listed names (Constellation Software, Topicus, LVMH) that never appear in a 13F, and a few small rows belong to the Braddock private funds and separate accounts rather than to the fund. Percentages computed from the 13F overstate the US names' weight in the strategy.

The third is that, since early 2026, Neff has been unusually active by his own standards: buying software and data businesses (Salesforce, ServiceNow, more FICO, CCC, CoStar, Roper) into an AI-driven sell-off he believes is wrong, and adding small new positions (Rollins, TransDigm). He has said the ETF wrapper lets him trim richly valued positions with less concern for taxes. Expect more name turnover than Akre's history would suggest.

## Who the investor is

**Said.** The firm's stated goal is to compound capital at above-average rates while taking below-average risk. The method is to find a small number of businesses that clear the three-legged-stool test and then hold them, letting reinvested free cash flow do the work. The letters return again and again to patience and to not chasing: "We will not abandon what works over time to chase what's working at the time" (Q4 2023 letter). In the Q1 2025 letter Neff describes underwriting a five-year base case with a mid-to-high-teens annual return and a low case that is still positive, and he notes that years can pass between new additions. In the Q4 2025 letter, after a year in which the fund returned about 1% against 18% for the S&P 500, he used the Gom Jabbar test from *Dune* as the image for refusing to flinch: "We do not chase performance."

**Said.** Neff frames the portfolio as being underweight what the market has rewarded lately. The Q1 2024 letter criticised the concentration of index returns in the largest technology stocks and passive flows "instructed" rather than "served." The Q2 2024 letter noted the portfolio's median market cap (about $65B) is far below the index's giants. From late 2025 onward the letters argue the market is wrong to treat AI as a threat to the portfolio's software and data businesses ("is AI eating software?" on the Animal Spirits podcast, December 2025; etf.com called the ETF a bet that "the market is wrong on AI," April 2026).

**Filings show.** Fees and clients from the March 2026 ADV: about $11.8B of regulatory assets at the end of 2025, of which about $9.9B (83%) was one registered fund, about $1.3B three Braddock private funds (fees 1.25% plus 20% over a hurdle), and the rest separate accounts for corporations and wealthy individuals (1.50%, $5M minimum). The ETF charges 0.98%. The firm describes its strategy as focused and non-diversified, buy and hold. ADV Part 1 (June 2026, read on the SEC adviser site) confirms $11.82B across 218 accounts and shows the private funds are one master-feeder structure: Braddock Partners LP and Braddock Partners Offshore Ltd feed the master, Braddock Partners Offshore LP ($1.25B gross assets), which is why that entity is the "other included manager" on the 13F. Akre and its related persons own about 47% of the private funds, so the Braddock-only rows are partly the firm's own money. The Braddock Form Ds (signed by Charles T. Akre Jr.) show little new money: cumulative sales rose only about $15M across 2024–2025.

**Inferred (high confidence).** This is, in practice, a single-portfolio firm whose 13F is driven by one vehicle. Anything that happens to the ETF's flows happens to the 13F.

## What the 13F actually contains

The 13F is filed by the adviser and covers three kinds of accounts together: the Akre Focus ETF (formerly the Akre Focus Fund), the Braddock private funds, and separate accounts. Every cover page lists Braddock Partners Offshore, LP as an "other included manager," but no row is tagged to it, so you cannot split the 13F by vehicle from the filing itself.

Comparing the fund's own schedules (January and July 2026) with the 13F for the nearest quarter, the fund holds roughly 65–95% of the 13F share count in most names (for example Mastercard about 65–70%, Brookfield about 75–80%, CCC about 90%, FICO about 75%, Salesforce shares 100%). The rest is the private funds and separate accounts. O'Reilly is the exception: the fund cut it from 6.56M shares (July 2025) to 0.70M (July 2026) while the other accounts kept roughly 1.3–1.5M, so by mid-2026 about two-thirds of the 13F's O'Reilly sits outside the fund. A name's 13F trend can therefore hide a fund-level exit.

Several small rows are not in the fund at all and belong to those other accounts: SOPHiA Genetics (a June 2025 Schedule 13G confirms it is held by the Braddock vehicles, 5.6% of the company), Goosehead Insurance, Perimeter Solutions, a sliver of Berkshire Hathaway, the residual American Tower, and the Salesforce call options (the fund reports holding no options). Treat these as legacy or private-fund positions. They rarely say anything about the main strategy.

What the 13F leaves out matters more. At 31 July 2026 the fund held Constellation Software (12.7% of the fund), Topicus (7.3%) and LVMH (2.5%), plus Constellation warrants. None of these is a US-listed security, so none appears in the 13F. Earlier in the period the fund also held Lumine Group (eliminated by mid-2024). The adviser's N-PX shows it also votes Constellation shares in non-fund accounts, so the private funds hold it too. Practical consequence: Mastercard at 21.9% of the Q3 2026 13F is about 14% of the fund. Constellation is effectively the fund's second-largest holding and is invisible here. The N-PORTs show these off-13F names do get traded: Constellation was held flat at 416,720 shares from October 2025 to April 2026 and then cut by a quarter to 310,679 by July 2026, and Topicus fell 11% in the same months; LVMH went from 429,539 shares (January 2026) to 206,922 (April) and back up to 242,964 (July). None of that reaches the 13F.

Since the conversion the ETF publishes its full holdings daily on akrefund.com. For the fund itself, that is fresher and more complete than any 13F. The 13F remains the only view of the whole firm, including the private funds and accounts.

## How the portfolio moved, in date order

The narrative is told as it unfolded: what Neff said, what the filings then showed, and what he said next.

### Late 2023: a new sole manager, a familiar book

**Said.** The September 2023 annual letter (the first after Cerrone's departure left Neff as sole PM) reported that the fund had exited Salesforce over 2022–23, added Brookfield Corporation, trimmed CarMax, and noted that American Tower was at its lowest valuation since 2017. The Q4 2023 letter added that the Veralto shares received in the Danaher spin-off were sold in October. Morningstar cut the fund to Neutral in October 2023, citing key-person risk.

**Filings show.** At the end of 2023 the 13F held 18 rows and $11.9B. Top five: Mastercard 18.2%, Moody's 15.7%, American Tower 12.4%, KKR 11.6%, O'Reilly 8.2%. Then Visa, Roper, Brookfield, CoStar and Danaher. The top ten were 96% of the value; the long tail was tiny.

### 2024: steady trimming, a few small experiments

**Filings show.** Through 2024 the shares of almost every core name fell by a few percent each quarter (Mastercard, Moody's, KKR, O'Reilly, American Tower) while values held up because prices rose. My estimate of net trading (change in shares times average price) is a net sale of $340–680M every quarter of 2024. New names: CCC Intelligent Solutions (Q1, 7.0M shares), Uber (Q1 only, 1.78M shares), Airbnb (Q2, built to 2.65M by Q3), SBA Communications (added in Q2 to 500k, gone by Q3). Exits: the leftover Brookfield Asset Management shares (Q1), Dollar Tree (Q1–Q2), Uber (Q2), SBA (Q3). DigitalBridge and CarMax shrank steadily. The Q1 2024 13F was amended ten days after filing to add a small SOPHiA line it had left out.

**Said.** The fund's July 2024 annual report called Uber "an exploratory position, and we have since pivoted to Airbnb," and said Adobe and Lumine were eliminated. The Q3 2024 letter explained Airbnb as bought while a travel-slowdown worry hung over it and said the firm "would relish the opportunity to add… at lower prices." The same report showed the fund paying out large redemptions in kind ($942.7M of in-kind sales in the six months to January 2025).

**Inferred (medium-high confidence).** The uniform small cuts across the core book in 2024 were mostly redemption-driven, not view-driven. The real decisions that year were the new small positions (CCC, Airbnb, the brief Uber and SBA tests) and the acceleration in Moody's and KKR selling in Q3 2024 (about −20% and −17% of shares in one quarter, well above the flow rate).

The adviser's own say-on-pay votes for the year to June 2024 show it abstained (recorded as against management) on American Tower, DigitalBridge and Uber, and voted for everywhere else. All three were reduced or exited within a year. Weak signal; I would not lean on it alone.

### Q4 2024 – mid 2025: the rate-sensitive exit and a cash buffer

**Said.** This is the clearest example in the period of a stated view followed by trades. The Q4 2024 letter (January 2025) said: "Where interest rate sensitivity intersects with lower growth or business quality, we are taking steps to reduce our exposure," and named American Tower, Moody's, CarMax and CoStar as rate-sensitive. In the Q1 2025 letter ("Gravity's Rainbow") Neff raised cash to 6.3% "given increasing economic uncertainty," wanting more dry powder. Q2 2025: cash 8.1%, which he described as within the usual 5–10% range.

**Filings show.** American Tower went from 5.5M shares (Q4 2024) to 2.6M (Q1 2025) to 0.9M (Q2 2025) and was effectively gone by end-2025 (a few tens of thousands of shares remain in non-fund accounts). CarMax went from 2.7M shares in late 2023 to 0.15M by Q2 2025 and was exited in Q1 2026. Moody's lost about a fifth of its shares in Q1 2025 alone. CoStar, though named, was not sold; its share count actually rose. The Q1 2025 quarter was the largest net sale up to that point (about −$1.34B, 11.6% of the prior quarter's value).

New positions arrived in Q2 2025: Fair Isaac (a token 9,148 shares, then 261k by Q3 2025) and Copart (1.2M shares, 7.9M by Q4 2025). O'Reilly's share count jumped from 0.86M to 9.4M in Q2 2025 because of a 15-for-1 split; adjusted, it was a cut of about 27%.

**Inferred (high confidence).** American Tower and CarMax were deliberate, explained exits. FICO and Copart were the first meaningful new compounders of Neff's sole tenure and were funded by the cash raised and by the American Tower proceeds.

### Q3 2025 – Q4 2025: the ETF conversion

**Said.** The firm announced in June 2025 that it would convert the $12.2B mutual fund into an ETF, dropping its earlier application to add an ETF share class. The Q3 2025 letter and the "Why We Are Converting" note explained that redemptions had been met in kind using third-party capital providers (the January 2026 report names the ReFlow liquidity program, which took $405M of securities in kind between August and mid-October 2025) and that the ETF would let the fund dispose of appreciated securities through in-kind redemptions "whenever practicable." The Q3 2025 letter also addressed Constellation Software's 26% fall (AI fears and Mark Leonard stepping down in September 2025): "no fundamental concerns" and no change to position size. The conversion closed on 24–27 October 2025.

The Q4 2025 letter set out the ETF mechanics that matter most for reading the 13F: creations are taken entirely in cash, and redemptions are met in kind "using select securities with lower expected returns or to adjust position sizing", not pro rata.

**Filings show.** In Q3 2025 Danaher was halved and then exited by Q1 2026. Roper lost 20% of its shares. FICO and Copart were built up. Brookfield's 3-for-2 split (October 2025) makes its Q4 2025 share count look like a 40% addition; adjusted, it was a 7% cut. Brookfield rose to 13.5% of the 13F at end-2025, the second-largest line.

### Q1 2026 – Q3 2026: outflows, an AI sell-off, and buying into it

**Filings show.** The 13F fell from $9.1B (Q4 2025) to $6.1B (Q1 2026), $5.1B (Q2) and $4.4B (Q3). The fund's July 2026 annual report shows ETF redemptions of $4.41B against creations of $417M in the year, $3.62B of gains realised through in-kind redemptions, and net assets down from $12.48B to $5.24B. The fund returned −20.1% in that year against +19.6% for the S&P 500.

In Q1 2026, the worst quarter for flows, the share counts of Brookfield (−37%), Visa (−39%), O'Reilly (−42%), Airbnb (−41%), Moody's (−28%) and Mastercard (−24%) fell sharply. At the same time Roper (+14%), FICO (+31%), CCC (+27%) and CoStar (+8%) rose, and Salesforce (718k shares plus a 100,000-share call) and ServiceNow (1.10M shares) appeared, along with a small Perimeter Solutions line. CCC crossed 5% of its company (13G filed May 2026). In Q2 2026 the cuts continued (Visa −32%, KKR −26%, O'Reilly −44%) while CoStar (+13%) and ServiceNow (+14%) were added. In Q3 2026 Airbnb was sold out entirely, Rollins (320k shares) and TransDigm (11.5k shares) were started, and FICO shares rose about 4%.

**Said.** The Q1 2026 letter (−19.4% for the quarter) said the portfolio's price to forward free cash flow per share had fallen from about 37x in July 2025 to about 19x, blamed two narratives (AI disrupting software and data, and private-credit fears hitting KKR and Brookfield), named the businesses it considered mispriced, and said the firm was building LVMH and Copart "under temporary overhangs." It also said: "as an ETF, we can more actively manage high valuation positions with less concern for tax consequences." The Q2 2026 letter said free cash flow per share had grown 23% while the multiple sank to about 17x, the lowest since 2016, and that the team would stay "lashed to the mast." The Q3 2026 letter (written October 2026) said the team had "initiated and built positions… under cover of temporary storm clouds", namely LVMH (weak luxury demand), Copart (lost a large insurance customer), FICO ("a political scapegoat for housing affordability") and CoStar (questions about its residential business), and had "initiated small positions in TransDigm and Rollins that may grow over time." Cash was 2.8%.

**Inferred (medium-high confidence).** Since the conversion, the redemption baskets have been used to shrink the long-held, richly valued, low-cost-basis positions (Visa, Mastercard, Moody's, O'Reilly, Brookfield, KKR) faster than the rest, and Neff has said as much. Some of that is a view on valuation and some is tax management, and the 13F cannot tell the two apart. One check against the letters: the Q1 2026 letter speaks of building LVMH, yet the fund's LVMH shares fell by half between January and April 2026 before partly recovering by July, so "building" fits 2025 and mid-2026 better than the first quarter. What is unambiguous is the buying: with creations in cash, new names and rising share counts during a period of heavy outflow are paid for with cash from sales or from new creations, and those are decisions.

The N-PORTs pin down the Airbnb timing: the fund held 3.26M shares at 31 October 2025, 2.40M at 31 January 2026, 1.17M at 30 April and 1.01M at 31 July, and none at 30 September (13F). It was a nine-month wind-down, much faster than flows, with the last million shares going in August–September 2026. I could not find a stated reason. It was one of the fund's best contributors in the year to July 2026, so selling it fits "trim what has done well to fund what has been marked down," but that is my inference, not something he said.

### The Fair Isaac drop in Q3 2026

**Filings show.** FICO's value roughly halved between Q2 and Q3 2026 (implied price about $1,195 to about $592) while Akre's share count rose from 361,826 to 376,072.

**Cause, from reporting (log has the links).** On 4 September 2026 FICO fell about 21% after FHFA Director Bill Pulte renewed his criticism of credit-scoring costs, following moves to let Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac accept VantageScore 4.0. On 28 September Pulte announced a single pricing grid treating VantageScore on par with FICO in mortgage pricing, and FICO fell about 26% on 29 September. Rocket Mortgage said it would make VantageScore its preferred score, and TransUnion committed to holding its VantageScore price.

**Said.** Neff's Q3 2026 letter treats this as a temporary overhang and describes FICO as a political scapegoat.

**Inferred (high confidence).** Given the roughly 12% outflow in the quarter, a 4% increase in shares is an active add of about 15–20% relative to where flows alone would have left it. This is a conviction hold, not capitulation. Q4 2026 is the test.

## How to read the next filing

**Start with the flow.** Before looking at any name, estimate how much client money left. The ETF's shares outstanding and net assets at quarter end are published on akrefund.com. Monthly creations and redemptions are in the ETF's N-PORT (trust CIK 811030, series S000089351), filed about 60 days after each fund quarter-end in October, January, April and July. The one ending 31 October 2026 should appear around late December 2026. As a rough check inside the 13F itself, take the median percentage change in share count across the old core names (Mastercard, Visa, Moody's, KKR, Brookfield, O'Reilly, Roper). In 2026 that ran about −12% to −30% a quarter. That median is your flow baseline.

**Then read names against the baseline, not against zero.**
- A new CUSIP is the strongest signal. Creations come in cash, so a new name is bought with cash on purpose. Expect it to start small and grow over two or three quarters. A position under about 0.5% may be in the private funds or accounts rather than the ETF; check whether it appears in the ETF's daily holdings.
- A share count that is flat or rising while the baseline is −10% or worse is an active add (FICO, CCC, CoStar, Roper, Copart and ServiceNow have all done this in 2026).
- A share count falling much faster than the baseline is either a sale on purpose or a name favoured for redemption baskets. Since conversion Neff says baskets hold "select securities with lower expected returns or to adjust position sizing," so a fast decline is still mildly informative, but long-held low-cost-basis names are favoured for tax reasons too. Check the next letter before calling it a change of view. A full exit (Airbnb in Q3 2026, American Tower in 2025) is a decision.
- Value changes on flat shares are price, not action. FICO in Q3 2026 is the textbook case.

**Watch for splits.** O'Reilly (15-for-1, June 2025) and Brookfield (3-for-2, October 2025) both created share-count jumps that look like huge buys. Check any jump of more than 30% against a split before reading it.

**Remember what the 13F leaves out.** Constellation Software, Topicus and LVMH are about a fifth of the fund and never appear. Their trades, such as the quarter of Constellation sold in May–July 2026, show only in N-PORT and the daily holdings. Divide 13F weights by roughly 1.25 to approximate fund weights for the US names, and remember that a name like O'Reilly can be largely held outside the fund. The 45-day lag also matters more here than for most managers: the ETF's daily holdings will usually have already shown any change, so the 13F's main added value is the firm-wide picture.

**Ignore the small tail unless it moves.** SOPHiA, Goosehead, Perimeter, Berkshire, the residual American Tower and the Salesforce calls are non-fund holdings. A SOPHiA 13G amendment or a Braddock-related filing would tell you about the private funds, not the strategy. The Salesforce call line is the only option position in the period and is about 0.5% of value; it is not an options strategy.

**Parts of the filing worth a glance.** The cover page total and row count reconcile cleanly every quarter, so a mismatch would be news. The other-included-managers list has been Braddock Partners Offshore alone throughout; a change there means the private-fund structure changed. There has been no confidential-treatment omission and no late filing; either would be unusual. An amendment has happened once (Q1 2024, to add a missed small line). The signer has been the head trader every quarter.

**Key-person risk is real.** The whole strategy rests on John Neff. Morningstar flagged this in 2023. Any change in the PM line in the ETF's filings, or in the ADV's control persons, would matter more than any single trade. Chuck Akre still owns the firm.

**What would change my read.** A quarter with net buying would mean outflows have stopped, and from then on share-count changes would be cleaner signals. A sharp cut to FICO, CCC or CoStar after Neff's public defence of them would mean the AI and regulatory theses have been abandoned, which would be a real break with what he has been writing since late 2025.

## Confidence and caveats

- The holdings files are exact copies of the filings and reconcile to the cover pages every quarter. The flow estimates are mine and approximate.
- The quotations come from the letters as published by Akre and reposted by Seeking Alpha, Yahoo/GuruFocus, hedgefundalpha and similar sites, cross-checked where possible against the fund's N-CSR reports. The fund-versus-13F ownership split is computed from fund report dates (end of January and end of July) that do not line up with 13F quarter ends, so treat the 65–95% range as indicative.
- The N-PORT flow figures are as filed. Fund quarters are offset by one month from 13F quarters.
- I could not find a free copy of the February 6, 2026 letter from the investment team. A paywalled summary suggests it argued that vertical software is too cheap and mission-critical for customers to replace with AI, and named Roper, Constellation, Topicus and CCC. I did not read the semi-annual call replays or the fund's own N-PX. The call replays might hold the Airbnb reasoning.
