# Greenlea Lane Capital Management — investor profile and 13F baseline

- **Manager:** Greenlea Lane Capital Management, LLC (Weston, CT). 13F filer CIK 0001766504; SEC adviser CRD 162012.
- **People:** Josh Tarasoff, founder, sole owner and portfolio manager since 2006 (signs every 13F). Panayiota Economou, Chief Operating Officer since December 2024 (Form ADV).
- **Period covered:** 13Fs for Q4 2023 through Q3 2026, all twelve present, with older filings read for context.
- **Written:** 2026-11-19. Data: `holdings/YYYY-QN.csv`. Every filing and source, with problems: `log.md`.

Throughout, **[said]** marks the manager's own words, **[filings]** marks what the documents show, and **[inference]** marks my reading, with confidence.

---

## 1. Who he is and what he is trying to do

Tarasoff started Greenlea Lane in 2006 out of Columbia Business School with $2.3M of his own and friends' and family's money. He has never run an office or a marketing effort, and through 2025 he was the only investment professional. **[said]** His stated strategy (2019 Ivey slides) is "to identify the most exceptional companies that I can understand, buy cheaply, and hold on for the long haul, ideally forever." The mission is "to be a world-class investment that makes a difference for a group of Partners with whom Josh feels a personal connection." ([slides](https://www.ivey.uwo.ca/media/3785963/2019-conference_tarasoff.pdf)) The last public performance figure is 17.3% a year net from 2006 to mid-2021 ([MITIMCo interview, July 2021](https://joelmcohen.medium.com/greenlea-lane-founder-josh-tarasoff-interviewed-by-mit-investment-management-company-53e5e3fdc910)). Nothing more recent is public.

His thinking is unusually well documented for so private a manager, because he publishes essays rather than letters. Read in order, they show a consistent core:

- **Owning companies permanently (2017 onward).** **[said]** "My approach is to have a permanent attitude towards all of our investments." The exceptional compounders are "systematically undervalued for decades" because they look odd, their accounting hides their economics (he cites Markel's unrealized gains and Amazon's reinvestment), and their edge pays out over 5 to 20 years, which most investors ignore. He bought Amazon in 2011 at about 200 times earnings, which he reckoned was 30 to 40 times normalized free cash flow: "And I thought that was cheap!" He also bets on change when change is as certain as constancy: online retail, the cloud, "probably electric vehicles", and pet medical insurance ("There's literally no loser in pet medical insurance"). ([Ben Graham Centre interview, Jan 2017](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/ben-graham-centre-interview-with-josh-tarasoff/))
- **The self-driving portfolio (2021).** **[said]** "I approach each investment with a permanent attitude, hoping and expecting the company to shoulder the work of compounding itself, ideally allowing Greenlea Lane to own it forever. Of course, I know it will not always work out that way, but what is important is the approach going in." He looks for positive feedback loops ("growth that is not self-reinforcing is inherently tenuous"), Quality (a mission-driven culture), and an "endgame": a state of affairs ten or more years out that rests on "overwhelming logic". He deliberately ignores the near-term "path". ([Essay about investing, July 2021](https://www.greenlealane.com/_files/ugd/25304a_e5930ca14e414cf59cabb0a32596c686.pdf))
- **Partners, duration, and the balance sheet (October 2023).** **[said]** Some recent Partners "have endured volatility without yet seeing sizable returns" and a few "are down from where they started." He has "all but stopped having introductory conversations with prospective investors"; any new Partners "will come from within its ecosystem." He prefers "balance-sheet" companies that reinvest over "income" companies that pay out, and notes "there are peculiarly few overcapitalized systems." ([Essay about impact and duration](https://www.greenlealane.com/_files/ugd/25304a_ed9e5e7f4c394cd8b3928672a780ec87.pdf))
- **Two kinds of conviction (March 2024).** **[said]** Explicit conviction comes from analysis and forecasts. Implicit conviction is trust in a company's people and culture. "My best investments have erred on the side of management and cultural excellence (implicit) when faced with disappointing fundamental performance or a seemingly stretched valuation (explicit)." ([Conviction and Quality](https://www.greenlealane.com/_files/ugd/25304a_8972cbb8d2034886b980ec0ca6f4b17d.pdf))
- **Direct and indirect (July 2024).** **[said]** He calls a large global asset manager that was "solely a principal investor" for decades before managing outside money "one of my favorite companies". The description fits Brookfield. He also praises a pet insurer that offers the most comprehensive coverage and sells through veterinarians, which fits Trupanion. He writes that investing should emphasize "potential over prediction". ([Direct and indirect](https://www.greenlealane.com/_files/ugd/25304a_248963ed1a394d69be38b02d1fc7b8e1.pdf)) The essay's disclaimer says the case studies "do not reflect actual investments". Both companies are in fact long-held 13F positions.
- **Implicit and explicit (February 2026).** **[said]** He argues that perceiving intangibles (desirability, "perfect" products, joyfulness) is an edge that AI-driven quantification will make rarer. The essay switches to "we": "We are increasingly focused on cultivating... implicit intelligence," through meditation and coaching. ([Implicit and explicit](https://www.greenlealane.com/_files/ugd/25304a_5b374d9b10774e2fba580207caf9c4fb.pdf)) Together with the December 2024 COO hire and the ADV's two advisory employees, this is the first sign the firm is more than one person. Nothing indicates a second portfolio manager.

**On selling,** the only on-record explanation is from 2017. It is quoted secondhand by Alpha-File, and I could not verify it against a transcript: "I would buy it and then later I thought I was really just compromising there and I would sell it." ([Alpha-File, July 2026](https://alpha-file.com/blog/greenlea-lane-the-anti-firm)) In the same interview he said he stopped giving talks and interviews because public commitment to recent ideas "causes a lot of portfolio turnover". That is why he says almost nothing about specific trades.

**What is not public:** the quarterly partner letters (the ADV says Partners receive them), performance after mid-2021, and any statement about a specific trade in 2023 to 2026, including the T-bill ETF.

## 2. The firm behind the filing, and why the 13F shows only part of it

**[filings]** The 13F aggregates three accounts run as one strategy (ADV and N-PX):

1. **Greenlea Lane Capital Partners, LP.** Gross assets $238.6M at 2025-12-31, 73 beneficial owners, 17% owned by Tarasoff and related persons, 15% non-US.
2. **Cassini Partners, L.P. – Greenlea Lane Series.** A series on an outside platform, gross assets $180.6M, only 2 beneficial owners. This is effectively one or two large investors.
3. **A pension-plan separately managed account.** Labelled "GREENLEA LANE SMA 238 PLAN" in N-PX, $42.7M, custodied at State Street. It shows up in N-PX from the Microsoft vote of December 2024 onward.

Regulatory AUM was **$461.9M at 2025-12-31**, against a 13F total of **$342.7M** the same day. About **$119M (26%) of the firm's assets is invisible to the 13F**. A secondary source puts the end-2023 RAUM at $366.1M against a 13F of $322.9M, a gap of about $43M (12%). **[inference, moderate confidence]** The invisible part is most likely Treasury bills and cash held directly (T-bills are not 13F securities), possibly with some non-US shares. The 13F has never shown a non-US-only listing, so I cannot confirm any. The gap grew while the 13F book was being sold down in 2024 (section 3), which fits sale proceeds being parked in T-bills.

Two things follow for a reader. First, **percentages in the 13F overstate position weights in the real portfolio** by roughly a quarter (at end-2025, Amazon was 21.3% of the 13F but about 16% of RAUM). Second, **a rising or falling 13F total says little about client flows**. Form D shows gross new money into the Partnership of only about $3M a year in 2023 to 2025, and he has said he is not seeking new investors. A single large Cassini investor or the pension SMA could move money in or out, and the 13F would show that only as unexplained buying or selling.

**Shape.** **[filings]** The book holds 8 to 10 lines, the top five make up 69 to 84% of it, and there is no leverage, no options and no shorts visible. Six names were held in all twelve quarters: Amazon, Brookfield, Berkshire, Markel, Alphabet and Trupanion. I call these the core. An outer ring of one to four names turns over: there were five full exits (monday.com, Shopify, Microsoft, Burford, Netflix) and five entries in the period (Berkshire in Q4 2023, Tesla, SGOV, Netflix, Spotify). The 13F total barely moved in three years ($323M to $348M), though most core names rose substantially. That is because he was a steady net seller of 13F stocks: roughly $160M of sales against $48M of purchases in 2024, and $51M against $38M in 2025. These figures are estimated from share changes at quarter-end prices.

**Before the period (context).** **[filings]** Between Q4 2022 and Q4 2023 he rotated out of a growth/software ring (Netflix, Salesforce, Spotify, and the Brookfield Asset Management spin-off) into Markel, Microsoft and, in Q4 2023, Berkshire Class A. Trupanion had been cut to 88,805 shares by end-2022 and was being rebuilt (301,470 by Q3 2023). In Q4 2023 he cut Alphabet by 40% (229,410 to 138,140 shares) and cut Markel by a third. The period therefore opens just after a deliberate tilt toward "balance-sheet" compounders. That tilt matches the October 2023 essay's praise of insurance and asset-management companies that reinvest and are "overcapitalized".

**Q2 2024: a clean swap.** **[filings]** He sold all of monday.com and Shopify (about $40M) and opened Tesla (116,600 shares, about $23M, owned by the April 2024 N-PX record date). He also nearly tripled Trupanion (425,070 to 1,100,000 shares, about $20M) after its fall. **[said]** Tesla fits his 2017 "probably electric vehicles" line. Trupanion is his longest-argued thesis: the "win-win" adoption case, and in July 2024 one of his "favorite companies". **[inference, moderate]** Adding hard to Trupanion after a collapse fits "erring on the side of management and cultural excellence" when fundamentals disappoint. He has done the same with Trupanion before.

**Q3 to Q4 2024: the big sell-down.** **[filings]** In Q3 2024 he sold about $75M and bought nothing: Amazon −23%, Brookfield −33%, Microsoft −56%, Tesla −17%. In Q4 2024 he sold about $40M more: the rest of Microsoft, plus 3 to 15% off nearly everything else (Tesla −14%, Trupanion −14%). **[inference, moderate]** This was not a fund-wide redemption. The cuts were uneven (Berkshire, Markel and Alphabet were barely touched) and firm AUM rose rather than fell. The proceeds most likely went into T-bills held directly, outside the 13F. Microsoft lasted about 1.5 years, which fits his "I was really just compromising" exit pattern. Why he cut Amazon and Brookfield that hard is not stated anywhere.

**2025: Burford is the one aggressive trade.** **[filings]** In Q2 2025 he more than quadrupled Burford Capital (790,405 to 3,362,322 shares, about $35M), taking it from 3.6% to 12.9% of the 13F. That was the largest single purchase in the period. In Q3 2025 he cut it 45% (about $16M of sales), held it in Q4, and in Q1 2026 sold it entirely. Burford's value hinged on the YPF judgment against Argentina. On 2026-03-27 the Second Circuit voided that judgment and Burford fell about 40% in a day ([Burford statement](https://investors.burfordcapital.com/news/news-details/2026/Burford-Capital-Statement-Re-YPF-Appeal-Decision/default.aspx)). **The 13F cannot tell whether he sold before or after the ruling.** Q1 2026 ends four days after it. **[inference, low]** The quick build-up and unwind looks event-driven, which is unusual for him. No source explains it. Treat Burford as the exception, not a template. Elsewhere in 2025 the activity was small trims (Amazon −10% in Q1, Trupanion −16% in Q2, Tesla −22% in Q4). Brookfield's apparent +48% in Q4 2025 is its 3-for-2 split, not a purchase.

**2026: cash becomes visible, and old names return.** **[filings]** In Q1 2026 SGOV appears ($38.6M, 11% of the 13F) alongside the Burford exit, a new Netflix stake ($26.9M) and small trims. Purchases in the 13F book exceeded sales by about $41M that quarter. **[inference, moderate]** So the SGOV purchase was largely funded from cash already held outside the 13F. It moved existing cash into a reportable wrapper rather than raising new cash from stocks. In Q2 2026 he opened Spotify ($18M) and trimmed Amazon (−10%), Markel (−10%) and Tesla (−13%). In Q3 2026 he sold all of Netflix, after two quarters in which its implied price fell about 26%. He cut Markel a further 29%, and SGOV grew to $69.5M (20%), its largest line. That quarter's stock sales (about $32M) almost exactly funded the SGOV addition (about $31M). **Netflix and Spotify are re-entries.** He held both from 2018 to 2022 and sold them in 2023. Netflix appears on his 2017 list of companies "systematically undervalued for decades". The Netflix round trip, two quarters, is the shortest hold in the record.

**Steady small trims.** **[filings]** Almost every quarter, share counts in Amazon, Alphabet, Berkshire and Tesla fall by 1 to 4%: Berkshire went from 71 to 63 Class A shares and Alphabet from 138,140 to 98,535. In three years he added to a core name only a few times: Markel in Q2 2024, Q1 2025 and Q4 2025, and Trupanion in Q2 2024 and Q4 2025. **[inference, moderate]** Small trims spread across names are probably housekeeping, such as funding fees, performance allocations, Partner withdrawals or rebalancing as winners grow. They are not changes of view.

## 4. How to read the next Greenlea 13F

**Usually noise:**
- Moves of 1 to 5% in share counts of core names. These are routine and recur almost every quarter.
- Value and weight changes in the core. With 8 to 10 lines, a 20% move in Amazon or Trupanion shifts weights several points with no trade. Check the share count before reading a weight change.
- Split artifacts. Brookfield's 3-for-2 split in October 2025 is the recent one. Check corporate actions before calling a share jump a buy.
- The total value. It mostly tracks prices and his own allocation between stocks and SGOV, and only partly tracks client flows.

**Meaningful:**
- **A new name.** He adds about one or two a year. Check first whether it is a former holding (2018 to 2022 names include KKR, Wayfair, Shake Shack, HubSpot, Salesforce, Slack and Spotify). Re-entries happen, and he has known these companies for years.
- **A full exit, or a cut of 25% or more, in one of the six core names.** That is rare and would be the strongest signal this filer gives. The Q3 2024 cuts to Amazon and Brookfield and the Q3 2026 Markel cut are the precedents, and he did not explain any of them. Do not assume a thesis break; he trimmed Amazon heavily and kept it as the top holding.
- **A large add after a price collapse.** He did this with Trupanion in 2023 and 2024 and with Burford in 2025. This is his most characteristic active move and signals conviction rising, not falling.
- **SGOV size.** Read changes together with that quarter's stock trades. If SGOV rose while stocks were sold by about the same amount (Q3 2026), he chose to hold more cash. If it rose while the 13F book was a net buyer (Q1 2026), cash probably moved from off-13F T-bills into the ETF, and his posture did not change. If stocks are sold pro rata across all names with no matching SGOV increase, suspect a client withdrawal.

**The T-bill ETF, specifically.** **[inference]** It is cash management (high confidence: it is a 0-3 month Treasury fund). It is not evidence of redemptions (moderate confidence: in Q3 2026 sales and SGOV purchases matched, and in Q1 2026 the 13F book was a net buyer). Whether it is dry powder or a defensive stance is unknown, because he has not said. His own framework points to dry powder rather than market timing: a self-driving portfolio lets him "be at my best during turbulent periods... able to make smart moves when exceptional opportunities arise." That is his description of the ideal, not a statement about this cash. Remember that the 13F's SGOV is only part of the firm's cash: at end-2025, before any SGOV, roughly $119M was already off-13F. A disclosed RAUM well above the 13F total plus SGOV would confirm that more cash, or foreign holdings, sits outside.

**Where the 13F misleads for this manager:**
- It omits a quarter or more of firm assets: directly held T-bills/cash and any non-US-only listings. Use the latest ADV RAUM (published each March for the prior year-end) to size the gap.
- It is quarter-end only, and he trades around events. Burford's exit could have happened before or after a 40% one-day drop. Never infer the exit price.
- Three accounts are blended. The annual N-PX (filed by August 31) shows the per-account split at proxy record dates and gives a few dated share counts between 13Fs. N-PX counts did not always match the 13F (Microsoft 2023, Netflix 2026), so treat them as approximate.
- Weights are of the 13F book, not the fund. Scale them down by about a quarter for true portfolio weight.
- **Not issues for this filer:** options, shorts, other included managers, confidential treatment, or PRN rows. None have appeared. There is one amendment, Q2 2025, a same-day CUSIP correction.

**Watch for:**
- A cut to Markel below about 5% or a full exit. It was cut 39% in 2026, and Markel announced a leadership change on 2026-09-08 (Steve Markel retiring as chairman). The timing is a coincidence until shown otherwise.
- Whether SGOV is deployed. A sharp fall in SGOV together with a new name or a big core add would be his "exceptional opportunity" move.
- Whether Spotify grows into a core position or follows Netflix out.
- Any new essay on greenlealane.com/public. These are his only public channel, and they name holdings only obliquely ("a large global asset manager", "a provider of insurance for dogs and cats").
- A second portfolio manager or a change in who signs the 13F. A COO arrived in December 2024, and the 2026 essay says "we".

## 5. Confidence and what would change my mind

The data in this profile is solid: all twelve quarters reconcile exactly to the cover pages. The philosophy is well sourced from his own essays. The weak point is motive. **There is no manager statement about any specific trade in the period.** Everything in section 3 tagged [inference] is my reading of share counts against his general principles. The biggest open questions:

1. **What the off-13F quarter of assets holds.** I assume T-bills/cash. A letter, or an ADV showing a big drop in RAUM once the cash was moved into SGOV, would settle it. If it turned out to be foreign equities, every weight above would be misleading in a different way.
2. **Whether 2024's net selling reflected his view or a client.** The uneven cuts argue it was his decision. A Cassini or SMA withdrawal in the same quarters would change that, and nothing public shows one.
3. **The reason for the Burford round trip, and for the 2026 Netflix entry and exit.** These are the least "Greenlea-like" trades in the record.

A future reader who gets one partner letter from 2024 to 2026 should check these three points against it before relying on this profile.
