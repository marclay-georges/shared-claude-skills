# Valley Forge Capital Management (Dev Kantesaria): investor profile and 13F baseline

- Filer: Valley Forge Capital Management, LP, Miami (moved from Wayne, PA during Q1 2024). CIK 0001697868, 13F file no. 028-17727.
- People: Devang ("Dev") Kantesaria, founder, sole portfolio manager and controlling owner. The firm had 9 employees, 2 in investment roles, at the March 2026 ADV. There is no co-PM or named successor, so the strategy is one person's judgment.
- Period covered: 13F-HR for Q4 2023 through Q3 2026 (twelve quarters, all originals, no amendments). Also read: three N-PX filings, Form ADV Part 1 and 2A (March 2026), Form D amendments for Valley Forge Capital, L.P., and Kantesaria's public interviews from 2019 to 2024.
- Written 2026-11-19. Quarterly holdings are in `holdings/`; sources and problems are in `log.md`.

## Who he is and what he says he is doing

Kantesaria trained at MIT and Harvard Medical School, spent two years at McKinsey and about 18 years in biotech venture capital, and launched Valley Forge in July 2007. He calls his method the Buffett–Munger playbook, applied narrowly: own the 8 to 12 best businesses from a "fishing pond" of about 40 to 50 companies worldwide, hold them for a decade or more, and stay fully invested. His own definition of quality is "finding the perfect intersection between growth and predictability" ([TIP, Dec 2024](https://www.theinvestorspodcast.com/episodes/investing-in-exceptional-businesses-for-the-long-run-dev-kantesaria/); transcript read via [Exa's copy](https://exa.ai/library/podcast/9n0n4fg4c6z/episode/d84mjqxkgsk)). He wants monopolies or "natural" duopolies with pricing power, low capital needs, little R&D risk, and buybacks rather than acquisitions. "Pricing power is the hallmark of a great business" ([Good Investing Talks, recorded 2022](https://www.good-investing.net/2023/01/26/dev-kantesaria-what-is-your-formula-for-quality-investing/)). He says about one third of his entries come from a sell-off he thinks the market has misread, and two thirds from "market neglect", where a change in pricing power goes unnoticed. FICO in 2018 is his standard example of the second kind.

Several of his stated views matter directly for reading his filings:

- **Cash.** "Today... cash sits around 1% for our fund. It'll probably sit around zero to 5%... if you own a great business, you should be fully invested" ([Grant's Current Yield, 2024-11-20](https://grantpub.libsyn.com/growth-and-profitability); [transcript](https://exa.ai/library/podcast/36jbmdd91r3/episode/920ykxmrzfb)). With almost no cash buffer, money that leaves the fund has to come out of the stocks, and that shows up in the 13F.
- **Selling.** He says low activity is not complacency: "we're vicious in how we think about companies. There's no emotional attachment" (TIP, Dec 2024). The sell triggers he has described are full valuation, declining long-term quality, poor capital allocation (above all acquisitions that dilute a great core business), or a better opportunity elsewhere ([VII, Oct 2019](https://valueinvestorinsight.com/Content_Free/investor-insight-standing-out-october-2019.aspx), where he explains selling Equifax to buy FICO). He prefers businesses whose quality would decline gradually, so that he can exit in time.
- **AI and Big Tech (late 2024). This is the most important baseline for reading 2026.** In November 2024 he said: "We could have bought NVIDIA, we could have bought Microsoft, Google, or even TSMC, but we wanted the most predictable way to participate in AI. And we felt that was ASML." He rejected TSMC because its advantage "comes from really scale. And that is not a[n] advantage that we prefer" (Grant's). In December 2024 he said: "it's our view that AI becomes commoditized... I think that party continues for a few years, but I think it will have a bad ending for most people" (TIP). On his own holdings he expected AI to help them, saying margins at labour-heavy firms "will be far greater than we could ever imagine," and he rejected AI displacing FICO, Moody's or S&P because their value is a network-effect "stamp of approval" (Grant's). He also expected AI to cause "massive unemployment" and admitted that this is a risk for Visa and Mastercard volumes.
- **US large caps.** In December 2024 he said only 5 to 7 names on his 50–60 company shortlist are foreign. The best business models, in his view, are almost all in the US.
- **Fees and structure.** He charges 1% management and a 20% performance allocation with a high-water mark ([ADV 2A, March 2026](https://files.adviserinfo.sec.gov/IAPD/Content/Common/crd_iapd_Brochure.aspx?BRCHR_VRSN_ID=1032470)). The ADV permits shorts, options and warrants. No option or short activity is visible, and Jim Grant called the fund "apparently unhedged".

## Who owns the money, and why it matters for flows

ADV Part 1 (filed 2026-03-30, data at 2025-12-31) shows $4.45B of regulatory AUM in four pooled vehicles. The 13F on that date showed $4.41B, so almost everything he manages is visible in the 13F.

- **BBH Valley Forge Master Fund, LP** (Cayman) held $2.61B gross, about 59% of AUM. Its feeders are Brown Brothers Harriman wealth-platform vehicles. The fund had 1,926 beneficial owners, a $100K minimum, and 5% owned by Valley Forge and related persons. In the March 2024 ADV it held $2.1B with 1,590 owners ([PPLI summary of that filing](https://ppli.com/hedge-funds/bbh-valley-forge-master-fund/), secondary). This is the "Sub-Advised Fund" for "a large financial services institution" described in the brochure.
- **Valley Forge Capital, L.P.** (the flagship) held $1.83B gross, with 104 owners in the ADV and 147 in the July 2026 Form D, a $5M minimum, and 15% owned by Kantesaria and related persons.
- **Valley Forge Capital Balanced L.P.** held $10M, all owned by insiders.

Most of the capital therefore sits with about 1,900 private-bank clients on a BBH platform with a $100K minimum, not with a few large endowments. My inference, at medium confidence: money on a wealth-platform feeder is more likely to move with the platform's allocation decisions and with clients' reactions to a bad year than money in the flagship. A fund-wide outflow can therefore appear quickly after a period of underperformance.

The flagship's Form D reports cumulative gross subscriptions. They rose by $47M (Aug 2023 to Jul 2024), $131M (to Jul 2025) and $220M (to Jul 2026) ([Form D/A 2026-07-14](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1473337/000147333726000001/0001473337-26-000001.txt)). Form D does not net out redemptions. Even so, new money was still entering the flagship through mid-2026 while the 13F book was shrinking. If redemptions drove the 2026 selling, they came net of those inflows, or mostly through the BBH vehicle. I cannot separate the two from public data. The next ADV, due around March 2027 with 2026-12-31 data, will show the BBH master's gross assets and owner count, and that is the check.

**2024, a fully invested and nearly static book.** The top five names (FICO, S&P, Mastercard, Moody's, Visa) were about 90% of value in every quarter until Q3 2026. All of them predate this period by years (FICO was bought around 2018, after he sold Equifax, per VII 2019). My estimate of one-way turnover is 0 to 3% a quarter. The only steady activity was selling FICO into its rally, every quarter of 2024: 903,587 shares fell to 784,122 (−13%) while the implied price went from about $1,164 to $1,991. The weight still rose from 30% to 35%. He told TIP in December 2024 he felt "a bit guilty" about FICO doubling two years running. The trims look like weight control on a winner, not a change of view (inference, high confidence). In Q3 2024 he doubled ASML (62,903 to 125,627 shares) and added about 6% to Moody's and 3% to S&P. In Q4 2024 he added ~3% to Moody's and S&P again, opened small (~0.8%) positions in Equifax and MSCI, cut Intuit 10%, and exited Aspen Technology. The Aspen exit followed Emerson's $240/share take-private proposal of 2024-11-05 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/deals/emerson-proposes-acquire-remaining-shares-aspentech-2024-11-05/)). My inference is that he sold into the bid rather than wait for a cash-out. He did not say so.

**2025, quiet trimming and a lost year.** Intuit was cut in five consecutive quarters (−26% in Q1 2025 alone) until it was a 3% position. FICO was trimmed 2% in Q1 2025 and then held. Nothing was bought apart from Q4 2025. In Q4 2025 every holding rose by a small, similar amount (+0.2% to +0.5%; EFX and MSCI about +1.6%) while Intuit was cut 15%. That fingerprint looks like a subscription spread pro rata across the book, not a decision about any one name (inference, high confidence). A secondary source, [Alpha-File, 2026-08-11](https://alpha-file.com/blog/valley-forge-capital-the-monopoly), reports fund performance of −2.8% in 2025 against roughly +18% for the S&P 500. It does not say where that figure comes from, so treat it as unverified.

**Q1 2026, the "AI scare" quarter.** The 13F book fell 23%, almost all from price: about −20% from price moves against about −3% from net selling. In early February 2026 a sell-off triggered by Anthropic's Claude Cowork plug-ins hit software and data providers. On 2026-02-03 S&P Global had its worst day since March 2020 (−11%), Moody's fell −8.9% and Equifax −12% ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/stock-market-today-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-02-03-2026/card/C97KvtPTsT9H3joFwS7C)). S&P then guided 2026 below consensus on 2026-02-10 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/sp-global-forecasts-2026-profit-below-estimates-shares-plunge-2026-02-10/)). In January 2026 Trump called for a 10% credit-card rate cap and endorsed the Credit Card Competition Act, and Visa and Mastercard fell 4–5% on 2026-01-13 ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/visa-and-mastercard-stock-falls-as-trump-doubles-down-on-fee-crackdown-4445207)). These were exactly the risks Kantesaria had dismissed or downplayed in late 2024. His moves in the quarter:
- exited Equifax and MSCI, both before February record dates, since neither appears in the 2026 N-PX;
- cut Visa 35% but left Mastercard untouched;
- cut Intuit 15%;
- added 36% to ASML;
- made trims of about 100 shares each in FICO, Moody's and S&P, which are noise and probably a pro-rata redemption.

Alpha-File (secondary) reports the fund at −23% for the quarter.

**Q2 2026, all sells, no buys.** Net selling was about $465M, roughly 14% of the starting book, even though prices rose about 6%. Mastercard was cut 28%, S&P 13%, FICO 13% and Visa 23%. Moody's and ASML were untouched. The N-PX adds timing. The Mastercard vote (meeting 2026-06-16, record date about mid-April) was cast on 1,282,808 shares. So about 205k of the 422k Mastercard shares sold that quarter had gone by mid-April, and the rest by June 30. The S&P vote (record date about late March) was on the full 1,758,074 shares, so all the S&P selling came after that. On 2026-04-22 Fannie and Freddie began accepting VantageScore 4.0 and FICO fell 13% ([Reuters via ET](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/us-stocks/news/us-stocks-fico-shares-slide-as-freddie-mac-fannie-mae-begin-accepting-rival-vantagescore/articleshow/130450555.cms)). The same quarter FICO was cut 13%, the first real FICO sale since early 2025. Commentator Joseph Carlson read the quarter as redemption-driven ([summary](https://reducer.xgoose.org/items/4167)). That is a reasonable inference, because a near-zero-cash fund selling only names it still rates, with no new buys, looks like meeting withdrawals. It is not confirmed.

**Q3 2026, the break with his own stated framework.** This is the largest repositioning in the twelve quarters and, as far as I can tell from the public record, in the fund's 13F history (estimated one-way turnover ~22%).
- **Bought:** new positions in Amazon (7.0%; a re-entry: he "owned a little bit of Amazon" (Grant's, 2024) and sold it in Q1 2023 per secondary 13F trackers), Broadcom (6.6%), Nvidia (5.8%) and TSMC ADRs (5.4%). He added 22% to ASML, which became a 14.9% position.
- **Sold:** Mastercard cut 84% (from 17.6% to 3.7%, about $494M at quarter-end prices, enough to fund most of the new buys). Moody's cut 31% (its first sale in the period), Visa 49%, S&P 9% and FICO 5%. Intuit was exited after eight quarters of steady selling.
- **Net:** still about −$170M, so outflows probably continued alongside the rotation.

Semiconductors and Amazon were about 40% of the book at September 30, against 1.4% (ASML only) at the start of the period. In November 2024 he named Nvidia and TSMC as the less predictable AI exposures he had deliberately passed over, and he had called AI's likely ending "bad... for most people." **I found no public statement from Kantesaria explaining the change.** I searched for 2025–2026 interviews, letters and conference appearances and found none. Every explanation circulating (for example "Amazon automation", "TSMC geopolitical risk", "monetizing Mastercard near highs") comes from YouTube commentators and 13F aggregators, not from him.

The best-supported reading I can offer (inference, medium-low confidence) has two parts:
- After his labour-heavy data and rating franchises were repriced as possible AI losers, and his payment networks as regulatory and stablecoin targets, he changed his mind about where predictability lies in an AI world. He moved from businesses that AI might threaten toward the physical bottlenecks AI spending must pass through. That fits the "picks and shovels" logic he applied to ASML in 2024, now extended to names he previously rejected as scale-based.
- What would change my mind is a letter or interview giving a different reason, for example client mandate pressure or a view specific to Mastercard. Separately, the Broadcom and Nvidia buys predate a sharp chip pullback after September 30. Aggregators show Nvidia about 33% below its Q3-end price by mid-November ([Valuesider](https://valuesider.com/guru/dev-kantesaria-valley-forge-capital-management/buys-sells)).

**FICO in Q3 2026.** The ~50% fall in value on almost unchanged shares is a price move, not a sale. On 2026-09-03/04 the FHFA director opened VantageScore to all GSE lenders, posting "FICO has enjoyed a monopoly. No more," and FICO fell 17% ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/is-fair-isaac-stock-a-buy-after-its-17-mortgage-score-selloff)). On 2026-09-28/29 Fannie and Freddie moved to a single pricing grid with VantageScore, TransUnion locked VantageScore at $0.99 through 2028, Rocket Mortgage made VantageScore its default, and FICO fell 26.5% in a day to $617.87 ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/29/why-fair-isaac-corporation-plunged-today/); [Kalkine](https://kalkine.com/news/financial/why-did-fair-isaac-nysefico-stock-plunge-as-fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac-move-to-one-pricing-grid)). Both events strike at the mortgage-score pricing power that was his whole FICO thesis ("On a $400,000 mortgage, the FICO score may cost the lender less than $1. We don't think it's unreasonable to assume FICO could charge a multiple of that", VII 2019). He sold only 5% of his FICO shares in Q3. My inference: much of that sale may have come before the September shocks, since the grid announcement came one trading day before quarter-end and the 13F cannot show trade dates. After quarter-end FICO launched a $10 bundled "Preferred Program", paused buybacks and gave no FY2027 guidance ([Yahoo, 2026-10-26](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/why-fair-isaac-corporation-fico-010247962.html); [TradingView, 2026-11-05](https://www.tradingview.com/news/stockstory:98a133676094b:0-fico-q3-cy2026-deep-dive-margin-compression-and-pricing-strategy-transition-overshadow-revenue-growth/)). Whether he holds, adds or exits FICO in Q4 2026 is the cleanest test available of whether he thinks its long-term quality has changed.

## How he votes (N-PX): a useful tell on management

He votes only on say-on-pay. Across 2024–2026 he voted **AGAINST** pay every year at FICO and Intuit, at Moody's in 2024 and 2025, and at MSCI in 2025. He voted FOR at S&P Global, Visa, Mastercard, ASML, Aspen and Equifax. Moody's switched to FOR in 2026. This matches his stated practice of netting stock compensation out of free cash flow, and shows he holds companies whose pay he dislikes when the franchise is strong enough (FICO). Intuit, with a vote against every year and steady selling from Q4 2024, is the one case where both signals pointed the same way. My inference is that he lost patience with its capital allocation and compensation before the AI threat to software. He did not say so. Absent votes are also informative: no N-PX vote implies the position was gone by the record date.

## How to read Valley Forge's next 13F

**Treat any change in a core name as meaningful, but first ask whether it is a flow.** With 8–10 positions, about 1% cash and a stated dislike of trading, he has no small moves. Before reading a trim as a change of view, check three things.
- Do all names move by a similar percentage? That is a pro-rata subscription or redemption: compare Q4 2025's uniform +0.2–0.5% and Q1 2026's −100-share trims.
- Are there sells with no buys while prices rose? That is probably withdrawals, as in Q2 2026.
- Did one name grow past ~33%? That gives weight-control trims, as with FICO in 2024.

A move that breaks these patterns is a decision. Examples are a cut in one name while a peer is untouched (Visa −35% and Mastercard 0 in Q1 2026), money moving from one holding into another, or any new name.

**New names are rare and deliberate.** Before Q3 2026 he made roughly one or two changes a year. Small starter positions (~0.8%, Equifax and MSCI) are trials and can be dropped within about a year without saying much. A position started at 5–7% is a conviction bet.

**Price explains most quarter-to-quarter value changes.** From 2023 to 2025 the price effect was usually 5 to 20 times the trading effect. Always split the change in value into the share change and the price change. FICO's Q3 2026 halving is pure price.

**Watch the regime question above all.** The Q3 2026 filing either marks a lasting widening of his "fishing pond" into AI hardware and hyperscalers, or a one-off reaction under performance and redemption pressure. Readings that would distinguish the two:
- Do Nvidia, Broadcom, TSMC and Amazon get added to or kept through a drawdown? That signals conviction. Small or exited positions within a few quarters signal an experiment that failed his predictability test.
- Do Mastercard and Visa go to zero (a thesis break on payments) or stabilise as small residuals?
- Does he add to FICO, Moody's or S&P after their declines? He said in 2024 that he buys these toll roads on cyclical or headline weakness. Not adding would mean his view of their quality has changed.

**Where the 13F misleads for this manager.**
- *Non-US holdings.* ASML is reported as the NY-registry shares and TSMC as the ADR. If he ever owns a foreign line with no US listing, it will not show. He said 5–7 shortlist names are foreign, and the master fund is Cayman-domiciled. I found no evidence of current non-13F holdings.
- *Cash, shorts and options.* The ADV permits them. None have appeared, and his stated cash is 0–5%. If 13F value falls much more than price explains while AUM in the ADV holds up, suspect cash, not a non-13F position.
- *Trade timing.* The 45-day lag is costly here because his moves are large and concentrated. N-PX share counts at record dates sometimes give an intra-quarter checkpoint, as with Mastercard on about 2026-04-15.
- *Fund-level flows.* These are invisible in the 13F. Use the ADV (annual, around March 30) and the flagship's Form D (annual, July; gross sales only). The BBH sub-advised vehicle is the larger pool and the more likely source of large swings.
- *Filing quirks, all harmless.* All rows are SH and SOLE, with no other managers and no confidential treatment in any quarter. The cover-page totals matched the summed rows in every quarter, apart from $1 rounding in Q2 2024 and Q2 2025. From Q2 2026 the signer's title is "Manager of the General Partner" instead of "Managing Partner", and the issuer name changed to "ASML HLDG NV". This looks like a filing-agent change, also seen in the 2026 N-PX, and is not a change of ownership; the ADV still shows Kantesaria in control.

**What a future reader should hold loosely.** I have no Valley Forge letter from this period and no 2025–2026 interview. Every reason given for 2026 trades is my inference or a commentator's. The performance figures (−2.8% in 2025, −23% in Q1 2026) are secondary and unverified. If a letter surfaces, it should replace the inferences in the Q1–Q3 2026 paragraphs.
