  - Anthony Massaro: investment partner, spoke on the Q3 2025 call.
  - The ADV (amended 2026-06-03) reports 48 employees, 11 of them in investment roles.

- **Written:** 2026-11-19. Sources and accession numbers are in `log.md`; quarterly holdings are in `holdings/`.

## 1. Before you open the next filing: four things that will otherwise fool you

**The filer changed and the filing now has a non-portfolio row worth a third of its value.**
- From Q2 2026 the 13F-HR comes from Pershing Square Inc. PS Inc. is the NYSE-listed management company that IPO'd on 2026-04-29/30 alongside the closed-end fund Pershing Square USA (PSUS).
- The old PSCM CIK now files only a 13F-NT pointing to PS Inc. Its two notices so far say "Holdings of this reporting manager are now included in the report of its public parent company".
- PS Inc.'s report lists six "other included managers", and the rows are tagged with them:
  1. PSCM
  2. PSCM GP
  3. Pershing Square Partner Group
  4. Pershing Square Management
  5. PSUS Holdings
  6. PS HHH Holdings
- Three rows are not fund investments at all:
  - **Pershing Square Inc. common stock** (CUSIP 71531U102, 184,289,699 shares, $10.3B at Q3 2026). These are PS Inc. shares owned by the management/insider vehicles (other managers 3 and 4), not something Pershing's funds bought. Media read its first appearance as a giant "new buy". It is not one.
  - **9,000,000 Howard Hughes shares** held by PS Inc.'s own subsidiary, bought with corporate capital in May 2025.
  - **4,000,000 PSUS shares**, PS Inc.'s $200M seed of the new fund.
- Strip the PS Inc. row before computing weights or totals. The like-for-like fund book at Q3 2026 is about $20.5B, not the $30.8B cover total. The CSVs keep percentages against the filed total so they match the raw filing, so recompute ex-self. Section 4 has a table.

**The 13F is a subset of what Pershing owns, and the omitted part is not small.**
- Over these three years the largest things missing from the 13F were:
  - Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac (OTC, a multi-year special situation that added about 11 points to PSH's 2025 return);
  - Universal Music Group (Euronext Amsterdam, held 2021 to mid-2026);
  - the Nike position after early 2025, when it was converted to deep-in-the-money call options;
  - most of the Hertz stake, which was largely total return swaps;
  - interest-rate swaptions and other hedges;
  - since 2026, equity forward contracts used by PSUS.
- A name can vanish from the 13F while Pershing still owns it economically. A share count can fall without a sale. A 2–3% "asymmetric" bet can be expressed entirely in derivatives and never appear.

**Ackman tells you first.** He announces most big new positions and exits himself (X posts, the PSH annual and interim reports, quarterly calls), usually before or around the 13F. By the time a 13F lands, check the most recent PSH/PS Inc. letter and Ackman's X feed. The 13F is usually confirmation, not news. When the 13F and his statements disagree, the statements are usually describing the economic position and the 13F a legal subset of it.

**Howard Hughes is a control investment, not a trade.**
- Combined ownership is 46.7–46.9%.
- Ackman is Executive Chairman, PSCM gets fees under a services agreement, and there is a 47% ownership cap and a 40% voting cap.
- The fund-held block of 18,852,064 shares has not changed in twelve quarters.
- Read HHH changes from the Schedule 13D/As, which are long and specific, not from the 13F.

## 2. Who Ackman is and what he says he is trying to do

Ackman runs a concentrated, long-only-in-spirit book of large North American businesses. He holds them for years and is occasionally activist, with hedges kept off to the side.

**Said.** The 2023 annual report (March 2024) is the cleanest statement of the method:
- eight to twelve core holdings, "simple, predictable, free-cash-flow generative" large caps;
- a long holding period;
- the view that "Frenetic investment activity is often the enemy of long-term performance."
- He also argues that his edge is not speed or information. The 2024 annual report says that what hedge funds win with ("access to information seconds and even milliseconds before others, and extraordinary trading capabilities") is "not present or relevant in our approach to investing". He welcomes volatility because it "occasionally leads to a high degree of undervaluation for even the largest companies."
- Sources: [2023 AR](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2024/03/22201541/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2023-Annual-Report.pdf), [2024 AR](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2025/03/14150847/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2024-Annual-Report.pdf).

**Said, and it matters for reading the book now.** By the Q3 2026 PS Inc. letter (November 2026) the framing has shifted:
- the funds are described as "holding companies" with "12 to 15 subsidiaries";
- Fannie/Freddie and Howard Hughes are called special situations;
- asymmetric bets are made mostly through "options, credit default swaps or similar instruments";
- 2026 brought seven new investments year-to-date, "about three times our typical average", with an eighth being bought after quarter end.
- Fee-paying AUM was about $23.0B, 98% of it permanent capital (PSH, PSUS).
- Source: [PS Inc. Q3 2026 letter](https://www.pershingsquareinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Q3-26-PS-Inc-Letter-to-Shareholders.pdf).

**Filings (ADV, amended 2026-06-03).**
- Regulatory AUM was $25.06B across 5 discretionary accounts, about $18.2B of it attributable to non-US clients.
- The Part 2A brochure (June 3, 2026, [IAPD](https://files.adviserinfo.sec.gov/IAPD/Content/Common/crd_iapd_Brochure.aspx?BRCHR_VRSN_ID=1047158)) gives two different targets: "typically 8 to 12 core investments" for PSLP, Pershing Square International and PSH, and "12 to 15 core investments" for PSUS, because a 1940-Act fund has diversification rules. So the rise in 13F names after the IPO is largely structural (the combined filing includes PSUS); it is not only a change of style.
- Also from Part 2A: each fund's target size includes "equity forwards and other derivatives that provide similar exposure as direct equity investments" (delta-one exposure), and "it is likely that investments may be held in different proportions between PSUS and the other Core Funds". The funds "generally do not use total return swaps to obtain leverage, but rather to manage regulatory, tax, legal or other issues". Counting HHH, total AUM is $33.7B.
- N-PX for 2024, 2025 and 2026 (filings): Pershing votes FOR management's say-on-pay almost everywhere. The one AGAINST was Nike's say-on-pay in September 2024, while the turnaround stake was being built. In 2026 it voted against a Meta shareholder proposal for annual pay votes, siding with management. Pershing Square Inc.'s own 9M HHH shares were voted partly in proportion to other holders, as the 40% voting cap requires. In short, Pershing does not use proxy votes to make a point; its activism happens privately or through 13D/As.
- Private-fund gross assets: PSH $17.9B, Pershing Square International $0.35B, Pershing Square L.P. $1.13B, PS Redemption L.P. $25M.
- PS Redemption L.P. was created for a special redemption of Pershing Square L.P. holders on 2026-04-21. The fifth account is presumably PSUS.

**My read (high confidence).** PSH, a permanent-capital closed-end vehicle, has dominated, and since April 2026 PSUS is a second permanent pool. So "fund-wide outflows" are rarely the explanation for a sale here. Redemptions are small and the vehicles do not shrink with investor sentiment. Inflows are a different story; see the PSUS point in section 5.

## 3. The story, in the order it happened

Read this forward. The pattern that matters is how a stated philosophy of "few changes" became, by 2026, the busiest repositioning of Ackman's recent career.

**Q4 2023 baseline (13F filed 2024-02-14).**
- Filings: eight rows, $10.4B, seven businesses: Alphabet 19%, Chipotle 18%, Restaurant Brands 18%, Hilton 16%, Howard Hughes 16%, Canadian Pacific 11%, Lowe's 3%.
- Said: in the 2023 annual report Ackman said they "added one company... Alphabet, and we exited one, Lowe's". The Lowe's exit was on near-term earnings uncertainty, after a roughly 175% gain. The Lowe's remnant (3%) was gone by Q1 2024.
- From prior knowledge, not re-verified here: QSR traces to the 2012 Burger King investment, HHH to the 2010 General Growth spin, Chipotle to 2016, Hilton to 2018 and CP to 2021. So the core was genuinely years old.

**H1 2024: new names.**
- Filings: Q2 2024 shows Brookfield (6.9M shares) and Nike (3.0M) as new. By Q3 2024 both were full-size (BN 13%, NKE 11%). Chipotle's 50-for-1 split took 743,984 shares to 28,815,165.
- Said: the [H1 2024 letter](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4716465-pershing-square-holdings-h1-2024-letter-to-shareholders) introduced Nike as a turnaround under new CEO Elliott Hill and Brookfield as "a leading alternative asset manager with high-quality and rapidly growing cash flows". The 2024 AR adds that BN was bought "at 11 times distributable earnings" and that Ackman had worked with Bruce Flatt on General Growth Properties.
- Corporate events: in June 2024 Pershing sold 10% of the management company to strategic investors for $1.05B ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240603875090/en/Pershing-Square-Capital-Management-L.P.-Announces-Sale-of-10-Common-Equity-Interest-for-1.05-Billion-to-Strategic-Investors)). The first attempt to IPO a US closed-end fund was pulled in July 2024. Howard Hughes spun off Seaport Entertainment on 2024-08-01, and Pershing backstopped the $175M rights offering. That is why Seaport rights and then about 5.0M Seaport shares appear from Q3 2024 on: an artifact of the HHH relationship, not a stock pick.

**Q4 2024: the confidential position.**
- Filings: the original Q4 2024 13F (filed 2025-02-14) was flagged as having confidential information omitted. A 13F-HR/A filed 2025-04-16 revealed Hertz, 12.7M shares ($46.5M).
- Said: Ackman then posted that Pershing had a 19.8% stake "comprised of outright share ownership and total return swaps" ([Bloomberg 2025-04-17](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-17/ackman-says-pershing-has-amassed-a-19-8-stake-in-hertz), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/04/16/-hertz-surges-after-bill-ackman-takes-big-stake-in-the-rental-car-firm.html)).
- Lesson: a confidential-treatment flag on the cover page means a position is being built, and the amendment shows only the share leg.

**Early 2025: Uber, the Nike conversion, and the first sign of a different pace.**
- Filings: Q1 2025 shows Uber as the largest position at once (30.3M shares, 19%). Nike disappears from the 13F entirely.
- Said, from the 2024 AR, March 2025:
  - Uber was bought in early 2025 when "concerns regarding a perceived long-term threat from autonomous vehicles" depressed the price.
  - Nike was "converted... to deep in-the-money call options. This structure allows us to maintain exposure... while unlocking capital for new investments."
- So Nike's disappearance from the 13F was not an exit. Nike options are not 13F-reportable in the way they were held, or at least they do not appear.

**Q1–Q2 2025: CP out, Amazon in, Hertz in, Holdco buys HHH.**
- Filings: Canadian Pacific is in Q1 2025 and gone by Q2. Amazon appears in Q2 (5.8M shares). Hertz is visible at 15.2M shares, a fraction of the 19.8% economic stake.
- Said: CP was sold "with regret" over tariff and economic sensitivity, to fund Amazon, bought from late April 2025 at about 25x earnings ([Reuters 2025-05-22](https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/sustainable-finance-reporting/ackmans-hedge-fund-pershing-square-bets-amazon-exits-canadian-pacific-2025-05-22/)).
- Filings, HHH: Holdco bought 9M newly issued Howard Hughes shares at $100 ($900M) on 2025-05-05. The HHH 13D/A Amendment 29, Item 4, lays out the services agreement ($3.75M a quarter plus a variable fee), the 47% ownership cap and the 40% voting cap. This is what led to Holdco/PS Inc. filing its own one-row 13F "combination reports" from Q2 2025, which I fold into the quarterly CSVs.
- Earlier, the January 2025 HHH proposal (13D/A exhibit 99.22) had floated an $85/share deal to make HHH a "modern-day Berkshire Hathaway". That was replaced by the 2025 equity-investment structure.

**H2 2025: Chipotle and Nike exited as mistakes; Meta bought.**
- Filings: Chipotle falls from 28.8M shares (Q3 2024) to 21.5M (Q1–Q3 2025) and is gone in Q4 2025. Meta appears in Q4 2025 (2.67M shares, 11%).
- Said, on the [Q3 2025 call](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4846231-pershing-square-holdings-ltd-pshzf-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript), Massaro and Ackman:
  - Chipotle was sold after its Q3 report at roughly a 22% IRR, about $2.4B of profit. The lesson drawn: "with a high-multiple stock, if kind of the trends slow for any reason, it's better to exit faster."
  - The Nike options were exited at about a 30% loss (over $600M). The lesson: demand higher hurdle rates for turnarounds.
  - Meta was bought after the October 2025 capex selloff, at about 20x earnings.
- Also in Q4 2025, Alphabet class A fell from 4.84M to 0.68M shares. Total Alphabet weight fell from 18% to 13%.

**Q1 2026: Hilton out, Microsoft in, Alphabet nearly gone.**
- Filings: Hilton (3.0M shares) is gone in Q1 2026. Microsoft appears at 5.65M shares (15%). Alphabet is down to about 1%.
- Said:
  - Hilton was exited in February 2026 on valuation: the multiple had gone from about 20x to 32x, and "prospective returns are unlikely to meet our high return threshold" ([2025 AR](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/18175039/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2025-Annual-Report.pdf); [Reuters 2026-02-11](https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/sustainable-finance-reporting/ackmans-hedge-fund-pershing-square-bets-meta-exits-hilton-2026-02-11/)).
  - On Alphabet, Ackman wrote on X on 2026-05-16: "our sale of $GOOG was not a bet against the company... in light of our finite capital base, we used $GOOG as a source of funds for $MSFT" ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/bill-ackman-says-alphabet-stake-sale-not-bet-against-company-2026-05-16/)).
  - The 2026 investor presentation framed Amazon, Microsoft and (formerly) Alphabet as "the three" hyperscalers.

**April 2026: the IPO, and the structure you now read the 13F through.**
- PS Inc. and PSUS listed on the NYSE together, raising about $5B ([Reuters 2026-04-29](https://www.reuters.com/business/bill-ackmans-pershing-square-raises-5-billion-new-us-fund-2026-04-29/)).
- Per the 424B4, management held about 64% of PS Inc. and the management vehicle held about 75% of the vote at IPO.
- PSUS deployed its capital quickly but not all at once, and not all in shares. Its first N-PORT and semi-annual N-CSRS (as of 2026-06-30) show:
  - 85.9% economic exposure to portfolio companies, with about 49% of net assets still in money-market funds and T-bills. Part of that was collateral.
  - $1.59B notional of equity forwards (Nomura, UBS; expiring 2029) on the legacy names: Microsoft 972,500 shares, Meta 538,960, RBI 3,180,478, Uber 4,493,000 and Brookfield 5,943,000.
  - The new names (Visa, Mastercard, S&P Global, Netflix) bought outright.
  - Microsoft is the clearest case: PSUS held 549,096 MSFT shares directly but had 1,521,596 shares of exposure. The 13F shows only the first number.
  - By 2026-09-30 exposure was 97.8% (PSUS Q3 summary).
- Filings: the RBI 13D/A Amendment 5 (event 2026-05-06) added PSUS as a reporting person. It shows PSUS holding 1.86M RBI shares plus **2,652,778 shares under forward purchase contracts**. This is concrete evidence that part of the new fund's exposure is in forwards the 13F does not show.
- Universal Music: Pershing bid for UMG in April 2026 (about $64B, rejected in June), then sold its remaining stake. MBW reported about 80.6M shares, including a €250M buyback by UMG ([MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/bill-ackman-who-called-universal-music-one-of-the-best-businesses-we-have-ever-owned-steers-clear-of-music-in-pershing-square-stock-buying-splurge/), secondary). None of this ever touched a 13F.

**Q2 2026: five new names in one quarter.**
- Filings: PS Inc.'s first full 13F (filed 2026-08-14) adds Visa, Mastercard, S&P Global and Netflix. Positions grow broadly with the PSUS money.
- A 13F-HR/A filed 2026-11-16, three months later, added the 184.3M-share PS Inc. self-stake row.

## 5. How to read the next filing

**Normalise first.** Drop CUSIP 71531U102 (PS Inc.) entirely, because it is management's own stock. Treat the 9.0M-share HHH row and the 4.0M PSUS row as PS Inc. corporate capital rather than fund trades. These have been static. A change there is corporate news, so look for an 8-K or 13D/A.

**A new name is signal, and it will probably already be public.**
- Ackman pre-announces. If a new name appears without a prior statement, check the latest PS Inc./PSUS letter and the PSH monthly report.
- If there is truly no statement, it may be the early stage of a build, which is often hidden by confidential treatment, as with Hertz in Q4 2024.
- New positions from Pershing are usually sized at 5–10%+ at once, not starter stakes. A sub-2% new row is either a partial reveal or an "asymmetric" bet held mostly in derivatives.

**A full exit is signal, and the reason usually belongs to one of three types.** Look for the stated reason in the next letter. Pershing reliably gives one, often with a post-mortem.
- Valuation reached: Hilton, Lowe's.
- Thesis broke: Chipotle, Nike, Hertz.
- Source of funds for a better idea: CP to Amazon, Alphabet to Microsoft.
- My read (high confidence): the "source of funds" type is common here because Ackman runs a fully invested book with almost no cash. A sale of a high-quality name the same quarter a new name appears is most likely that, not a negative view. He said exactly that about Alphabet.

**Share-count changes of ±10–20% are mostly noise since Q2 2026. Read them cautiously.**
- Three mechanics now move counts without a change of view:
  - **PSUS inflows/rebalancing.** The combined 13F covers PSH, the private funds and PSUS. PSUS raised and deployed about $5B in Q2 2026.
  - **Forwards.** PSUS holds much of its exposure to the legacy names in equity forwards, which are not on the 13F. At 2026-06-30 that was $1.59B notional, and two-thirds of its Microsoft exposure.
  - **Ordinary trims to fund new names.**
- Worked example, Q2 to Q3 2026. Filings:
  - Microsoft fell from 6.21M to 4.06M shares (−35%) and Restaurant Brands from 25.8M to 22.0M, with smaller cuts in Brookfield, Meta, Visa and Mastercard.
  - PSUS's economic exposure (shares plus forwards) was unchanged in all of those names from June 30 to September 30. Microsoft stayed at 1,521,596 shares, RBI at 5,040,478 and Brookfield at 10,813,093. PSUS's direct Microsoft holding was only 549,096 shares.
  - So the cuts came from the legacy funds (PSH, PSLP, PS International).
  - The arithmetic supports this. Q2's 6,206,730 MSFT shares minus PSUS's 549,096 equals 5,657,634, the same as Q1's 5,654,078. The legacy funds held steady in Q2 and cut in Q3.
  - At Q3 prices the share cuts total about $2.15B, matching new money into ICE, Alcon and the Netflix add (about $2.13B).
- My read (medium-high confidence): the legacy funds (PSH had only $201M of cash at June 30) sold across their holdings, taking most from Microsoft after its 38% Q3 rise, to fund ICE and Alcon. PSUS paid for its share of the new names from its own cash. This is the "source of funds" pattern again, not a negative view on Microsoft. Ackman's Q3 letter remains bullish on Microsoft.
  - The alternative I cannot rule out is that part of the cut moved into PSH total return swaps. PSH showed $93M of TRS liabilities at June 30 with the underlyings undisclosed.
  - PSUS's Q3 N-PORT (due about late November 2026) and the PSH 2026 annual report would settle it.
- Rule of thumb: split any share-count change into PSUS (from its quarterly N-PORT or letter table, which include forwards) and the legacy funds (the 13F total minus PSUS's direct shares) before reading anything into it.

**What the 13F can't show for Pershing.** Assume any of these may be in the book:
- Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac (OTC common; owned since about 2013 per my prior knowledge, not re-verified here; 2025 attribution +5.8% and +5.0% per the 2025 AR; Ackman published a recap plan on X in November 2025, per press);
- non-US listings (UMG until 2026; watch for any new European or Canadian-listed name, though Alcon is US-listed);
- options (Nike 2025; and per the Q3 2026 letter, asymmetric bets "mostly" via options and CDS);
- total return swaps (Hertz; PSH also carried $93M of TRS liabilities at June 30, 2026, on undisclosed underlyings);
- forwards (PSUS: $1.59B notional on MSFT, META, QSR, UBER and BN at June 30, 2026, per its N-PORT);
- interest-rate swaptions and other macro hedges, a recurring Ackman tool that cost about 1.7–1.9% in 2024;
- QSR exchangeable partnership units (357,841 in the 2026 13D; Ackman's own units are held personally).

**Howard Hughes is read from 13D/As.**
- The fund block of 18,852,064 shares has not moved in three years and will not move without a 13D/A first.
- The real HHH news in the period ran through the 13D/As, not the 13F: the 2025 equity deal, the cap structure, and the June 2026 Vantage insurance acquisition with $1B of non-voting preferred from PSH (Amendment 33).

**Watch these specific things next quarter:**
